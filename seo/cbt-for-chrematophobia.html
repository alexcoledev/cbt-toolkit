Chrematophobia is an intense irrational fear of fear of money and wealth. It is a specific phobia (DSM-5 300.29, closely related to chrometophobia (a variant spelling), but chrematophobia specifically centers on money and wealth as the feared trigger). It becomes clinically significant when it is: (1) persistent and leads to excessive avoidance; (2) driven by cognitive distortions, not a realistic assessment of risk; (3) the person experiences anxiety when encountering triggers and relief when avoiding them; (4) safety behaviors include excessive cleaning, avoidance, and checking; (5) manifests as distress in triggering situations and relief after avoidance. It is distinct from chrometophobia/financial-anxiety/general-anxiety/general-phobias. The avoidance is what CBT targets — and the key insight is that chrematophobia is maintained by the cognitive distortions described below, plus avoidance reinforcement (avoiding triggers prevents the feared outcome, confirming the fear).

Cognitive Distortions → Reframes

The three most common distortion-reframe patterns in chrematophobia:

Distortion (Catastrophizing / Magical Thinking): "Money is dangerous. Wealth will corrupt me. Having money means I will become a bad person. Earning money is inherently wrong. If I accumulate any wealth, something terrible will happen. Money is the root of all evil and having it makes me evil. Any financial success is a sign of moral failure. I must reject all money to stay pure."
Reframe: "The belief that money is inherently dangerous and that wealth inevitably corrupts is the catastrophizing and magical-thinking distortion that chrematophobia has created. The reality is that money is a neutral tool: it can be used for good or ill, and wealth does not determine character. The "money corrupts" is magical thinking attributing moral properties to an inanimate medium of exchange. The evidence-based response is cognitive restructuring and magical-thinking correction. I can reframe: money is a neutral tool; wealth does not determine character; many wealthy people are ethical; catastrophizing overestimates harm; magical thinking is not evidence."
Distortion (Hypervigilance / Somatic Focus): "Every financial transaction is a threat. I can feel the anxiety rising when I handle money. If I look at my bank account, something bad will happen. My constant monitoring of avoiding financial responsibility is keeping me safe. The slightest amount of money is dangerous. Refusing to think about finances is the only thing standing between me and harm. I must avoid all financial planning."
Reframe: "The belief that every financial transaction is threatening and that constant avoidance prevents harm is the hypervigilance and somatic-focus distortion that chrematophobia has created. The reality is that financial functioning is a normal part of life, and hypervigilance around money actually increases anxiety and can lead to financial neglect. The "I can feel anxiety when handling money" is somatic focus amplifying normal stress into threat signals. The evidence-based response is cognitive restructuring and attentional retraining. I can reframe: financial management is normal; somatic focus is not evidence; hypervigilance maintains fear, not safety; avoidance creates real problems; money handling is a skill."
Distortion (Safety Behavior / Avoidance-Reinforcement): "I must avoid all money, financial decisions, and wealth accumulation. Staying poor and refusing to engage with finances keeps me safe. The fact that nothing bad has happened is proof that my avoidance is working. If I stop avoiding and accept money, the harm I fear will finally happen. Rejecting all financial opportunities is protecting me. Refusing to earn or save is the only safe option."
Reframe: "The belief that you must avoid all money and that constant poverty keeps you safe is the safety behavior and avoidance-reinforcement distortion that chrematophobia has created. The reality is that avoidance maintains the phobia: every time you refuse money and nothing bad happens, the avoidance is reinforced. Worse, avoidance prevents you from receiving the evidence that would disconfirm the fear. The "nothing bad happened so avoidance works" confuses correlation with causation. Extreme avoidance also creates real financial hardship. The evidence-based response is graded exposure. I can reframe: avoidance maintains fear, not safety; poverty prevents evidence; correlation is not causation; exposure reduces anxiety over time; financial engagement is healthy; the phobia is the problem, not the money."

Frequently Asked Questions

What is chrematophobia and how common is it?

Chrematophobia is an intense irrational fear of money, wealth, or financial success. It is a specific phobia (DSM-5 300.29) closely related to chrometophobia (a variant spelling). It frequently overlaps with financial anxiety and atychiphobia (fear of failure). Specific phobias affect approximately 7-9% of the population. CBT is highly effective with 60-80% reduction in phobic anxiety.

How is chrematophobia different from financial anxiety?

Chrematophobia is distinct from general financial anxiety because the fear is specifically about money itself as a harmful object, not about practical financial concerns like debt or expenses. The key insight is that chrematophobia is maintained by catastrophizing and magical thinking, plus avoidance reinforcement. CBT with cognitive restructuring and graded exposure directly targets these distortions.

What does CBT for chrematophobia involve and does it work?

CBT for chrematophobia involves cognitive restructuring, graded exposure, and relaxation techniques. The core components: (1) cognitive restructuring challenging the money-is-dangerous, the wealth-will-corrupt-me, the i-must-avoid-all-money, the having-money-means-harm, the earning-money-is-bad, and the avoiding-money-protects-me; (2) graded exposure (progressively engage with money and financial activities); (3) relaxation. CBT is evidence-based with 60-80% reduction in phobic anxiety.
