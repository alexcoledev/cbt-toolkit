What is Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to HIV Infection?

Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to HIV Infection (DSM-5 294.0x) is a diagnosis in the Neurocognitive Disorders category, used when: (1) there is evidence of modest cognitive decline from a previous level of performance in one or more cognitive domains (complex attention, executive function, learning and memory, language, perceptual-motor, social cognition) — based on both concern from the individual, a knowledgeable informant, or the clinician, AND objective evidence of decline; (2) the cognitive deficits do NOT interfere with independence in everyday activities (IADLs are preserved), though greater effort, compensatory strategies, or assistance may be needed — this is the 'mild' threshold, distinguishing it from Major NCD; (3) the deficits do not occur exclusively during delirium; (4) the deficits are not better explained by another mental or medical disorder; (5) there is evidence of HIV CNS pathology (HIV viral CNS infection causing subcortical neuroinflammation, white matter changes, basal ganglia involvement, HAND spectrum). The distinctive clinical features include: subcortical cognitive decline pattern (psychomotor slowing, attention and working memory deficits, and executive dysfunction — unlike the episodic memory prominence of Alzheimer's or the stepwise decline of vascular dementia), preserved independence in IADLs (the person can still manage medications, finances, appointments, and complex tasks, though with greater effort or compensatory strategies), evidence of HIV CNS pathology (HIV CNS infection, subcortical pattern with psychomotor slowing, white matter changes on MRI, CSF HIV RNA detection), a variable progression trajectory at this early stage (while HIV neurocognitive disorder progression varies with ART adherence, the mild stage may represent the earliest detectable decline where the trajectory is still being characterized, and good ART adherence may stabilize or slow progression), and a window for early intervention where behavioral strategies, cognitive engagement, monitoring, and compensatory approaches may maximize function and quality of life while the trajectory is assessed. The HAND spectrum includes asymptomatic neurocognitive impairment (subtle deficits detectable only on testing), mild neurocognitive disorder (this diagnosis — modest decline with preserved independence), and major neurocognitive disorder (significant decline interfering with independence). In the ART era, progression is variable and influenced by ART effectiveness, viral suppression in CSF, comorbid factors (vascular disease, substance use, depression), and immune reconstitution. CBT is evidence-based for both the person with the disorder (reducing anxiety, depression, and adjustment difficulties; building compensatory strategies; supporting engagement with evaluation and monitoring) and the caregiver (reducing burden, depression, and anxiety).

Why does mild neurocognitive disorder due to HIV infection need CBT instead of just monitoring the symptoms medically?

Monitoring the symptoms medically (regular cognitive assessment, monitoring, neuropsychological testing, CSF HIV RNA, neuroimaging, referral if progression occurs) is necessary but not sufficient. The cognitive distortions layered on top of the early HIV CNS pathology — the The-It-Is-Just-Normal-Aging, the The-It-Is-Not-Bad-Enough-For-It-To-Be-HIV-Neurocognitive, the The-It-Is-Just-Mild-So-Nothing-Needs-To-Be-Done, the The-It-Is-Just-The-HIV-Not-The-Brain, the The-The-Meds-Are-Working-So-The-Brain-Is-Fine, and the The-It-Is-Just-Dementia-All-The-Same — are thought patterns that medical monitoring alone does not address, and they drive disengagement from the very monitoring and early intervention that helps. The The-It-Is-Just-Normal-Aging distortion drives missed evaluation. The The-It-Is-Just-Mild-So-Nothing-Needs-To-Be-Done distortion drives missed early intervention. The The-It-Is-Just-The-HIV-Not-The-Brain distortion drives misattribution of the rapid decline as stress or delirium, missing the HIV-specific neurocognitive evaluation that the speed warrants. The The-The-Meds-Are-Working-So-The-Brain-Is-Fine distortion drives exclusion of the diagnosis based on rarity, missing the evaluation that the clinical picture warrants. The The-It-Is-Just-Dementia-All-The-Same distortion drives generic care that misses the HIV-specific features and the stage-specific early intervention that mild NCD due to HIV infection requires. CBT addresses these distortions by examining evidence, building coping skills, and supporting engagement with the evaluation, monitoring, and early intervention that the mild stage enables. The integrated approach produces better outcomes than medical monitoring alone.
