CBT for Survivors of Trauma, Abuse & Violence

Free cognitive behavioral therapy tools for survivors of trauma, abuse, domestic violence, sexual assault, and disasters — PTSD flashbacks, shame and self-blame, trust and safety concerns, triggers and hypervigilance, grief, and rebuilding identity.

7 survivor-specific tools · 100% free · no signup · privacy-first

Surviving trauma leaves distinctive cognitive patterns that generic mental health advice does not address: shame and self-blame (I should have known better, it was my fault), hypervigilance and trigger responses, trust and safety concerns, grief for the person you were before, anger at the perpetrator or at systems that failed you, and the complex work of rebuilding identity. These 7 free CBT tools are built around the cognitive distortions survivors actually experience — with examples, scenarios, and reframes that match the survivor journey.

Important: These tools are self-help, not therapy

These CBT tools are designed for self-help and education. If you are experiencing severe PTSD symptoms, flashbacks, panic attacks, thoughts of self-harm, or any condition affecting your safety, please contact a licensed trauma therapist, a crisis line, or a local domestic violence/sexual assault organization. In the US, call or text 988. In the UK, call 116 123. RAINN (1-800-656-HOPE) is the US sexual assault hotline. The National Domestic Violence Hotline (1-800-799-SAFE) is available 24/7. Seeking help is a sign of strength.
