What is Major Neurocognitive Disorder Due to HIV Infection?

Major Neurocognitive Disorder Due to HIV Infection (DSM-5 294.1x) is a diagnosis in the Neurocognitive Disorders category, used when: (1) there is evidence of significant cognitive decline from a previous level of performance in one or more cognitive domains (complex attention, executive function, learning and memory, language, perceptual-motor, social cognition) — based on both concern from the individual, a knowledgeable informant, or the clinician, AND objective evidence of decline; (2) the cognitive deficits interfere with independence in everyday activities (paying bills, managing medications, appointments, travel, complex tasks) — this is the 'major' threshold; (3) the deficits do not occur exclusively during delirium; (4) the deficits are not better explained by another mental or medical disorder; (5) the disorder is not better explained by another neurocognitive disorder. The distinctive clinical features supporting an HIV infection etiology include: subcortical cognitive profile (slowed processing speed and psychomotor slowing are prominent early, with executive dysfunction, in contrast to the cortical memory/language/visuospatial profile of Alzheimer's), HIV CNS infection (HIV crosses the blood-brain barrier, infects microglia and perivascular macrophages, causes chronic neuroinflammation and white matter damage), CNS viral reservoir (the brain harbors HIV even when blood viral load is undetectable, with ongoing low-level replication and neuroinflammation), cART impact (combination antiretroviral therapy has dramatically reduced HIV-associated dementia incidence but asymptomatic and mild neurocognitive impairment persist in the cART era), opportunistic CNS complications (toxoplasmosis, cryptococcal meningitis, progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy, CNS lymphoma can co-occur and complicate the neurocognitive picture), and HIV stigma (a unique psychosocial burden that affects symptom disclosure, help-seeking, and treatment engagement). The spectrum of HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder (HAND): asymptomatic neurocognitive impairment (ANI) (cognitive impairment on testing without functional decline), mild neurocognitive disorder (MND) (cognitive impairment with mild functional decline), HIV-associated dementia (HAD) (marked cognitive impairment with significant functional decline, formerly called AIDS dementia complex). CBT is evidence-based for both the person living with HIV (reducing depression, anxiety, distress, and improving engagement with neurocognitive care) and the caregiver (reducing burden, depression, and anxiety).

The "The-It-Is-Just-A-Side-Effect-Of-The-Meds" Distortion (medication-attribution / treatment-as-cause)

Distortion: "I am having trouble thinking clearly — my memory is worse, I am slower, I forget words. It must be the HIV meds. These antiretrovirals are toxic — they have all these side effects. The cognitive problems started after I began the new regimen. I should stop or reduce my medication. The drugs are causing the brain fog, not the HIV. If I change or stop the meds, the cognitive problems will go away."
Reframe: "I am examining the evidence and challenging the The-It-Is-Just-A-Side-Effect-Of-The-Meds. The medication-attribution (cognitive decline means drug toxicity) is the distortion: COGNITIVE DECLINE IS A CORE CLINICAL FEATURE of HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder, produced by HIV infection of the CNS (HIV infects microglia and macrophages, causes chronic neuroinflammation, damages white matter), not a side effect of antiretroviral therapy — in fact, cART REDUCES the risk of HIV-associated dementia by suppressing viral replication. The treatment-as-cause is the distortion: while some medications can cause cognitive side effects, the subcortical profile of HAND (slowed processing speed, executive dysfunction, motor slowing) is distinct from typical medication fog, and the temporal relationship (cognitive changes after starting meds) reflects disease progression that cART may not fully prevent, not drug causation. The drug-blame is the distortion: attributing cognitive decline to medication toxicity drives non-adherence, which WORSENS the CNS HIV by allowing viral replication, creating a self-fulfilling spiral. CBT helps by examining the evidence (what is the cognitive profile — subcortical HAND vs medication fog, what does neuropsychological testing show, what is the temporal relationship, what happens with medication review vs adherence), challenging the medication-attribution, and reframing the cognitive decline as a feature of CNS HIV that cART helps manage, not a side effect cART causes. This distortion is harmful because it drives medication non-adherence, which allows HIV replication in the CNS, worsening the neurocognitive deficits and accelerating the disease."

The "The-The-Viral-Load-Is-Undetectable-So-The-Brain-Is-Fine" Distortion (viral-load-as-brain-health / blood-as-brain)

Distortion: "My viral load is undetectable. The doctor says the HIV is suppressed. That means the virus is gone — or at least inactive. If the virus is suppressed in my blood, it is suppressed everywhere, including my brain. I do not need to worry about HIV affecting my brain. The cognitive changes I am noticing cannot be from HIV because the HIV is under control. Undetectable means undetectable — everywhere."
Reframe: "I am examining the evidence and challenging the The-The-Viral-Load-Is-Undetectable-So-The-Brain-Is-Fine. The viral-load-as-brain-health (blood suppression means brain health) is the distortion: AN UNDETECTABLE BLOOD VIRAL LOAD DOES NOT MEAN THE BRAIN IS UNAFFECTED — the central nervous system is a VIRAL RESERVOIR where HIV can persist and cause ongoing low-level replication and neuroinflammation even when blood viral load is fully suppressed, because the blood-brain barrier limits antiretroviral penetration into the CNS. The blood-as-brain is the distortion: blood viral load measures virus in the blood, not in the brain — HIV in the CNS can be detectable and active even when blood viral load is undetectable, because the CNS is a pharmacologically privileged compartment with different drug penetration. The suppression-as-cure is the distortion: 'undetectable' means the virus is below the detection threshold in blood, NOT that it is eradicated or inactive everywhere — the CNS reservoir is a well-established concept in HIV research. CBT helps by examining the evidence (what does the research say about the CNS reservoir, what does neuropsychological testing show, what is the cognitive profile, what does the CSF viral load show if measured), challenging the viral-load-as-brain-health, and reframing the undetectable blood viral load as excellent news for overall health that does not guarantee brain health, warranting ongoing neurocognitive monitoring. This distortion is harmful because it prevents neurocognitive evaluation, dismisses real cognitive symptoms, and delays the HAND management that could improve function and quality of life."

The "The-It-Is-Just-Part-Of-Aging-With-HIV" Distortion (aging-with-hiv-attribution / age-as-cause)

Distortion: "I am getting older, and people with HIV are living longer now thanks to the meds. Of course I am getting a bit slower and more forgetful — that is just aging. Everyone's memory gets worse with age. It is not HIV, it is just getting older. My doctor says my viral load is undetectable, so there is nothing to worry about. These cognitive changes are just the normal aging process that everyone goes through."
Reframe: "I am examining the evidence and challenging the The-It-Is-Just-Part-Of-Aging-With-HIV. The aging-with-hiv-attribution (cognitive decline means normal aging) is the distortion: COGNITIVE DECLINE IN HIV CAN EXCEED WHAT IS EXPECTED FOR AGE — HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder produces cognitive deficits that are greater than age-related cognitive changes, and the subcortical profile (slowed processing speed, executive dysfunction, motor slowing) is distinct from typical age-related memory changes. The age-as-cause is the distortion: while people with HIV are living longer and age-related cognitive changes do occur, the cognitive decline in HAND is a PATHOLOGICAL process produced by CNS HIV, not a normal aging process — neuropsychological testing can distinguish HAND from age-related changes. The normalizing-the-decline is the distortion: dismissing cognitive changes as 'just aging' prevents the evaluation that could identify HAND and lead to management that improves function. CBT helps by examining the evidence (what does neuropsychological testing show, how does the decline compare to age norms, what is the cognitive profile — subcortical HAND vs age-related, what does the research say about cognitive aging in HIV), challenging the aging-with-hiv-attribution, and reframing the cognitive decline as potentially pathological, warranting evaluation rather than dismissal. This distortion is harmful because it prevents neurocognitive evaluation, normalizes a potentially treatable condition, and delays the HAND management that could improve function and quality of life."

The "The-The-Stigma-Means-I-Should-Not-Talk-About-It" Distortion (stigma-silence / shame-as-silence)

Distortion: "I cannot tell anyone I am having cognitive problems. People already treat me differently because of the HIV. If I say I am also losing my memory and getting confused, they will think I am losing my mind. They will judge me even more. I should just deal with it privately. Talking about cognitive problems on top of HIV is too much stigma. No one would understand. It is better to stay silent and manage on my own."
Reframe: "I am examining the evidence and challenging the The-The-Stigma-Means-I-Should-Not-Talk-About-It. The stigma-silence (HIV stigma means do not disclose cognitive symptoms) is the distortion: HIV STIGMA IS REAL AND HARMFUL, but silence does not protect against stigma — it isolates the person, prevents help-seeking, and allows the cognitive symptoms to progress without evaluation or management. The shame-as-silence is the distortion: the shame about HIV and about cognitive decline are both real, but shame is an emotion, not a reason — the cognitive symptoms are a medical condition, not a moral failing, and disclosing them to a healthcare provider is a clinical action, not a social one. The fear-as-isolation is the distortion: the fear of judgment is understandable, but isolation worsens the cognitive symptoms, the depression, and the anxiety, and prevents the support that could improve outcomes. CBT helps by examining the evidence (what does disclosure to a healthcare provider offer — evaluation, management, support; what is the cost of silence — isolation, progression, untreated distress; who is a safe person to tell), challenging the stigma-silence, and building a disclosure plan that starts with a healthcare provider (who is bound by confidentiality) and expands to trusted others as safe. This distortion is harmful because it blocks help-seeking, prevents neurocognitive evaluation, isolates the person, and allows the cognitive symptoms and the distress to progress without support or management."

The "The-It-Is-Just-An-Opportunistic-Infection" Distortion (opportunistic-infection-attribution / OI-as-cause)

Distortion: "The cognitive problems must be from an infection in the brain — toxoplasmosis, or crypto, or one of those opportunistic infections. HIV itself does not really affect the brain that much. It is the OIs that cause the problems. Once we treat the opportunistic infection, the cognitive problems will get better. The HIV is just the underlying condition; the brain problems are from the OI, not from the HIV directly."
Reframe: "I am examining the evidence and challenging the The-It-Is-Just-An-Opportunistic-Infection. The opportunistic-infection-attribution (cognitive decline means OI) is the distortion: while opportunistic CNS infections (toxoplasmosis, cryptococcal meningitis, PML) CAN cause cognitive symptoms and MUST be evaluated, HIV DIRECTLY INFECTS THE CNS and causes neurocognitive deficits through chronic neuroinflammation, microglial activation, and white matter damage — HAND is a DIRECT effect of HIV on the brain, not only an indirect effect through OIs. The OI-as-cause is the distortion: in the cART era, opportunistic CNS infections are less common, but HAND persists — the cognitive decline can be from direct CNS HIV even when no OI is present, and attributing all cognitive decline to OIs misses the direct HIV effect. The infection-as-explanation is the distortion: treating an OI (when present) is necessary but may not fully resolve the cognitive deficits if there is also ongoing direct CNS HIV — the two processes can co-occur and require different management. CBT helps by examining the evidence (has an OI been identified and treated, do the cognitive deficits persist after OI treatment, what does neuropsychological testing show, what does the research say about direct CNS HIV effects), challenging the opportunistic-infection-attribution, and reframing the cognitive decline as potentially from direct CNS HIV, warranting HAND-specific management alongside OI treatment. This distortion is harmful because it drives OI-only workup, misses the direct HIV CNS effect, and delays the HAND management that could improve function and quality of life."

The "The-It-Is-Just-Dementia-All-The-Same" Distortion (dementia-oversimplification / category-as-uniform)

Distortion: "Dementia is dementia. Whether it is Alzheimer's, HIV, Lewy body, or vascular, it is all the same thing — memory loss and confusion. The specific type does not matter. The treatment is the same, the prognosis is the same, and there is no point in distinguishing them. HIV dementia is just another word for Alzheimer's that happens in people with HIV."
Reframe: "I am examining the evidence and challenging the The-It-Is-Just-Dementia-All-The-Same. The dementia-oversimplification (all dementia is the same) is the distortion: the different types of dementia have DISTINCTIVE FEATURES, DIFFERENT MANAGEMENT, and DIFFERENT PROGNOSIS — HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder has a subcortical profile (slowed processing speed, executive dysfunction, motor slowing), a viral reservoir in the CNS, and can be partially prevented or stabilized with cART, which Alzheimer's does not have and cannot be; these features require DIFFERENT MANAGEMENT: (1) the subcortical profile in HAND affects processing speed and executive function prominently, while Alzheimer's affects memory and language prominently, requiring different cognitive strategies and accommodations; (2) cART optimization and CNS penetration are HAND-specific interventions that Alzheimer's does not use; (3) the prognosis in HAND can be stabilized with treatment, while Alzheimer's is progressively degenerative; (4) the co-occurrence with opportunistic CNS infections in HIV requires additional monitoring and management; (5) HIV stigma adds a psychosocial dimension that requires specific support. The category-as-uniform (the type does not matter) is the distortion: the specific diagnosis drives the treatment, the care planning, the family support, and the prognosis. CBT helps by examining the evidence, challenging the oversimplification, and building engagement with the HAND-specific management. This distortion is harmful because it drives generic treatment that misses the HAND-specific interventions (cART optimization, CNS penetration, OI monitoring, stigma-informed support) and the prognostic information that is critical for planning."

Is CBT evidence-based for major neurocognitive disorder due to HIV infection?

Yes. CBT is evidence-based for two populations affected by major neurocognitive disorder due to HIV infection. For the person living with HIV, CBT addresses depression, anxiety, and distress that accompany cognitive decline — these symptoms are common, distressing, and treatable, and CBT reduces their severity and improves quality of life and engagement with care. For the caregiver, CBT reduces caregiver burden, depression, and anxiety by targeting the cognitive distortions that maintain distress (the medication-attribution, the viral-load-as-brain-health, the aging-with-hiv-attribution, the stigma-silence, the opportunistic-infection-attribution, the dementia-oversimplification). HIV stigma adds a unique psychosocial burden that affects symptom disclosure and help-seeking, making CBT especially important for building disclosure skills and reducing isolation. Clinical guidelines recognize CBT as a non-pharmacological intervention for HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder. The evidence shows that CBT improves quality of life, function, and outcomes for both the person living with HIV and the caregiver.

What happens in a CBT session for major neurocognitive disorder due to HIV infection?

A CBT session for major neurocognitive disorder due to HIV infection involves different approaches for the person living with HIV and the caregiver. For the person living with HIV: (1) Engagement and assessment — the therapist builds a relationship adapted to the cognitive level, assesses current symptoms, distress, stigma experiences, and quality of life. (2) Distress and anxiety work — cognitive restructuring adapted to the cognitive level, targeting the distress about the diagnosis and the cognitive decline. (3) Medication adherence — addressing the medication-attribution distortion that drives non-adherence, building engagement with cART. (4) Stigma and disclosure work — building a safe disclosure plan, reducing isolation, connecting to support. (5) Symptom coping — strategies for cognitive deficits, functional changes, and co-occurring conditions. For the caregiver: (1) Assessment of burden, depression, anxiety, and the cognitive distortions. (2) Psychoeducation about HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder, the subcortical profile, the CNS reservoir, cART impact, and the prognosis. (3) Cognitive restructuring — targeting the six distortions. (4) Behavioral activation — building engagement with support, self-care, and meaningful activity. (5) Caregiving skills — communication, symptom management, coordination with the infectious disease and neurology teams. CBT for HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder and caregivers is typically delivered by a therapist trained in CBT with HIV/neurodegenerative experience.
