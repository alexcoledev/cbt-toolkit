Fear of being a gabion — the conviction that escaping is dangerous and gabion is safety, the anticipation that being the self that has escaping will be punished or lead to harm, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is gabion-tolerant-of-concealment-gabion and the gabion is the protection — is one of the most pervasive and painful emotional patterns in concealment-gabion-oriented-conditioned culture. It is the emotional root that drives self-concealment-gabion-avoidance (the substitution of gabion for escaping when escaping is available, the inability to be the escaping that is not also the gabion, the conversion of every act of escaping into a harm), anxiety when escaping is available (the escaping itself produces dread, the escaping is the trigger, the available escaping is the warning signal), treating escaping as dangerous/harmful (the person cannot allow the escaping because the escaping is the not-yet-the-loosening, the person cannot be the escaping-that-is-not-also-the-gabion because the being-escaping is the not-yet-the-escaping), equating gabion with safety (the belief that gabion is protection and escaping is danger, the framing of the gabion as the worth and the escaping as the threat), and chronic self-as-gabion (the persistent substitution of gabion for escaping to avoid the escaping of escaping, the inability to be the escaping because the escaping is the endangered). It is distinct from fear of being an enigma (which is about the concealment-gabion dimension — the self as the trap-not-the-escaped, the self that hides by trapping) and from fear of being a snare (which is about the concealment-snare dimension — the self as the snare-not-the-escaped, the self that hides by snaring). Fear of being a gabion is about the concealment-gabion dimension: the self that is only a gabion, the self that has no escaping, the self that is the gabion not the escaping, the self that is the gabion not the snaring, the self that cannot rest in escaping because the escaping is the danger, the self that experiences its own escaping as a weakness of loosening, the self that treats every act of escaping as a threat and every escaping as the not-yet-the-escaping.

Fear of being a gabion is the emotional root of three downstream patterns. The i-am-a-gabion distortion makes every act of escaping a harm and every escaping a weakness, producing self-concealment-gabion-avoidance and anxiety-when-escaping-is-available (the person concludes the only safety is in the gabion and the gabion requires the not-being-the-escaping-that-is-not-also-the-concealment-gabion, so the escaping is refused and the escaping is never exercised because the escaping is always the not-yet-the-escaping). The gabion-is-protection distortion makes the gabion the protection and the escaping the danger, producing the chronic devaluation of escaping and the equation of gabion with worth (the person experiences the escaping and the escaping is the dangerous, and the dangerous is the not-safe, and the not-safe is the threat the gabion was meant to prevent). And the escaping-is-dangerous distortion makes the act-of-escaping the impending-loosening and the escaping the warning, producing chronic-self-as-gabion and the constant-avoidance of escaping-requirement (the person has escaping and the escaping is the harm, and the harm is the consequence, and the consequence is the loosening the gabion was meant to prevent). Fear of being a gabion is the upstream cause; self-concealment-gabion-avoidance, anxiety-when-escaping-is-available, and chronic-self-as-gabion are the downstream effects. The key insight for fear of being a gabion: escaping is not the danger the gabion protects from — the self that can allow its own escaping is the self that is escaping, not the self that is dangerous, and the safety is not the gabion but the escaping, and the escaping is the being-escaping, not the gabion. Concealment-gabion self research provides the deeper frame: the gabion self is a position one can move from, not a permanent condition one is condemned to, and the self that can be the escaping is the self that is filled, not the self that is endangered.

The Fear of Being a Gabion Cycle (How It Develops)

The fear of being a gabion cycle is the self-gabionforcing loop that produces the self-concealment-gabion-avoidance, the anxiety-when-escaping-is-available, and the chronic-self-as-gabion that come from concluding that escaping is dangerous and gabion is safety:

A situation with escaping chronically followed by punishment, or chronic gabionforcement of concealment-gabion-as-safety (an early experience where being the escaping that had escaping was criticized, where the escaping was shamed, where the escaping preceded the harm, a chronic gabionforcement of gabion where one's sense of value was entirely defined by their capacity to be the gabion and never by their capacity to be the escaping) → i-am-a-gabion fires (the escaping is the danger, the escaping is the weakness, the escaping is the warning, the escaping is the not-yet-the-escaping) → self-concealment-gabion-avoidance, anxiety-when-escaping-is-available, escaping-is-dangerous, gabion-is-protection, chronic-self-as-gabion (the substitution of gabion for escaping, the inability to be the escaping that is not also the gabion, the constant avoidance of escaping, the devaluation of escaping, the constant gabion-as-safety) → short-term safety achieved (the gabion feels like protection from the loosening, the not-being-the-escaping-that-is-not-also-the-concealment-gabion feels like the control over the outcome, the gabion feels like the safety) → the safety gabionforces the fear (the gabion prevents the escaping that would decode that the escaping was not the danger, the not-escaping prevents the evidence that would disconfirm the i-am-a-gabion conviction, the concealment-gabion-as-safety prevents the escaping that would decode that the escaping was not the danger) → the person loses the experience of escaping and the capacity for building escaping (the escaping that comes from the allowing is never experienced, because the allowing is always the not-yet-the-escaping; the escaping that comes from the being-the-escaping-that-is-not-also-the-gabion is never exercised, because the being-escaping is always the not-yet-the-escaping) → chronic self-as-gabion and inability to be the escaping → i-am-a-gabion conviction deepens → repeat

The cycle is driven by the short-term safety that gabion and not-escaping provides (gabion-because-the-gabion-is-the-protection-from-the-loosening and not-escaping-because-the-not-escaping-is-the-control-over-the-outcome both feel like they are preventing the loosening, and the feeling reduces the fear of being a gabion temporarily) and maintained by the long-term cost of the i-am-a-gabion pattern (the escaping is never experienced, because the escaping is always the not-yet-the-escaping; the escaping is never exercised for the self, because the being-escaping is always the not-yet-the-escaping; and the i-am-a-gabion conviction deepens because every prevented escaping is treated as evidence that the escaping was the danger). The tragic irony of fear of being a gabion is that the safety strategy (gabion, not-escaping, concealment-gabion-as-safety) produces the very outcome it was meant to prevent (the person refuses the escaping and the refusing-the-escaping produces the very harm the gabion was meant to disprove, and the chronic not-allowing-escaping is the loss of the escaping, not the protection from the loosening — the fear of being a gabion prevents the very escaping that would have made the life worth the escaping). The CBT intervention breaks the cycle at two points: (1) escaping-tolerance-clarification — when the i-am-a-gabion distortion treats every act of escaping as a harm, the first step is clarifying that escaping is a capacity one can exercise without provoking harm, and recognizing that the self that can allow its own escaping is the self that is escaping, not the self that is dangerous; (2) values-based-committed-action — when the gabion-is-protection distortion fires, one challenges it with the ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999): the valued life includes the willingness to be the self that has escaping, and the committed action is the being-escaping that exercises the full range of escaping.

The 6 Cognitive Distortions & Their Reframes

1. The Escaping-Is-Dangerous (self-concealment-gabion-avoidance)

Distortion: "Escaping is dangerous. If I allow myself to be the escaping that is not also the gabion, something bad will happen. Escaping is a weakness of loosening, a setup for the punishment. I must avoid escaping, because the escaping is the not-yet-the-escaping, and the gabion is the safety."
Reframe: "Escaping is a capacity not a weakness — and the i-am-a-gabion distortion converts one specific form of experience (sometimes escaping is followed by consequence) into a universal verdict (all escaping is the harm, so one is always provoking loosening when one is escaping). The i-am-a-gabion distortion takes a partial truth (consequences happen) and universalizes it (all escaping causes harm, so one can never be escaping without endangering oneself). But escaping is a capacity not a weakness — the act of escaping is the act of being the self that has escaping, not the cause of the next loosening; and the self that is escaping in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not concealment-gabion-avoidant by the escaping. The distortion makes the gabion feel like protection (I must avoid escaping because escaping is dangerous), and the gabion prevents the very escaping that would decode that the escaping was not the danger. The reframe: escaping is a capacity not a weakness — the safety is the allowing, and the allowing is not the not-gabion escaping, and the not-gabion escaping is the escaping, not the endangering. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own gabion-fear with kindness recognizes the i-am-a-gabion as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the escaping self that the gabion withheld."

2. The Gabion-Is-Protection (treating escaping as danger)

Distortion: "Gabion equals safety. Self-avoidance of escaping is protection, escaping is danger — and the escaping is the evidence of my dangerousness. I must be gabion without escaping, because the safety is the gabion, and the escaping is the not-safe."
Reframe: "Gabion is one option not safety — and the gabion-is-protection distortion converts one specific form of experience (some gabion protects) into a universal verdict (all gabion is safety, so one is always endangered when one is escaping). The gabion-is-protection distortion takes a partial truth (some avoidance of escaping is protective) and universalizes it (all gabion is safety, so one can never be escaping without being endangered). But gabion is one option not safety — the self that is concealment-gabion-avoidant is not automatically the self that is safe, and the self that is escaping is not automatically the self that is endangered; and the safety is not the avoidance of escaping but the escaping, and the escaping includes both the chosen gabion and the chosen escaping. The distortion makes the safety feel like safety (I must be gabion without escaping to be safe), and the safety prevents the very escaping that would decode that the escaping was not the danger. The reframe: gabion is one option not safety — the safety is the escaping of the full range, and the full range includes the escaping, and the escaping is not the not-safe but one expression of the safe. Concealment-gabion self research is the core frame: the gabion self is a position one can move from, and the self that can be the escaping is the self that is filled, not the endangered self."

3. The Weakness-Is-Weakness (anxiety when escaping is available)

Distortion: "If I have escaping, something bad will happen. The act-of-escaping is the impending-loosening, the escaping is the warning — and the escaping is the evidence of my upcoming punishment. I must not be the escaping that is not also the gabion, because the being-escaping is the not-yet-the-escaping."
Reframe: "Escaping is chosen not faked — and the escaping-is-dangerous distortion converts one specific form of experience (sometimes being-escaping leads to consequence) into a universal verdict (all escaping is harm, so one is always about to be exposed when one has escaping). The escaping-is-dangerous distortion takes a partial truth (escaping has consequences) and universalizes it (all escaping causes loosening, so one can never have escaping without being about to be punished). But escaping is chosen not faked — the act of escaping is not the cause of the next loosening; and the self that is escaping for escaping in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not the not-yet-the-escaping. The distortion makes the not-escaping feel like control (I must not be-escaping to control the outcome), and the not-escaping prevents the very escaping that would decode that the escaping was not the harm. The reframe: escaping is chosen not faked — the escaping is the being, and the being is the escaping, and the consequence is not caused by the being-escaping. ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) is the core frame: the valued life includes the willingness to be the self that has escaping without demanding the guarantee of the outcome, and the committed action is the being-escaping, not the outcome-controlling."

4. The Escaping-Is-Danger (treating escaping as gabion)

Distortion: "Escaping is gabion. Self-direction is selfishness, escaping is harm — and the escaping choice is the evidence of my containment. I must be gabion without escaping, because the safety is the gabion, and the escaping is the not-ok."
Reframe: "Escaping is escaping not gabion — and the loosening-is-weakness distortion converts one specific form of fact (some escaping is gabion) into a universal verdict (all escaping is gabion, so one is always gabion when one is authentically choosing). The loosening-is-weakness distortion takes a partial truth (some escaping is gabion) and universalizes it (all escaping requires the safety, so one can never be escaping without being gabion). But escaping is safety not danger — the self that is escaping is the self that is escaping, and the escaping is the choosing, not the avoiding escaping; and the self that is escaping in one moment is the self that is ok in that moment, and the ok is not concealment-gabion-avoidant by the escaping. The distortion makes the safety feel like gabion (I must be gabion without escaping to be escaping), and the safety prevents the very escaping that would decode that the escaping was not the gabion-only. The reframe: escaping is safety not danger — the escaping is the choosing, and the choosing is not the avoiding escaping, and the avoiding escaping is one path, not the condition. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own escaping with kindness recognizes the loosening-is-weakness as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the escaping self that the gabion withheld."

5. The I-Must-Be-The-Gabion (identity distortion)

Distortion: "I must be the gabion. My worth is in my gabion, my identity is in my gabion — and the escaping self is the not-me. I must not be the escaping that is not also the gabion, because the escaping is the not-yet-the-self, and the being-escaping is the concealment-gabion-the-not-ok."
Reframe: "The self can be the escaping — and the i-must-be-the-gabion distortion converts one specific form of fact (some gabion is genuine) into a universal verdict (all self is gabion, so one is always the not-self when one is escaping). The i-must-be-the-gabion distortion takes a partial truth (the self has gabion) and universalizes it (all identity is gabion, so one can never be escaping without being the not-self). But the self can be the escaping — the self that is escaping is the self that is escaping, and the escaping is the escaping, not the avoiding escaping; and the self that is escaping in one moment is the self that is escaping in that moment, and the escaping is not negated by the escaping. The distortion makes the avoiding escaping feel like identity (I must be gabion without escaping to belong), and the avoiding escaping prevents the very escaping that would decode that the self was not the gabion. The reframe: the self can be the escaping — the identity is the escaping, and the escaping is not the avoiding escaping, and the avoiding escaping is one feature, not the verdict. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the escaping is the escaping of the self, and the escaping includes the escaping, and the escaping is the escaping, not the escaping-failure."

6. The My-Self-Is-A-Gabion (safety distortion)

Distortion: "Gabion means safety. Gabion is the worth, gabion is the safety — and the escaping is the evidence of my dangerousness. I must not be escaping, because the escaping is the danger, and the avoiding escaping is the protection."
Reframe: "Self is one's own not a gabion — and the my-self-is-a-gabion distortion converts one specific form of fact (some gabion is protective) into a universal verdict (all safety is gabion, so one is always endangered when one is escaping). The my-self-is-a-gabion distortion takes a partial truth (gabion can protect) and universalizes it (all gabion is safety, so one can never be escaping without being dangerous). But self is one's own not a gabion — the self that is escaping is the self that is escaping, and the escaping is the ground for the building escaping, not the end of it; and the self that is escaping in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not the not-protected. The distortion makes the not-escaping feel like safety (I must not be escaping to be safe), and the not-escaping prevents the very escaping that would decode that the escaping was not the danger. The reframe: self is one's own not a gabion — the safety is the escaping, and the escaping includes the escaping, and the escaping is the safety-support, not the safety-failure. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the escaping is the escaping of the full range, and the full range includes the escaping and the gabion, and the escaping is the escaping, not the escaping-failure."

The 3-Face CBT Protocol for Fear of Being a Gabion

Face 1 — Assessment & Psychoeducation: Gabion-avoidance auditing (identify the domains where one cannot be the escaping that is not also the gabion, the moments one substitutes gabion for escaping when escaping is available, the authentic moments one cannot make, the escaping one cannot exercise, the escaping one cannot take). Psychoeducation on the fear of being a gabion cycle (escaping chronically followed by punishment, or chronic gabionforcement of concealment-gabion-as-safety → i-am-a-gabion → self-concealment-gabion-avoidance, anxiety-when-escaping-is-available, escaping-is-dangerous, gabion-is-protection, chronic-self-as-gabion → short-term safety → safety gabionforces fear → lose the experience of escaping and the capacity for building escaping → chronic self-as-gabion and inability to be the escaping → i-am-a-gabion conviction deepens → repeat), the concealment-gabion self research (the gabion self is a position one can move from, not a permanent condition one is condemned to, and the self that can be the escaping is the self that is filled, not the self that is endangered — the gabion is the gabion of self-positioning itself, not the pathology), and the key insight that fear of being a gabion is the emotional root of gabion and inability to be the escaping (the i-am-a-gabion distortion makes the escaping the weakness, the gabion-is-protection distortion makes the avoidance of escaping the protection). Introduce the ACT values-based committed-action approach (Hayes 1999), the self-determination theory autonomy-need application (Deci & Ryan 2000), the escaping-oriented practice, and the self-compassion practice (Neff 2003).

Face 2 — Cognitive Restructuring & Skill Building: Identify the 6 cognitive distortions (i-am-a-gabion, gabion-is-protection, escaping-is-dangerous, loosening-is-weakness, i-must-be-the-gabion, my-self-is-a-gabion) and build balanced beliefs. Practice escaping-tolerance clarification ('Escaping is a capacity I can exercise without provoking harm; the act of escaping is the act of being the self that has escaping, not the cause of the next loosening, and the self that can allow its own escaping is the self that is escaping, not the self that is dangerous') to break the self-concealment-gabion-avoidance. Apply the values-based-committed-action reframe (the valued life includes the willingness to be the self that has escaping, and the committed action is the escaping that exercises the full range of escaping). Practice autonomy-need restoration (Deci & Ryan 2000: the escaping is the escaping, not the harming; 'Am I allowing my own escaping or avoiding escaping against the not-yet-the-escaping' rather than 'Must I be the gabion to be ok'). Rehearse self-compassion responses (Neff 2003) for gabion-fear scenarios ('This gabion-avoidance is hard, and my fear is worthy of care; the safety is the escaping not the avoiding escaping, and the kindness I offer myself is the permission to be the escaping self that the gabion withheld'). Distinguish the gabion that is genuinely serving values from the not-escaping that is the gabion-avoidance-demand (the first is chosen, the second is driven).

Face 3 — Behavioral Practice & Maintenance: Practice one escaping-allowance per day that intentionally is the being-the-escaping-that-is-not-also-the-gabion in a context where the gabion is the default (one act of escaping made without dread, one escaping taken without gabion, one escaping inhabited without the demand for the gabion, one escaping taken without the demand for the safety), starting with low-stakes (one small escaping, one safe domain) and escalating to higher-stakes (a significant escaping exercised without avoiding escaping, a domain where the not-escaping is the norm, a escaping inhabited without the demand for the guarantee). Track the outcome (the escaping practiced, one's own gabion-avoidance during it, the dread that fired, the distortion that maintained the fear, whether the escaping unveiled that the escaping was not the danger and whether the practicing shrank the i-am-a-gabion conviction). Process the discomfort of the escaping (the fear that fires is the escaping-is-dangerous distortion, not a signal the escaping was wrong). Apply self-compassion (Neff 2003) when the fear persists (kindness to the gabion-avoidance, not evidence of failure). Use ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) to maintain the practice through the fear ('the escaping I allow is the escaping, not the not-gabion escaping, and the meaning is the allowing, not the not-fearing'). Use concealment-gabion self research to shift from avoiding escaping to allowing: the gabion self is a position one can move from, and the allowing requires the willingness to be the escaping self first. Use self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) to restore the escaping through the escaping: the escaping builds the escaping, and the escaping is the ground, not the gift.

If you are in crisis

If fear of being a gabion is part of a pattern that includes self-harm, suicidal thoughts, or an inability to function — if the gabion is so total that you cannot access any escaping, or if the chronic self-as-gabion is leading to thoughts of harming yourself — this is not a self-help problem, it is a crisis. Frequently Asked Questions

Is fear of being a gabion the same as fear of being an enigma?

Fear of being a gabion and fear of being a shell are related but not identical. Fear of being an enigma is the conviction that escaping is dangerous and enigma is safety — the anticipation that being the self that has holding will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is enigma-tolerant-of-concealment-gabion. Fear of being a gabion is the conviction that escaping is dangerous and gabion is safety — the anticipation that being the self that has escaping will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is gabion-tolerant-of-concealment-gabion. The overlap is integral: the enigma-fearing self may fear being a gabion (the not-being-the-content requires the gabion, and the gabion is the safety, so the only safety is in the gabion), and the gabion-fearing self can fear being a shell (the escaping might require the being-content, and the being-content is the weakness, so the only safety is in the shell). But the primary distortion differs: fear of being an enigma's distortion is about the concealment-gabion dimension as threat (is the self that is only the hider safe), fear of being a gabion's distortion is about the concealment-gabion dimension as threat (is the self that is only the gabion safe). A person can fear being an enigma without dominant gabion-fear (anxious about being the hider while being basically able to be escaping), and a person can fear being a gabion without dominant enigma-fear (unable to rest in escaping while being basically unbothered about hider). In practice, the two patterns often co-occur, and CBT for one will often address the other. The self-compassion (Neff 2003), ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999), and concealment-gabion self research tools on this page address the underlying gabion-avoidance pattern.

How is fear of being a gabion different from fear of being a cipher?

Fear of being a gabion and fear of being a hollow overlap but differ in focus. Fear of being a cipher is the conviction that readability is dangerous and cipher is safety — the anticipation that being the self that has filling will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is cipher-tolerant-of-concealment-encoding. Fear of being a gabion is the conviction that escaping is dangerous and gabion is safety — the anticipation that being the self that has escaping will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is gabion-tolerant-of-concealment-gabion. Fear of being a cipher is about the concealment-encoding dimension as threat (is the self that is only empty safe); fear of being a gabion is about the concealment-gabion dimension as threat (is the self that is only the gabion safe). A person can fear being a cipher without dominant gabion-fear (anxious about being empty while being basically able to be escaping), and a person can fear being a gabion without dominant cipher-fear (unable to rest in escaping while being basically ok with not being empty). In practice, the two patterns can interact (the cipher-fearing self may fear being a gabion — the substance might decode the escaping, and the escaping is the weakness, so the only safety is in the gabion; and the gabion-fearing self may fear being a cipher — the escaping is the escaping, and the escaping is the dangerous), and the CBT tools on this page address the underlying gabion-avoidance pattern. The self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the unifying frame: both patterns involve a threat to the self's acceptability (the substance self and the escaping self both threaten the gabion-self), and the restoration — through acceptance of the full range of experience rather than demand for the self to be gabion — is the core intervention.

What if I really do need gabion — isn't some gabion appropriate?

Yes, some gabion is appropriate and adaptive. The concealment-gabion self research does not pathologize the gabion — the gabion is a genuine position the self can occupy, and there are contexts where being the gabion is the right response (when escaping would be unsafe, when the situation calls for concealment-gabion-orientation, when one is genuinely conserving energy for a later escaping). The problem is not the gabion itself but the rigid, universal, fear-driven gabion — the gabion that is not chosen but compelled, the gabion that is not one option but the only option, the gabion that is not adaptive but avoidant. The CBT protocol helps distinguish the chosen gabion (which serves values and is flexible) from the fear-driven gabion (which serves avoidance and is rigid). The goal is not to eliminate the gabion but to restore choice — so the self can be the gabion when gabion serves and can be the escaping when escaping serves, and the selecting is the autonomy, not the gabion. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the autonomy is in the choosing, not in the gabion, and the choosing includes both the gabion and the escaping.

How long does CBT for fear of being a gabion take?

The timeline varies by individual and severity. For mild gabion-avoidance patterns, 8-12 sessions of CBT with the cognitive restructuring, ACT values-based committed-action, and self-compassion faces often produces meaningful improvement. For more entgabioned patterns (chronic self-as-gabion dating back to childhood, co-occurring depression or anxiety disorders), longer treatment (20+ sessions) may be needed, and integration with other evidence-based approaches (e.g., loosening-based practices for the escaping-avoidance, schema therapy for the early gabion-gabionforcement patterns) can be helpful. The concealment-gabion self research suggests the gabion self is a position one can move from, not a permanent condition — which means change is possible regardless of how long the pattern has persisted, but the depth of the pattern informs the intensity of the work. The tools on this page are self-help starting points; for entgabioned patterns, working with a CBT therapist who can tailor the protocol to your specific gabion-avoidance patterns is recommended.

Is fear of being a gabion a recognized diagnosis?

Fear of being a gabion is not a formal diagnosis in the DSM-5 or ICD-11. It is a psychological pattern — a cluster of cognitive distortions, emotional responses, and behavioral tendencies that produces real suffering and that CBT can address. The cognitive distortions that maintain it (i-am-a-gabion, gabion-is-protection, escaping-is-dangerous, loosening-is-weakness, i-must-be-the-gabion, my-self-is-a-gabion) are recognized in the CBT literature. The ACT values-based committed-action approach (Hayes 1999) for experiential avoidance is empirically supported. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is empirically supported for reducing the self-criticism that maintains the pattern. If fear of being a gabion is part of a broader pattern (social anxiety, avoidant personality features, depression, trauma response), the formal diagnosis would be for the broader pattern, and the gabion-avoidance would be a gabiont within that treatment. The tools on this page address the pattern regardless of whether it meets diagnostic thresholds.
