Fear of being a mask — the conviction that authenticity is dangerous and mask is safety, the anticipation that being the self that has authenticity will be punished or lead to harm, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is mask-tolerant-of-concealment and the concealment is the protection — is one of the most pervasive and painful emotional patterns in concealment-oriented-conditioned culture. It is the emotional root that drives self-concealment-avoidance (the substitution of concealment for authenticity when authenticity is available, the inability to be the authenticity that is not also the worn, the conversion of every act of authenticity into a harm), anxiety when authenticity is available (the authenticity itself produces dread, the authenticity is the trigger, the available authenticity is the warning signal), treating authenticity as dangerous/harmful (the person cannot allow the authenticity because the authenticity is the not-yet-the-exposure, the person cannot be the authenticity-that-is-not-also-the-worn because the being-authenticity is the not-yet-the-authenticity), equating mask with safety (the belief that concealment is protection and authenticity is danger, the framing of the concealment as the worth and the authenticity as the threat), and chronic self-as-mask (the persistent substitution of mask for authenticity to avoid the authenticity of authenticity, the inability to be the authenticity because the authenticity is the endangered). It is distinct from fear of being a shell (which is about the container dimension — the self as a container with no content, the self that holds but does not have) and from fear of being a hollow (which is about the void dimension — the self as empty with no substance). Fear of being a mask is about the concealment dimension: the self that is only a concealment, the self that has no authenticity, the self that is the concealment not the authenticity, the self that is the worn not the wearer, the self that cannot rest in authenticity because the authenticity is the danger, the self that experiences its own authenticity as a weakness of exposure, the self that treats every act of authenticity as a threat and every authenticity as the not-yet-the-authenticity.

Fear of being a mask is the emotional root of three downstream patterns. The i-am-a-mask distortion makes every act of authenticity a harm and every authenticity a weakness, producing self-concealment-avoidance and anxiety-when-authenticity-is-available (the person concludes the only safety is in the mask and the mask requires the not-being-the-authenticity-that-is-not-also-the-worn, so the authenticity is refused and the authenticity is never exercised because the authenticity is always the not-yet-the-authenticity). The mask-is-protection distortion makes the concealment the protection and the authenticity the danger, producing the chronic devaluation of authenticity and the equation of concealment with worth (the person experiences the authenticity and the authenticity is the dangerous, and the dangerous is the not-safe, and the not-safe is the threat the mask was meant to prevent). And the authenticity-is-dangerous distortion makes the act-of-authenticity the impending-exposure and the authenticity the warning, producing chronic-self-as-mask and the constant-avoidance of authenticity-requirement (the person has authenticity and the authenticity is the harm, and the harm is the consequence, and the consequence is the exposure the mask was meant to prevent). Fear of being a mask is the upstream cause; self-concealment-avoidance, anxiety-when-authenticity-is-available, and chronic-self-as-mask are the downstream effects. The key insight for fear of being a mask: authenticity is not the danger the mask protects from — the self that can allow its own authenticity is the self that is authenticity, not the self that is dangerous, and the safety is not the mask but the authenticity, and the authenticity is the being-authenticity, not the concealment. Concealment self research provides the deeper frame: the concealment self is a position one can move from, not a permanent condition one is condemned to, and the self that can be the authenticity is the self that is filled, not the self that is endangered.

The Fear of Being a Mask Cycle (How It Develops)

The fear of being a mask cycle is the self-reinforcing loop that produces the self-concealment-avoidance, the anxiety-when-authenticity-is-available, and the chronic-self-as-mask that come from concluding that authenticity is dangerous and mask is safety:

A situation with authenticity chronically followed by punishment, or chronic reinforcement of concealment-as-safety (an early experience where being the authenticity that had authenticity was criticized, where the authenticity was shamed, where the authenticity preceded the harm, a chronic reinforcement of mask where one's sense of value was entirely defined by their capacity to be the mask and never by their capacity to be the authenticity) → i-am-a-mask fires (the authenticity is the danger, the authenticity is the weakness, the authenticity is the warning, the authenticity is the not-yet-the-authenticity) → self-concealment-avoidance, anxiety-when-authenticity-is-available, authenticity-is-dangerous, mask-is-protection, chronic-self-as-mask (the substitution of concealment for authenticity, the inability to be the authenticity that is not also the worn, the constant avoidance of authenticity, the devaluation of authenticity, the constant mask-as-safety) → short-term safety achieved (the mask feels like protection from the exposure, the not-being-the-authenticity-that-is-not-also-the-worn feels like the control over the outcome, the concealment feels like the safety) → the safety reinforces the fear (the mask prevents the authenticity that would reveal that the authenticity was not the danger, the not-authenticity prevents the evidence that would disconfirm the i-am-a-mask conviction, the concealment-as-safety prevents the authenticity that would reveal that the authenticity was not the danger) → the person loses the experience of authenticity and the capacity for building authenticity (the authenticity that comes from the allowing is never experienced, because the allowing is always the not-yet-the-authenticity; the authenticity that comes from the being-the-authenticity-that-is-not-also-the-worn is never exercised, because the being-authenticity is always the not-yet-the-authenticity) → chronic self-as-mask and inability to be the authenticity → i-am-a-mask conviction deepens → repeat

The cycle is driven by the short-term safety that mask and not-authenticity provides (mask-because-the-mask-is-the-protection-from-the-exposure and not-authenticity-because-the-not-authenticity-is-the-control-over-the-outcome both feel like they are preventing the exposure, and the feeling reduces the fear of being a mask temporarily) and maintained by the long-term cost of the i-am-a-mask pattern (the authenticity is never experienced, because the authenticity is always the not-yet-the-authenticity; the authenticity is never exercised for the self, because the being-authenticity is always the not-yet-the-authenticity; and the i-am-a-mask conviction deepens because every prevented authenticity is treated as evidence that the authenticity was the danger). The tragic irony of fear of being a mask is that the safety strategy (mask, not-authenticity, concealment-as-safety) produces the very outcome it was meant to prevent (the person refuses the authenticity and the refusing-the-authenticity produces the very harm the mask was meant to disprove, and the chronic not-allowing-authenticity is the loss of the authenticity, not the protection from the exposure — the fear of being a mask prevents the very authenticity that would have made the life worth the authenticity). The CBT intervention breaks the cycle at two points: (1) authenticity-tolerance-clarification — when the i-am-a-mask distortion treats every act of authenticity as a harm, the first step is clarifying that authenticity is a capacity one can exercise without provoking harm, and recognizing that the self that can allow its own authenticity is the self that is authenticity, not the self that is dangerous; (2) values-based-committed-action — when the mask-is-protection distortion fires, one challenges it with the ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999): the valued life includes the willingness to be the self that has authenticity, and the committed action is the being-authenticity that exercises the full range of authenticity.

The 6 Cognitive Distortions & Their Reframes

1. The Authenticity-Is-Dangerous (self-concealment-avoidance)

Distortion: "Authenticity is dangerous. If I allow myself to be the authenticity that is not also the worn, something bad will happen. Authenticity is a weakness of exposure, a setup for the punishment. I must avoid authenticity, because the authenticity is the not-yet-the-authenticity, and the mask is the safety."
Reframe: "Authenticity is a capacity not a weakness — and the i-am-a-mask distortion converts one specific form of experience (sometimes authenticity is followed by consequence) into a universal verdict (all authenticity is the harm, so one is always provoking exposure when one is authenticity). The i-am-a-mask distortion takes a partial truth (consequences happen) and universalizes it (all authenticity causes harm, so one can never be authenticity without endangering oneself). But authenticity is a capacity not a weakness — the act of authenticity is the act of being the self that has authenticity, not the cause of the next exposure; and the self that is authenticity in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not concealment-avoidant by the authenticity. The distortion makes the mask feel like protection (I must avoid authenticity because authenticity is dangerous), and the mask prevents the very authenticity that would reveal that the authenticity was not the danger. The reframe: authenticity is a capacity not a weakness — the safety is the allowing, and the allowing is not the not-concealment authenticity, and the not-concealment authenticity is the authenticity, not the endangering. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own mask-fear with kindness recognizes the i-am-a-mask as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the authenticity self that the mask withheld."

2. The Mask-Is-Protection (treating authenticity as danger)

Distortion: "Mask equals safety. Self-avoidance of authenticity is protection, authenticity is danger — and the authenticity is the evidence of my dangerousness. I must be concealment without authenticity, because the safety is the concealment, and the authenticity is the not-safe."
Reframe: "Mask is one option not safety — and the mask-is-protection distortion converts one specific form of experience (some concealment protects) into a universal verdict (all mask is safety, so one is always endangered when one is authenticity). The mask-is-protection distortion takes a partial truth (some avoidance of authenticity is protective) and universalizes it (all mask is safety, so one can never be authenticity without being endangered). But mask is one option not safety — the self that is concealment-avoidant is not automatically the self that is safe, and the self that is authenticity is not automatically the self that is endangered; and the safety is not the avoidance of authenticity but the authenticity, and the authenticity includes both the chosen mask and the chosen authenticity. The distortion makes the safety feel like safety (I must be concealment without authenticity to be safe), and the safety prevents the very authenticity that would reveal that the authenticity was not the danger. The reframe: mask is one option not safety — the safety is the authenticity of the full range, and the full range includes the authenticity, and the authenticity is not the not-safe but one expression of the safe. Concealment self research is the core frame: the concealment self is a position one can move from, and the self that can be the authenticity is the self that is filled, not the endangered self."

3. The Weakness-Is-Weakness (anxiety when authenticity is available)

Distortion: "If I have authenticity, something bad will happen. The act-of-authenticity is the impending-exposure, the authenticity is the warning — and the authenticity is the evidence of my upcoming punishment. I must not be the authenticity that is not also the worn, because the being-authenticity is the not-yet-the-authenticity."
Reframe: "Authenticity is chosen not faked — and the authenticity-is-dangerous distortion converts one specific form of experience (sometimes being-authenticity leads to consequence) into a universal verdict (all authenticity is harm, so one is always about to be exposed when one has authenticity). The authenticity-is-dangerous distortion takes a partial truth (authenticity has consequences) and universalizes it (all authenticity causes exposure, so one can never have authenticity without being about to be punished). But authenticity is chosen not faked — the act of authenticity is not the cause of the next exposure; and the self that is authenticity for authenticity in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not the not-yet-the-authenticity. The distortion makes the not-authenticity feel like control (I must not be-authenticity to control the outcome), and the not-authenticity prevents the very authenticity that would reveal that the authenticity was not the harm. The reframe: authenticity is chosen not faked — the authenticity is the being, and the being is the authenticity, and the consequence is not caused by the being-authenticity. ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) is the core frame: the valued life includes the willingness to be the self that has authenticity without demanding the guarantee of the outcome, and the committed action is the being-authenticity, not the outcome-controlling."

4. The Authenticity-Is-Danger (treating authenticity as concealment)

Distortion: "Authenticity is concealment. Self-direction is selfishness, authenticity is harm — and the authenticity choice is the evidence of my containment. I must be concealment without authenticity, because the safety is the mask, and the authenticity is the not-ok."
Reframe: "Authenticity is authenticity not concealment — and the weakness-is-weakness distortion converts one specific form of fact (some authenticity is concealment) into a universal verdict (all authenticity is concealment, so one is always concealment when one is authentically choosing). The weakness-is-weakness distortion takes a partial truth (some authenticity is concealment) and universalizes it (all authenticity requires the safety, so one can never be authenticity without being concealment). But authenticity is safety not danger — the self that is authenticity is the self that is authenticity, and the authenticity is the choosing, not the avoiding authenticity; and the self that is authenticity in one moment is the self that is ok in that moment, and the ok is not concealment-avoidant by the authenticity. The distortion makes the safety feel like mask (I must be concealment without authenticity to be authenticity), and the safety prevents the very authenticity that would reveal that the authenticity was not the concealment-only. The reframe: authenticity is safety not danger — the authenticity is the choosing, and the choosing is not the avoiding authenticity, and the avoiding authenticity is one path, not the condition. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own authenticity with kindness recognizes the weakness-is-weakness as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the authenticity self that the mask withheld."

5. The I-Must-Be-The-Worn (identity distortion)

Distortion: "I must be the concealment. My worth is in my concealment, my identity is in my mask — and the authenticity self is the not-me. I must not be the authenticity that is not also the worn, because the authenticity is the not-yet-the-self, and the being-authenticity is the worn-the-not-ok."
Reframe: "The self can be the authenticity — and the i-must-be-the-worn distortion converts one specific form of fact (some concealment is genuine) into a universal verdict (all self is mask, so one is always the not-self when one is authenticity). The i-must-be-the-worn distortion takes a partial truth (the self has concealment) and universalizes it (all identity is mask, so one can never be authenticity without being the not-self). But the self can be the authenticity — the self that is authenticity is the self that is authenticity, and the authenticity is the authenticity, not the avoiding authenticity; and the self that is authenticity in one moment is the self that is authenticity in that moment, and the authenticity is not negated by the authenticity. The distortion makes the avoiding authenticity feel like identity (I must be concealment without authenticity to belong), and the avoiding authenticity prevents the very authenticity that would reveal that the self was not the mask. The reframe: the self can be the authenticity — the identity is the authenticity, and the authenticity is not the avoiding authenticity, and the avoiding authenticity is one feature, not the verdict. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the authenticity is the authenticity of the self, and the authenticity includes the authenticity, and the authenticity is the authenticity, not the authenticity-failure."

6. The My-Self-Is-A-Mask (safety distortion)

Distortion: "Mask means safety. Mask is the worth, mask is the safety — and the authenticity is the evidence of my dangerousness. I must not be authenticity, because the authenticity is the danger, and the avoiding authenticity is the protection."
Reframe: "Self is one's own not a mask — and the my-self-is-a-mask distortion converts one specific form of fact (some mask is protective) into a universal verdict (all safety is concealment, so one is always endangered when one is authenticity). The my-self-is-a-mask distortion takes a partial truth (mask can protect) and universalizes it (all concealment is safety, so one can never be authenticity without being dangerous). But self is one's own not a mask — the self that is authenticity is the self that is authenticity, and the authenticity is the ground for the building authenticity, not the end of it; and the self that is authenticity in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not the not-protected. The distortion makes the not-authenticity feel like safety (I must not be authenticity to be safe), and the not-authenticity prevents the very authenticity that would reveal that the authenticity was not the danger. The reframe: self is one's own not a mask — the safety is the authenticity, and the authenticity includes the authenticity, and the authenticity is the safety-support, not the safety-failure. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the authenticity is the authenticity of the full range, and the full range includes the authenticity and the mask, and the authenticity is the authenticity, not the authenticity-failure."

The 3-Face CBT Protocol for Fear of Being a Mask

Face 1 — Assessment & Psychoeducation: Mask-avoidance auditing (identify the domains where one cannot be the authenticity that is not also the worn, the moments one substitutes concealment for authenticity when authenticity is available, the authentic moments one cannot make, the authenticity one cannot exercise, the authenticity one cannot take). Psychoeducation on the fear of being a mask cycle (authenticity chronically followed by punishment, or chronic reinforcement of concealment-as-safety → i-am-a-mask → self-concealment-avoidance, anxiety-when-authenticity-is-available, authenticity-is-dangerous, mask-is-protection, chronic-self-as-mask → short-term safety → safety reinforces fear → lose the experience of authenticity and the capacity for building authenticity → chronic self-as-mask and inability to be the authenticity → i-am-a-mask conviction deepens → repeat), the concealment self research (the concealment self is a position one can move from, not a permanent condition one is condemned to, and the self that can be the authenticity is the self that is filled, not the self that is endangered — the mask is the concealment of self-positioning itself, not the pathology), and the key insight that fear of being a mask is the emotional root of concealment and inability to be the authenticity (the i-am-a-mask distortion makes the authenticity the weakness, the mask-is-protection distortion makes the avoidance of authenticity the protection). Introduce the ACT values-based committed-action approach (Hayes 1999), the self-determination theory autonomy-need application (Deci & Ryan 2000), the authenticity-oriented practice, and the self-compassion practice (Neff 2003).

Face 2 — Cognitive Restructuring & Skill Building: Identify the 6 cognitive distortions (i-am-a-mask, mask-is-protection, authenticity-is-dangerous, weakness-is-weakness, i-must-be-the-worn, my-self-is-a-mask) and build balanced beliefs. Practice authenticity-tolerance clarification ('Authenticity is a capacity I can exercise without provoking harm; the act of authenticity is the act of being the self that has authenticity, not the cause of the next exposure, and the self that can allow its own authenticity is the self that is authenticity, not the self that is dangerous') to break the self-concealment-avoidance. Apply the values-based-committed-action reframe (the valued life includes the willingness to be the self that has authenticity, and the committed action is the authenticity that exercises the full range of authenticity). Practice autonomy-need restoration (Deci & Ryan 2000: the authenticity is the authenticity, not the harming; 'Am I allowing my own authenticity or avoiding authenticity against the not-yet-the-authenticity' rather than 'Must I be the mask to be ok'). Rehearse self-compassion responses (Neff 2003) for mask-fear scenarios ('This mask-avoidance is hard, and my fear is worthy of care; the safety is the authenticity not the avoiding authenticity, and the kindness I offer myself is the permission to be the authenticity self that the mask withheld'). Distinguish the mask that is genuinely serving values from the not-authenticity that is the mask-avoidance-demand (the first is chosen, the second is driven).

Face 3 — Behavioral Practice & Maintenance: Practice one authenticity-allowance per day that intentionally is the being-the-authenticity-that-is-not-also-the-worn in a context where the mask is the default (one act of authenticity made without dread, one authenticity taken without concealment, one authenticity inhabited without the demand for the mask, one authenticity taken without the demand for the safety), starting with low-stakes (one small authenticity, one safe domain) and escalating to higher-stakes (a significant authenticity exercised without avoiding authenticity, a domain where the not-authenticity is the norm, a authenticity inhabited without the demand for the guarantee). Track the outcome (the authenticity practiced, one's own mask-avoidance during it, the dread that fired, the distortion that maintained the fear, whether the authenticity revealed that the authenticity was not the danger and whether the practicing shrank the i-am-a-mask conviction). Process the discomfort of the authenticity (the fear that fires is the authenticity-is-dangerous distortion, not a signal the authenticity was wrong). Apply self-compassion (Neff 2003) when the fear persists (kindness to the mask-avoidance, not evidence of failure). Use ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) to maintain the practice through the fear ('the authenticity I allow is the authenticity, not the not-concealment authenticity, and the meaning is the allowing, not the not-fearing'). Use concealment self research to shift from avoiding authenticity to allowing: the concealment self is a position one can move from, and the allowing requires the willingness to be the authenticity self first. Use self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) to restore the authenticity through the authenticity: the authenticity builds the authenticity, and the authenticity is the ground, not the gift.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is fear of being a mask the same as fear of being a shell?

Fear of being a mask and fear of being a shell are related but not identical. Fear of being a shell is the conviction that content is dangerous and shell is safety — the anticipation that being the self that has holding will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is shell-tolerant-of-container. Fear of being a mask is the conviction that authenticity is dangerous and mask is safety — the anticipation that being the self that has authenticity will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is mask-tolerant-of-concealment. The overlap is integral: the shell-fearing self may fear being a mask (the not-being-the-content requires the concealment, and the concealment is the safety, so the only safety is in the mask), and the mask-fearing self can fear being a shell (the authenticity might require the being-content, and the being-content is the weakness, so the only safety is in the shell). But the primary distortion differs: fear of being a shell's distortion is about the container dimension as threat (is the self that is only the container safe), fear of being a mask's distortion is about the concealment dimension as threat (is the self that is only the concealment safe). A person can fear being a shell without dominant mask-fear (anxious about being the container while being basically able to be authenticity), and a person can fear being a mask without dominant shell-fear (unable to rest in authenticity while being basically unbothered about container). In practice, the two patterns often co-occur, and CBT for one will often address the other. The self-compassion (Neff 2003), ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999), and concealment self research tools on this page address the underlying mask-avoidance pattern.

How is fear of being a mask different from fear of being a hollow?

Fear of being a mask and fear of being a hollow overlap but differ in focus. Fear of being a hollow is the conviction that substance is dangerous and hollow is safety — the anticipation that being the self that has filling will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is hollow-tolerant-of-void. Fear of being a mask is the conviction that authenticity is dangerous and mask is safety — the anticipation that being the self that has authenticity will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is mask-tolerant-of-concealment. Fear of being a hollow is about the void dimension as threat (is the self that is only empty safe); fear of being a mask is about the concealment dimension as threat (is the self that is only the concealment safe). A person can fear being a hollow without dominant mask-fear (anxious about being empty while being basically able to be authenticity), and a person can fear being a mask without dominant hollow-fear (unable to rest in authenticity while being basically ok with not being empty). In practice, the two patterns can interact (the hollow-fearing self may fear being a mask — the substance might reveal the authenticity, and the authenticity is the weakness, so the only safety is in the mask; and the mask-fearing self may fear being a hollow — the authenticity is the authenticity, and the authenticity is the dangerous), and the CBT tools on this page address the underlying mask-avoidance pattern. The self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the unifying frame: both patterns involve a threat to the self's acceptability (the substance self and the authenticity self both threaten the concealment-self), and the restoration — through acceptance of the full range of experience rather than demand for the self to be concealment — is the core intervention.

What if I really do need mask — isn't some concealment appropriate?

Yes, some concealment is appropriate and adaptive. The concealment self research does not pathologize the mask — the mask is a genuine position the self can occupy, and there are contexts where being the concealment is the right response (when authenticity would be unsafe, when the situation calls for concealment-orientation, when one is genuinely conserving energy for a later authenticity). The problem is not the mask itself but the rigid, universal, fear-driven mask — the mask that is not chosen but compelled, the mask that is not one option but the only option, the mask that is not adaptive but avoidant. The CBT protocol helps distinguish the chosen mask (which serves values and is flexible) from the fear-driven mask (which serves avoidance and is rigid). The goal is not to eliminate the mask but to restore choice — so the self can be the concealment when concealment serves and can be the authenticity when authenticity serves, and the selecting is the autonomy, not the mask. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the autonomy is in the choosing, not in the concealment, and the choosing includes both the mask and the authenticity.

How long does CBT for fear of being a mask take?

The timeline varies by individual and severity. For mild mask-avoidance patterns, 8-12 sessions of CBT with the cognitive restructuring, ACT values-based committed-action, and self-compassion faces often produces meaningful improvement. For more entrenched patterns (chronic self-as-mask dating back to childhood, co-occurring depression or anxiety disorders), longer treatment (20+ sessions) may be needed, and integration with other evidence-based approaches (e.g., exposure-based practices for the authenticity-avoidance, schema therapy for the early mask-reinforcement patterns) can be helpful. The concealment self research suggests the concealment self is a position one can move from, not a permanent condition — which means change is possible regardless of how long the pattern has persisted, but the depth of the pattern informs the intensity of the work. The tools on this page are self-help starting points; for entrenched patterns, working with a CBT therapist who can tailor the protocol to your specific mask-avoidance patterns is recommended.

Is fear of being a mask a recognized diagnosis?

Fear of being a mask is not a formal diagnosis in the DSM-5 or ICD-11. It is a psychological pattern — a cluster of cognitive distortions, emotional responses, and behavioral tendencies that produces real suffering and that CBT can address. The cognitive distortions that maintain it (i-am-a-mask, mask-is-protection, authenticity-is-dangerous, weakness-is-weakness, i-must-be-the-worn, my-self-is-a-mask) are recognized in the CBT literature. The ACT values-based committed-action approach (Hayes 1999) for experiential avoidance is empirically supported. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is empirically supported for reducing the self-criticism that maintains the pattern. If fear of being a mask is part of a broader pattern (social anxiety, avoidant personality features, depression, trauma response), the formal diagnosis would be for the broader pattern, and the mask-avoidance would be a target within that treatment. The tools on this page address the pattern regardless of whether it meets diagnostic thresholds.
