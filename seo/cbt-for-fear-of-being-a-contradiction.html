Fear of being a contradiction — the conviction that coherence is dangerous and contradiction is safety, the anticipation that holding consistent beliefs will be punished or lead to harm, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is contradiction-tolerant-of-fragmentation and the inconsistency is the protection — is one of the most pervasive and painful emotional patterns in contradiction-conditioned culture. It is the emotional root that drives self-contradiction-avoidance (the substitution of mutually exclusive beliefs for integrated coherence when coherence is available, the inability to hold a consistent position, the conversion of every act of integration into a harm), anxiety when coherence is available (the coherence itself produces dread, the consistency is the trigger, the available coherence is the warning signal), treating coherence as dangerous/harmful (the person cannot allow the integration because the integration is the not-yet-the-exposure, the person cannot hold the consistent position because the holding is the not-yet-the-exposed), equating contradiction with safety (the belief that fragmentation is protection and coherence is danger, the framing of the inconsistency as the worth and the integration as the threat), and chronic self-contradiction (the persistent substitution of contradiction for integration to avoid the coherence of coherence, the inability to be integrated because the coherence is the endangered). It is distinct from fear of being a hypocrite (which is about the moral dimension — the gap between professed values and actual behavior) and from fear of being a fraud (which is about the ontological dimension — the self IS fundamentally fake). Fear of being a contradiction is about the logical dimension: the self that contains mutually exclusive beliefs, identities, or desires, and the exposure is the danger, the self that cannot rest in coherence because the coherence is the danger, the self that experiences its own integration as a vulnerability of exposure, the self that treats every consistent position as a threat and every coherence as the not-yet-the-exposed.

Fear of being a contradiction is the emotional root of three downstream patterns. The i-am-a-contradiction distortion makes every act of integration a harm and every coherence a vulnerability, producing self-contradiction-avoidance and anxiety-when-coherence-is-available (the person concludes the only safety is in the contradiction and the contradiction requires the not-holding-the-consistency, so the coherence is refused and the coherence is never exercised because the coherence is always the not-yet-the-exposed). The contradiction-is-protection distortion makes the inconsistency the protection and the integration the danger, producing the chronic devaluation of coherence and the equation of fragmentation with worth (the person experiences the coherence and the coherence is the dangerous, and the dangerous is the not-safe, and the not-safe is the threat the contradiction was meant to prevent). And the coherence-is-dangerous distortion makes the act-of-integration the impending-exposure and the consistency the warning, producing chronic-self-contradiction and the constant-avoidance of coherence-requirement (the person has coherence and the coherence is the harm, and the harm is the consequence, and the consequence is the exposure the contradiction was meant to prevent). Fear of being a contradiction is the upstream cause; self-contradiction-avoidance, anxiety-when-coherence-is-available, and chronic-self-contradiction are the downstream effects. The key insight for fear of being a contradiction: coherence is not the danger the contradiction protects from — the self that can allow its own coherence is the self that is whole, not the self that is dangerous, and the safety is not the contradiction but the integrating, and the integrating is the holding, not the fragmenting. Cognitive dissonance research (Festinger 1957) provides the deeper frame: coherence-seeking is associated with greater well-being, lower anxiety, and more fulfilling belief integration — the self that allows coherence is the self that is at peace, not the self that is endangered by the consistency.

The Fear of Being a Contradiction Cycle (How It Develops)

The fear of being a contradiction cycle is the self-reinforcing loop that produces the self-contradiction-avoidance, the anxiety-when-coherence-is-available, and the chronic-self-contradiction that come from concluding that coherence is dangerous and contradiction is safety:

A situation with coherence chronically followed by punishment, or chronic reinforcement of fragmentation-as-safety (an early experience where holding consistent beliefs was criticized, where the integrated position was shamed, where the coherence preceded the harm, a chronic reinforcement of contradiction where one's sense of value was entirely defined by their capacity to hold opposites and never by their capacity to integrate) → i-am-a-contradiction fires (the coherence is the danger, the consistency is the vulnerability, the coherence is the warning, the coherence is the not-yet-the-exposed) → self-contradiction-avoidance, anxiety-when-coherence-is-available, coherence-is-dangerous, contradiction-is-protection, chronic-self-contradiction (the substitution of fragmentation for integration, the inability to hold a consistent position, the constant avoidance of coherence, the devaluation of consistency, the constant contradiction-as-safety) → short-term safety achieved (the contradiction feels like protection from the exposure, the not-holding-the-consistency feels like the control over the outcome, the fragmentation feels like the safety) → the safety reinforces the fear (the contradiction prevents the integrating that would reveal that the coherence was not the danger, the not-integrating prevents the evidence that would disconfirm the i-am-a-contradiction conviction, the fragmentation-as-safety prevents the coherence that would reveal that the coherence was not the danger) → the person loses the experience of coherence and the capacity for integrated living (the coherence that comes from the allowing is never experienced, because the allowing is always the not-yet-the-safe; the coherence that comes from the holding-consistent-beliefs is never experienced, because the holding is always the not-yet-the-exposed) → chronic self-contradiction and inability to hold a consistent position → i-am-a-contradiction conviction deepens → repeat

The cycle is driven by the short-term safety that contradiction and not-integrating provides (contradiction-because-the-contradiction-is-the-protection-from-the-exposure and not-integrating-because-the-not-integrating-is-the-control-over-the-outcome both feel like they are preventing the exposure, and the feeling reduces the fear of being a contradiction temporarily) and maintained by the long-term cost of the i-am-a-contradiction pattern (the coherence is never experienced, because the coherence is always the not-yet-the-safe; the coherence is never exercised for the self, because the holding is always the not-yet-the-exposed; and the i-am-a-contradiction conviction deepens because every prevented coherence is treated as evidence that the coherence was the danger). The tragic irony of fear of being a contradiction is that the safety strategy (contradiction, not-integrating, fragmentation-as-safety) produces the very outcome it was meant to prevent (the person refuses the coherence and the refusing-the-coherence produces the very harm the contradiction was meant to disprove, and the chronic not-allowing-coherence is the loss of the integration, not the protection from the exposure — the fear of being a contradiction prevents the very coherence that would have made the life worth the living). The CBT intervention breaks the cycle at two points: (1) coherence-tolerance-clarification — when the i-am-a-contradiction distortion treats every act of integration as a harm, the first step is clarifying that coherence is a capacity one can exercise without provoking harm, and recognizing that the self that can allow its own coherence is the self that is whole, not the self that is dangerous; (2) values-based-committed-action — when the contradiction-is-protection distortion fires, one challenges it with the ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999): the valued life includes the willingness to hold coherent beliefs, and the committed action is the integrating that exercises the full range of belief-integration.

The 6 Cognitive Distortions & Their Reframes

1. The Coherence-Is-Dangerous (self-contradiction-avoidance)

Distortion: "Coherence is dangerous. If I allow myself to hold consistent beliefs, something bad will happen. Consistency is a vulnerability of exposure, a setup for the punishment. I must avoid coherence, because the coherence is the not-yet-the-exposed, and the contradiction is the safety."
Reframe: "Coherence is a capacity not a vulnerability — and the i-am-a-contradiction distortion converts one specific form of experience (sometimes coherence is followed by consequence) into a universal verdict (all coherence is the harm, so one is always provoking exposure when one is integrated). The i-am-a-contradiction distortion takes a partial truth (consequences happen) and universalizes it (all coherence causes harm, so one can never be integrated without endangering oneself). But coherence is a capacity not a vulnerability — the act of integration is the act of holding one's beliefs, not the cause of the next exposure; and the self that is coherent in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not fragmentation-avoidant by the coherence. The distortion makes the contradiction feel like protection (I must avoid coherence because coherence is dangerous), and the contradiction prevents the very coherence that would reveal that the coherence was not the danger. The reframe: coherence is a capacity not a vulnerability — the safety is the allowing, and the allowing is not the not-fragmenting coherence, and the not-fragmenting coherence is the integrating, not the endangering. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own contradiction-fear with kindness recognizes the i-am-a-contradiction as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the integrated self that the contradiction withheld."

2. The Contradiction-Is-Protection (treating coherence as danger)

Distortion: "Contradiction equals safety. Self-avoidance of coherence is protection, coherence is danger — and the consistency is the evidence of my dangerousness. I must fragment without integrating, because the safety is the inconsistency, and the coherence is the not-safe."
Reframe: "Contradiction is one option not safety — and the contradiction-is-protection distortion converts one specific form of experience (some fragmentation protects) into a universal verdict (all contradiction is safety, so one is always endangered when one is integrated). The contradiction-is-protection distortion takes a partial truth (some avoidance of coherence is protective) and universalizes it (all contradiction is safety, so one can never be integrated without being endangered). But contradiction is one option not safety — the self that is fragmentation-avoidant is not automatically the self that is safe, and the self that is coherent is not automatically the self that is endangered; and the safety is not the avoidance of coherence but the integrating, and the integrating includes both the chosen contradiction and the chosen coherence. The distortion makes the safety feel like safety (I must fragment without integrating to be safe), and the safety prevents the very coherence that would reveal that the coherence was not the danger. The reframe: contradiction is one option not safety — the safety is the integrating of the full range, and the full range includes the coherence, and the coherence is not the not-safe but one expression of the safe. Cognitive dissonance research (Festinger 1957) is the core frame: coherence-seeking is associated with greater well-being, and the integrated self is the whole self, not the endangered self."

3. The Consistency-Is-Vulnerability (anxiety when coherence is available)

Distortion: "If I have coherence, something bad will happen. The act-of-integration is the impending-exposure, the consistency is the warning — and the coherence is the evidence of my upcoming punishment. I must not hold consistent beliefs, because the holding is the not-yet-the-exposed."
Reframe: "Consistency is chosen not faked — and the coherence-is-dangerous distortion converts one specific form of experience (sometimes being-integrated leads to consequence) into a universal verdict (all coherence is harm, so one is always about to be exposed when one has coherence). The coherence-is-dangerous distortion takes a partial truth (coherence has consequences) and universalizes it (all coherence causes exposure, so one can never have coherence without being about to be punished). But consistency is chosen not faked — the act of integration is not the cause of the next exposure; and the self that is present for coherence in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not the not-yet-the-exposed. The distortion makes the not-integrating feel like control (I must not hold to control the outcome), and the not-integrating prevents the very presence that would reveal that the coherence was not the harm. The reframe: consistency is chosen not faked — the present is the present, and the coherence is the coherence, and the consequence is not caused by the being-integrated. ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) is the core frame: the valued life includes the willingness to hold one's beliefs without demanding the guarantee of the outcome, and the committed action is the being-integrated, not the outcome-controlling."

4. The Coherence-Is-Danger (treating coherence as fragmented)

Distortion: "Coherence is fragmented. Self-direction is selfishness, coherence is harm — and the consistent choice is the evidence of my incoherence. I must fragment without integrating, because the safety is the contradiction, and the belief-integration is the not-ok."
Reframe: "Coherence is coherence not fragmented — and the consistency-is-vulnerability distortion converts one specific form of fact (some coherence is fragmented) into a universal verdict (all belief-integration is fragmented, so one is always incoherent when one is consistently choosing). The consistency-is-vulnerability distortion takes a partial truth (some coherence is fragmented) and universalizes it (all coherence requires the safety, so one can never be integrated without being fragmented). But coherence is safety not danger — the self that is coherent is the self that is coherent, and the coherence is the choosing, not the avoiding coherence; and the self that is coherent in one moment is the self that is ok in that moment, and the ok is not fragmentation-avoidant by the belief-integration. The distortion makes the safety feel like contradiction (I must fragment without integrating to be coherent), and the safety prevents the very coherence that would reveal that the coherence was not the fragmented. The reframe: coherence is safety not danger — the coherence is the choosing, and the choosing is not the avoiding coherence, and the avoiding coherence is one path, not the condition. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own coherence with kindness recognizes the consistency-is-vulnerability as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the integrated self that the contradiction withheld."

5. The I-Must-Contain-Opposites (identity distortion)

Distortion: "I must contain opposites. My worth is in my inconsistency, my identity is in my fragmentation — and the coherent self is the not-me. I must not hold consistent beliefs, because the coherence is the not-yet-the-self, and the holding is the becoming-the-not-ok."
Reframe: "The self can be integrated — and the i-must-contain-opposites distortion converts one specific form of fact (some inconsistencies are genuine) into a universal verdict (all self is fragmentation, so one is always the not-self when one is coherent). The i-must-contain-opposites distortion takes a partial truth (the self has inconsistencies) and universalizes it (all identity is fragmentation, so one can never be coherent without being the not-self). But the self can be integrated — the self that is coherent is the self that is whole, and the wholeness is the integrating, not the avoiding coherence; and the self that is coherent in one moment is the self that is coherent in that moment, and the coherent is not negated by the integration. The distortion makes the avoiding coherence feel like identity (I must fragment without integrating to belong), and the avoiding coherence prevents the very coherence that would reveal that the self was not the contradiction. The reframe: the self can be integrated — the identity is the integrating, and the integrating is not the avoiding coherence, and the avoiding coherence is one feature, not the verdict. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the coherence is the living of the self, and the living includes the coherence, and the coherence is the belief-integration, not the coherence-failure."

6. The My-Beliefs-Are-Fragmented (safety distortion)

Distortion: "Fragmentation means safety. Inconsistency is the worth, self-fragmentation is the safety — and the coherence is the evidence of my dangerousness. I must not be coherent, because the coherence is the danger, and the avoiding coherence is the protection."
Reframe: "Beliefs are one's own not fragmented — and the my-beliefs-are-fragmented distortion converts one specific form of fact (some contradiction is protective) into a universal verdict (all safety is fragmentation, so one is always endangered when one is coherent). The my-beliefs-are-fragmented distortion takes a partial truth (contradiction can protect) and universalizes it (all fragmentation is safety, so one can never be coherent without being dangerous). But beliefs are one's own not fragmented — the self that is coherent is the self that is whole, and the coherence is the ground for the integrated living, not the end of it; and the self that is coherent in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not the not-protected. The distortion makes the not-coherent feel like safety (I must not be coherent to be safe), and the not-coherent prevents the very coherence that would reveal that the coherence was not the danger. The reframe: beliefs are one's own not fragmented — the safety is the integrating, and the integrating includes the coherence, and the coherence is the safety-support, not the safety-failure. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the coherence is the living of the full range, and the full range includes the coherence and the contradiction, and the coherence is the belief-integration, not the coherence-failure."

The 3-Component CBT Protocol for Fear of Being a Contradiction

Component 1 — Assessment & Psychoeducation: Contradiction-avoidance auditing (identify the domains where one cannot hold consistent beliefs, the moments one substitutes fragmentation for integration when coherence is available, the beliefs one cannot integrate, the coherence one cannot exercise, the belief-integration one cannot take). Psychoeducation on the fear of being a contradiction cycle (coherence chronically followed by punishment, or chronic reinforcement of fragmentation-as-safety → i-am-a-contradiction → self-contradiction-avoidance, anxiety-when-coherence-is-available, coherence-is-dangerous, contradiction-is-protection, chronic-self-contradiction → short-term safety → safety reinforces fear → lose the experience of coherence and the capacity for integrated living → chronic self-contradiction and inability to hold a consistent position → i-am-a-contradiction conviction deepens → repeat), the cognitive dissonance research (Festinger 1957: coherence-seeking is associated with greater well-being, lower anxiety, and more fulfilling belief integration), and the key insight that fear of being a contradiction is the emotional root of fragmentation and inability to hold a consistent position (the i-am-a-contradiction distortion makes the coherence the vulnerability, the contradiction-is-protection distortion makes the avoidance of coherence the protection). Introduce the ACT values-based committed-action approach (Hayes 1999), the self-determination theory autonomy-need application (Deci & Ryan 2000), the coherence-oriented practice, and the self-compassion practice (Neff 2003).

Component 2 — Cognitive Restructuring & Skill Building: Identify the 6 cognitive distortions (i-am-a-contradiction, contradiction-is-protection, coherence-is-dangerous, consistency-is-vulnerability, i-must-contain-opposites, my-beliefs-are-fragmented) and build balanced beliefs. Practice coherence-tolerance clarification ('Coherence is a capacity I can exercise without provoking harm; the act of integration is the act of holding my beliefs, not the cause of the next exposure, and the self that can allow its own coherence is the self that is whole, not the self that is dangerous') to break the self-contradiction-avoidance. Apply the values-based-committed-action reframe (the valued life includes the willingness to hold coherent beliefs, and the committed action is the integrating that exercises the full range of belief-integration). Practice autonomy-need restoration (Deci & Ryan 2000: the coherence is the living, not the harming; 'Am I allowing my own coherence or avoiding coherence against the not-yet-the-exposed' rather than 'Must I fragment to be ok'). Rehearse self-compassion responses (Neff 2003) for contradiction-fear scenarios ('This contradiction-avoidance is hard, and my fear is worthy of care; the safety is the integrating not the avoiding coherence, and the kindness I offer myself is the permission to be the integrated self that the contradiction withheld'). Distinguish the contradiction that is genuinely serving values from the not-integrating that is the contradiction-avoidance-demand (the first is chosen, the second is driven).

Component 3 — Behavioral Practice & Maintenance: Practice one coherence-allowance per day that intentionally is the holding-of-beliefs in a context where the contradiction is the default (one act of coherence made without dread, one belief integrated without self-fragmentation, one consistency inhabited without the demand for the contradiction, one belief-integration taken without the demand for the safety), starting with low-stakes (one small coherence, one safe domain) and escalating to higher-stakes (a significant coherence exercised without avoiding coherence, a domain where the not-integrating is the norm, a coherence inhabited without the demand for the guarantee). Track the outcome (the coherence practiced, one's own contradiction-avoidance during it, the dread that fired, the distortion that maintained the fear, whether the coherence revealed that the coherence was not the danger and whether the practicing shrank the i-am-a-contradiction conviction). Process the discomfort of the coherence (the fear that fires is the coherence-is-dangerous distortion, not a signal the coherence was wrong). Apply self-compassion (Neff 2003) when the fear persists (kindness to the contradiction-avoidance, not evidence of failure). Use ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) to maintain the practice through the fear ('the coherence I allow is the living, not the not-fragmenting coherence, and the meaning is the allowing, not the not-fearing'). Use cognitive dissonance research (Festinger 1957) to shift from avoiding coherence to allowing: coherence-seeking is associated with greater well-being, and the allowing requires the willingness to be the integrated self first. Use self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) to restore the coherence through the coherence: the coherence builds the coherence, and the coherence is the living, not the contradiction-avoidant-self. Generalize to higher-stakes domains as the i-am-a-contradiction conviction shrinks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is fear of being a contradiction the same as fear of being a hypocrite?

Fear of being a contradiction and fear of being a hypocrite are related but not identical. Fear of being a hypocrite is the conviction that integrity is dangerous and hypocrisy is safety — the anticipation that living one's values will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is profession-avoidant-of-enactment. Fear of being a contradiction is the conviction that coherence is dangerous and contradiction is safety — the anticipation that holding consistent beliefs will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is contradiction-tolerant-of-fragmentation. The overlap is substantial: the hypocrisy-fearing self may fear being a contradiction (the integrity requires the coherence, and the coherence is the dangerous, so the only safety is in the contradiction), and the contradiction-fearing self can fear being a hypocrite (the coherence might require the integrity, and the integrity is the vulnerability, so the only safety is in the fragmentation). But the primary distortion differs: fear of being a hypocrite's distortion is about the moral dimension as threat (is the gap between professed values and actual behavior safe), fear of being a contradiction's distortion is about the logical dimension as threat (is the self containing mutually exclusive beliefs safe). A person can fear being a hypocrite without dominant contradiction-fear (anxious about values-behavior gap while being basically able to hold consistent beliefs), and a person can fear being a contradiction without dominant hypocrisy-fear (unable to rest in coherence while being basically unbothered about moral dimension). In practice, the two patterns often co-occur, and CBT for one will often address the other. The self-compassion (Neff 2003), ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999), and cognitive dissonance research (Festinger 1957) tools on this page serve both patterns: they build the capacity to allow coherence (fear of being a contradiction) and the capacity to allow integrity (fear of being a hypocrite).

How is fear of being a contradiction different from fear of being a fraud?

Fear of being a contradiction and fear of being a fraud overlap but differ in focus. Fear of being a fraud is the conviction that the genuine self is dangerous and the fraudulence is the safety — the anticipation that genuineness will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is maskulence-avoidant. Fear of being a contradiction is the conviction that coherence is dangerous and contradiction is safety — the anticipation that holding consistent beliefs will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is contradiction-tolerant-of-fragmentation. Fear of being a fraud is about the ontological dimension as threat (is the self fundamentally fake); fear of being a contradiction is about the logical dimension as threat (is the self containing mutually exclusive beliefs safe). A person can fear being a fraud without dominant contradiction-fear (anxious about ontological fakeness while being basically able to hold consistent beliefs), and a person can fear being a contradiction without dominant fraud-fear (unable to rest in coherence while being basically ok with ontological genuineness). In practice, the two patterns can interact (the fraud-fearing self may fear being a contradiction — the coherence might reveal the genuineness, and the genuineness is the vulnerability, so the only safety is in the contradiction; and the contradiction-fearing self may fear being a fraud — the genuineness is the coherent, and the coherent is the dangerous), and the CBT tools on this page address the underlying contradiction-avoidance pattern. The self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the unifying frame: both patterns involve a threat to the self's acceptability (the genuine self and the coherent self both threaten the fragmented-self), and the restoration — through acceptance of the full range of experience rather than demand for the self to be fragmented — is the core intervention.

What if I really do need contradiction — isn't some holding-opposites appropriate?

Yes — and this is a crucial nuance. Fear of being a contradiction is not the claim that all contradiction is bad or that one should never hold opposites. The fragmented self is the self that chooses many forms of inconsistency, and the contradiction that serves one's values is the valued-action, not the contradiction-avoidance-driven avoiding coherence. The diagnostic question is not 'am I being fragmented' but 'am I living my life or avoiding coherence against the not-yet-the-exposed'. The avoiding coherence that comes from the i-am-a-contradiction distortion has a specific signature: it is driven (the person cannot inhabit coherence, the coherence produces dread, the coherence feels like a harm), not chosen (the person could inhabit coherence, the contradiction is welcome, the coherence is a gift). The contradiction that serves values is chosen and can include coherence; the avoiding coherence that serves the contradiction-avoidance distortion is driven and cannot include coherence. The ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) framework makes this explicit: the valued life includes both contradiction and coherence, and the coherence is not the not-safety but the ground from which the contradiction comes. The goal of CBT for fear of being a contradiction is not to eliminate contradiction but to liberate it — to move from avoiding coherence that is driven by the fear of being a contradiction (and therefore cannot pause, and therefore anxiously guards against coherence, and therefore is unsustainable) to contradiction that is chosen from values (and therefore can pause, and therefore is sustainable, and therefore is more effective). The self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the coherence is the living of the full range, and the full range includes the contradiction and the coherence, and the coherence is the coherence-support, not the coherence-failure.

What does CBT for Fear of Being a Contradiction involve and does it work?

CBT for Fear of Being a Contradiction involves coherence-tolerance clarification, cognitive restructuring of the contradiction-avoidance distortions, ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999), self-determination theory autonomy-need application (Deci & Ryan 2000), coherence-oriented practice, and self-compassion practice (Neff 2003). The core components: (1) coherence-tolerance clarification — the i-am-a-contradiction distortion treats every act of integration as a harm; the first step is clarifying that coherence is a capacity one can exercise without provoking harm, and recognizing that the self that can allow its own coherence is the self that is whole, not the self that is dangerous; (2) cognitive restructuring — identifying the 6 cognitive distortions (i-am-a-contradiction, contradiction-is-protection, coherence-is-dangerous, consistency-is-vulnerability, i-must-contain-opposites, my-beliefs-are-fragmented) and building balanced beliefs (coherence is a capacity not a vulnerability, contradiction is one option not safety, consistency is chosen not faked, coherence is safety not danger, the self can be integrated, and beliefs are one's own not fragmented); (3) ACT values-based committed-action — the Hayes (1999) approach: the valued life includes the willingness to hold coherent beliefs, and the committed action is the integrating that exercises the full range of belief-integration. Yes, CBT for fear of being a contradiction works — the cognitive distortions are well-established in the CBT literature, the ACT values-based committed-action approach has strong empirical support for experiential avoidance patterns, self-compassion (Neff 2003) is empirically supported for reducing the self-criticism that maintains the contradiction-avoidance, and cognitive dissonance research (Festinger 1957) documents that coherence-seeking is associated with greater well-being and lower anxiety.
