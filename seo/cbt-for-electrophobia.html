Electrophobia is an intense irrational fear of fear of electricity or electrical devices. It is a specific phobia (DSM-5 300.29). It becomes clinically significant when it is: (1) persistent and leads to excessive avoidance; (2) driven by cognitive distortions, not a realistic assessment of risk; (3) the person experiences anxiety when encountering triggers and relief when avoiding them; (4) safety behaviors include excessive avoidance and checking; (5) manifests as distress in triggering situations and relief after avoidance. The avoidance is what CBT targets — and the key insight is that electrophobia is maintained by the cognitive distortions described below, plus avoidance reinforcement (avoiding triggers prevents the feared outcome, confirming the fear).

Cognitive Distortions → Reframes

The three most common distortion-reframe patterns in electrophobia:

Distortion (Catastrophizing / Probability Overestimation): "Electricity will electrocute me, any device will short circuit and cause a fire, and being near power is a death sentence. Every outlet is a potential killer. The probability of electrocution is near certain whenever I touch a device."
Reframe: "The belief that every electrical device will electrocute and that harm is near-certain is the catastrophizing and probability overestimation distortion that electrophobia has created. The reality is that modern electrical devices are engineered with multiple safety features (insulation, grounding, circuit breakers), and the probability of electrocution from normal use is extremely low. The evidence-based response is cognitive restructuring and probability correction. I can reframe: devices are engineered for safety; the probability of harm is low; I have used countless devices safely; catastrophizing overestimates harm."
Distortion (Hypervigilance / Somatic Focus): "I can feel the electricity in the walls and it means danger is present. Every hum or warmth from a device is a warning sign. My constant monitoring of outlets and cords is keeping me safe. The slightest tingle means electrocution is coming."
Reframe: "The belief that every electrical hum is a warning and that constant monitoring prevents harm is the hypervigilance and somatic-focus distortion that electrophobia has created. The reality is that normal device operation (humming, warmth) is not a danger signal, and hypervigilance to electrical cues actually increases anxiety. The evidence-based response is cognitive restructuring and attentional retraining. I can reframe: normal operation sounds are not danger; somatic focus is not evidence; hypervigilance maintains fear, not safety."
Distortion (Safety Behavior / Avoidance-Reinforcement): "I must avoid electrical devices whenever possible. Staying away from power keeps me safe from electrocution. The fact that I have not been shocked is proof that my avoidance is working. If I stop avoiding, the electrocution I fear will finally happen."
Reframe: "The belief that you must avoid electricity and that staying away keeps you safe is the safety behavior and avoidance-reinforcement distortion that electrophobia has created. The reality is that avoidance maintains the phobia and chronic electrical avoidance causes practical harm (unable to use essential devices, social impairment). The evidence-based response is graded exposure. I can reframe: avoidance maintains fear and causes real harm; electrical devices are safe; correlation is not causation; exposure reduces anxiety over time; modern devices are insulated."

Frequently Asked Questions

What is electrophobia and how common is it?

Electrophobia is an intense irrational fear of electricity or electrical devices. It is a specific phobia (DSM-5 300.29) closely related to technophobia (fear of technology) and astraphobia (fear of lightning). It frequently overlaps with health anxiety and contamination fears. Specific phobias affect approximately 7-9% of the population. CBT is highly effective with 60-80% reduction in phobic anxiety.

How is electrophobia different from technophobia or astraphobia?

Electrophobia is closely related to technophobia but centers specifically on electricity and electrocution rather than technology broadly, while astraphobia focuses on lightning as a natural phenomenon. The key insight is that electrophobia is maintained by catastrophizing and probability overestimation, plus avoidance reinforcement. CBT with cognitive restructuring and graded exposure directly targets these distortions.

What does CBT for electrophobia involve and does it work?

CBT for electrophobia involves cognitive restructuring, graded exposure, and relaxation techniques. The core components: (1) cognitive restructuring challenging the catastrophizing and probability overestimation; (2) graded exposure (progressively use electrical devices); (3) relaxation. CBT is evidence-based with 60-80% reduction in phobic anxiety.
