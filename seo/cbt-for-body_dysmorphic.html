Body dysmorphic disorder (BDD) is a mental health condition characterized by persistent preoccupation with a perceived defect or flaw in appearance that is either unnoticeable or appears slight to others. It affects approximately 1-2% of the population and is classified as a distinct disorder in DSM-5 (300.7). The preoccupation can focus on any body area but commonly involves skin (acne, scars, wrinkles), hair (thinning, loss), nose, or overall body shape. It is related to muscle dysmorphia (a subtype focused on perceived insufficient muscularity), appearance anxiety, selfie dysmorphia, Snapchat dysmorphia, and nose dysmorphia. It often co-occurs with depression, social anxiety, and OCD. The all-or-nothing thinking that the appearance is completely ruined is what CBT targets — and the key insight is that BDD is maintained by all-or-nothing thinking plus mind-reading plus catastrophizing, combined with repetitive safety behaviors (mirror checking, grooming, comparing, reassurance seeking), which is why ERP that prevents these safety behaviors is so powerful.

Cognitive Distortions → Reframes

The three most common distortion-reframe patterns:

Distortion (All-or-Nothing Thinking): "My skin is completely ruined. My nose is totally deformed. My hair is a disaster. Either my appearance is perfect or it is a catastrophe. There is no middle ground. One blemish means my whole face is ugly. One scar means my skin is destroyed."
Reframe: "The belief that your appearance is completely ruined is the all-or-nothing thinking distortion that BDD has created. The reality is that appearance exists on a continuum, one blemish does not define a whole face, most people have skin imperfections, and the perceived flaw is either unnoticeable or slight to others. The evidence-based response is cognitive restructuring and perceptual retraining. I can reframe: appearance is a continuum; one blemish is not a whole face; imperfections are universal; the flaw is slight to others; I can see my appearance objectively.
Distortion (Mind-Reading): "Everyone notices my flaw. People are staring at my nose. They are judging my skin. She saw my scar and thought I was ugly. He noticed my hair thinning and was disgusted. Everyone can see the defect that I see."
Reframe: "The belief that everyone notices your flaw is the mind-reading distortion that BDD has created. The reality is that others do not perceive the flaw as you do, people are generally focused on themselves not your appearance, you cannot know what others are thinking, and most perceived judgments are projections of your own self-criticism. The evidence-based response is cognitive restructuring and behavioral experiments. I can reframe: others do not see the flaw as I do; people focus on themselves; I cannot read minds; perceived judgments are projections; I can test this with experiments.
Distortion (Catastrophizing): "My appearance ruins my life. I will never find a partner looking like this. My career is over because of my face. No one will accept me with this flaw. My appearance determines my worth. This defect means I am unlovable."
Reframe: "The belief that your appearance ruins your life is the catastrophizing distortion that BDD has created. The reality is that appearance is one factor among many in relationships and success, character and competence matter far more than physical flaws, people are accepted and loved with visible imperfections every day, and your worth is not determined by your appearance. The evidence-based response is cognitive restructuring and ERP. I can reframe: appearance is one factor; character matters more; imperfection does not prevent love; worth is not appearance; I can live a full life with this flaw.

If you are in crisis or experiencing severe symptoms

This tool is educational, not a substitute for professional care. If appearance preoccupation is causing significant distress, repetitive behaviors (mirror checking, grooming, comparing), or avoidance of social situations, professional support is recommended. BDD has elevated rates of suicidal ideation, so professional evaluation is important. Pairing CBT with ERP that prevents safety behaviors produces the best outcomes. If you are experiencing severe anxiety, depression, or suicidal thoughts:

  • US Crisis: Call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, 24/7)
  • UK: Call 116 123 (Samaritans, 24/7)
  • NAMI: 1-800-950-6264 or text "Helpline" to 62640
  • ADAA: Visit adaa.org for anxiety resources
  • International: Visit findahelpline.com for local crisis lines

Frequently Asked Questions

What is body dysmorphic disorder (BDD) and how common is it?

BDD is persistent preoccupation with a perceived appearance defect that others barely notice. It affects 1-2% of the population, classified as DSM-5 300.7. It is related to muscle dysmorphia, appearance anxiety, and selfie dysmorphia. It often co-occurs with depression, social anxiety, and OCD. CBT with ERP is the first-line, evidence-based treatment.

How is BDD different from normal appearance concerns?

Normal concerns are occasional and do not impair functioning; BDD involves persistent preoccupation (often hours per day), repetitive behaviors (mirror checking, comparing, grooming), and significant avoidance. It is maintained by all-or-nothing thinking, mind-reading, and catastrophizing. CBT with ERP directly targets these distortions and the repetitive behaviors.

What does CBT for BDD involve and does it work?

CBT for BDD involves cognitive restructuring, ERP, and perceptual retraining. ERP exposes the person to feared situations while preventing safety behaviors. CBT with ERP is the first-line treatment with strong RCT support, recommended by NICE guidelines. Treatment typically produces significant reduction in BDD symptoms and improved quality of life.
