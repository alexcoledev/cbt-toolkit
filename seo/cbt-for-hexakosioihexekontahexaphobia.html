Hexakosioihexekontahexaphobia is a specific phobia rooted in magical thinking and often connected to religious or cultural beliefs about 666 as the 'number of the beast' from the Book of Revelation. It becomes clinically significant when it is: (1) persistent and leads to avoidance of addresses, phone numbers, license plates, prices, or any context containing 666; (2) driven by a fear of the number itself, not just a mild preference; (3) the person changes routes, channels, or plans to avoid encountering 666; (4) safety behaviors include checking, replacing, or rearranging to eliminate 666; (5) manifests as anticipatory anxiety before situations where 666 might appear, distress upon encountering it, and relief when avoidance succeeds. The driver is the magical thinking that the number has supernatural power to cause harm, the trigger is any situation involving or approaching the number 666, and the content is the belief that 666 is dangerous and will bring misfortune. It is distinct from triskaidekaphobia because the fear is of 666, not 13. It is distinct from OCD scrupulosity because it is a phobic fear of a specific number, not a broader pattern of religious obsession. It is distinct from general superstition because it is clinically significant and drives avoidance. The magical thinking is what CBT targets.

Cognitive Distortions → Reframes

The three most common distortion-reframe patterns in hexakosioihexekontahexaphobia:

Distortion (Magical Thinking / Superstition): "The number 666 is evil. It has supernatural power to cause harm. If I see 666, something bad will happen. The number is cursed, and encountering it invites misfortune into my life. 666 means Satan is near, and seeing it is a sign of danger. I must treat the number as a real threat, not just a symbol. The number has power over me and my fate."
Reframe: "The belief that the number 666 is evil and has supernatural power to cause harm — that is the magical thinking and superstition distortion that hexakosioihexekontahexaphobia has created. The reality is that 666 is a sequence of digits, a symbol that has cultural and religious meaning but no causal power over events. The 'if I see 666, something bad will happen' is the magical thinking that confuses correlation with causation — billions of people encounter 666 daily without harm. The '666 means Satan is near' is the magical thinking that treats a symbol as a supernatural signal. The 'the number has power over me' is the loss-of-control-fear that surrenders agency to a symbol. The evidence-based response is cognitive restructuring (separate symbol from reality, challenge the supernatural attribution) and graded exposure (encounter 666 and learn nothing happens). I can reframe: 666 is a symbol, not a force; seeing it does not cause harm; symbols have cultural meaning, not causal power; I retain agency over my fate."
Distortion (Catastrophizing / Doom-Forecast): "Encountering 666 will bring doom. If I see the number on a license plate, I will have an accident. If my address contains 666, my home is cursed. If a price ends in 666, the purchase will go wrong. Every encounter with the number is a warning of impending disaster. I cannot be safe if 666 is anywhere in my environment."
Reframe: "The belief that encountering 666 will bring doom and every encounter is a warning of disaster — that is the catastrophizing and doom-forecast distortion that hexakosioihexekontahexaphobia has created. The reality is that the number 666 appears in countless contexts (license plates, addresses, prices, phone numbers) with no statistical association with harm. The 'if I see 666 on a license plate, I will have an accident' is the catastrophizing that invents a causal link where none exists. The 'if my address contains 666, my home is cursed' is the magical thinking that attributes supernatural properties to a digit sequence. The 'every encounter is a warning' is the confirmation bias that selectively remembers bad events following 666 encounters while ignoring the vast majority of uneventful ones. The evidence-based response is cognitive restructuring (examine the evidence for and against the doom-forecast) and behavioral experiments (track outcomes after 666 encounters). I can reframe: 666 encounters are common and uneventful; correlation is not causation; I am not doomed by a digit; bad events have real causes, not numerical ones."
Distortion (Safety-Behavior / Avoidance-Reinforcement): "I must avoid anything with 666. I should change routes, channels, or plans to avoid the number. I should replace items, rearrange numbers, or refuse situations containing 666. If I feel anxious about 666, I should escape immediately. These precautions are necessary because the number is dangerous. Avoiding 666 is what keeps me safe."
Reframe: "The belief that you must avoid anything with 666 and that avoidance keeps you safe — that is the safety-behavior and avoidance-reinforcement distortion that hexakosioihexekontahexaphobia has created. The reality is that avoidance prevents disconfirmation of the fear and reinforces the belief that 666 is dangerous. The 'change routes or channels' is the safety-behavior that provides temporary relief but prevents learning the number is harmless. The 'replace items or rearrange numbers' is the avoidance that shrinks your life and maintains the phobia. The 'if I feel anxious, escape immediately' is the safety-behavior that teaches anxiety is intolerable and escape is necessary. The evidence-based response is graded exposure (encounter 666 in various contexts, prevent avoidance, learn anxiety subsides) and response prevention (resist the urge to check, replace, or escape). I can reframe: avoidance reinforces fear; escaping teaches anxiety is intolerable; I can encounter 666 and learn it is harmless; the goal is tolerance, not avoidance."

Frequently Asked Questions

What is hexakosioihexekontahexaphobia and how common is it?

Hexakosioihexekontahexaphobia is an intense, irrational fear of the number 666. The person fears the number itself — encountering it, seeing it, or being associated with it. It is classified as a specific phobia in DSM-5. The fear is rooted in magical thinking (the belief that a number has supernatural power to cause harm) and is often connected to religious or cultural beliefs about 666 as the 'number of the beast' from the Book of Revelation. Many people have mild superstition about 666; hexakosioihexekontahexaphobia is the severe form where the fear drives significant avoidance of addresses, phone numbers, license plates, prices, or any context containing 666. It can develop from religious upbringing, cultural conditioning, or co-occur with OCD scrupulosity. The condition is maintained by avoidance and safety behaviors that prevent learning that the number is harmless. CBT breaks this cycle by targeting the magical thinking and safety behaviors that maintain it.

How is hexakosioihexekontahexaphobia different from related conditions?

Hexakosioihexekontahexaphobia is distinct from triskaidekaphobia (fear of 13) because it concerns a different number with different cultural associations. It is distinct from OCD scrupulosity because it is a phobic fear of a specific number, not a broader pattern of religious or moral obsession (though it can co-occur). It is distinct from general superstition because it is clinically significant and drives avoidance, not just a mild preference. It is distinct from numerology obsession because the fear is of harm from the number, not preoccupation with its meaning. Hexakosioihexekontahexaphobia often co-occurs with OCD, religious scrupulosity, and other specific phobias. The key insight is that the magical thinking creates an avoidance of the number that prevents disconfirmation of the fear.

What does CBT for Hexakosioihexekontahexaphobia involve and does it work?

CBT for Hexakosioihexekontahexaphobia involves cognitive restructuring and graded exposure. The core components: (1) cognitive restructuring challenging the number-666-is-evil (magical thinking: a number has supernatural power), the encountering-666-brings-doom (catastrophizing: seeing the number causes harm), the i-must-avoid-anything-with-666 (avoidance-compulsion: the number must be escaped), the number-has-power-over-me (loss-of-control-fear: the number controls outcomes), the 666-means-satan-is-near (magical thinking: the number signals supernatural presence), and the avoiding-666-protects-me (safety-behavior: avoidance prevents harm); (2) graded exposure to the number 666 in various contexts (seeing it, writing it, hearing it) while preventing safety behaviors.
