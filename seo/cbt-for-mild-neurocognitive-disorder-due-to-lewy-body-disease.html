What is Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to Lewy Body Disease?

Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to Lewy Body Disease (DSM-5 294.0x) is a diagnosis in the Neurocognitive Disorders category, used when: (1) there is evidence of modest cognitive decline from a previous level of performance in one or more cognitive domains (complex attention, executive function, learning and memory, language, perceptual-motor, social cognition) — based on both concern from the individual, a knowledgeable informant, or the clinician, AND objective evidence of decline; (2) the cognitive deficits do NOT interfere with independence in everyday activities (IADLs are preserved), though greater effort, compensatory strategies, or assistance may be needed — this is the 'mild' threshold, distinguishing it from Major NCD; (3) the deficits do not occur exclusively during delirium; (4) the deficits are not better explained by another mental or medical disorder; (5) there is evidence of Lewy body pathology (Lewy body pathology evidenced by fluctuating cognition, recurrent visual hallucinations, spontaneous parkinsonism, and REM sleep behavior disorder). The distinctive clinical features include: fluctuating cognition, visual attention deficits, and executive function decline as the hallmark early feature (difficulty planning, set-shifting, and processing speed, with memory relatively preserved early on), preserved independence in IADLs (the person can still manage medications, finances, appointments, and complex tasks, though with greater effort or compensatory strategies), evidence of Lewy body pathology (Lewy body features: fluctuating cognition, visual hallucinations, parkinsonism), an uncertain progression trajectory (while progression to Major NCD is common, some individuals remain stable or even improve, and the rate of progression varies widely), and a window for early intervention (Lewy body progression control, cognitive engagement, physical exercise, social engagement, and monitoring may slow progression and maximize function during the mild stage). Mild NCD due to Lewy body disease sits on the Lewy body pathology continuum between normal aging and Major NCD due to Lewy body disease, and was previously called mild cognitive impairment (MCI) due to Alzheimer's. The cognitive changes are more pronounced than normal age-related cognitive change but do not meet the major threshold.

Why does mild neurocognitive disorder due to Lewy body disease need CBT instead of just monitoring the symptoms medically?

Monitoring the symptoms medically (regular cognitive assessment, monitoring, imaging and biomarker follow-up, referral if progression occurs) is necessary but not sufficient. The cognitive distortions layered on top of the early Lewy body pathology — the The-It-Is-Just-Normal-Aging, the The-It-Is-Not-Bad-Enough-For-It-To-Be-Lewy-Body-Dementia, the The-It-Is-Just-Mild-So-Nothing-Needs-To-Be-Done, the The-The-Fluctuating-Means-It-Is-Not-Real, the The-The-Hallucinations-Mean-It-Is-Just-Psychosis, and the The-It-Is-Just-Dementia-All-The-Same — are thought patterns that medical monitoring alone does not address, and they drive disengagement from the very monitoring and early intervention that helps. The The-It-Is-Just-Normal-Aging distortion drives missed evaluation. The The-It-Is-Just-Mild-So-Nothing-Needs-To-Be-Done distortion drives missed early intervention. The The-The-Fluctuating-Means-It-Is-Not-Real distortion drives missed evaluation and disengagement from monitoring. The The-The-Hallucinations-Mean-It-Is-Just-Psychosis distortion drives wrong-specialist-referral and dangerous medication choices. The The-It-Is-Just-Dementia-All-The-Same distortion drives generic care that misses the stage-specific early intervention that mild NCD due to Lewy body disease requires. CBT addresses these distortions by examining evidence, building coping skills, and supporting engagement with the evaluation, monitoring, and early intervention that the mild stage enables. The integrated approach produces better outcomes than medical monitoring alone.
