1. CBT Thought Record Worksheet Print: A4/Letter landscape

The 7-column thought record is the single most used CBT exercise. It teaches you to identify automatic thoughts, examine the evidence, and develop balanced alternatives. This printable version has wide columns for handwriting.

Layout (7 columns):

SituationEmotionsAutomatic ThoughtEvidence ForEvidence AgainstBalanced ThoughtOutcome
What happened?Feeling + intensity 0-100What went through your mind?Facts supporting the thoughtFacts against the thoughtAlternative interpretationRe-rate emotion 0-100

Print one sheet per situation. Aim for 3-5 thought records per week.

How to print:

  1. Open the interactive Thought Record Template
  2. Press Ctrl+P (Windows/Linux) or Cmd+P (Mac)
  3. Select Landscape orientation for best column width
  4. Set margins to Minimum for maximum writing space
  5. Print and fill in by hand

3. Cognitive Distortions Reference Sheet Print: A4/Letter portrait

A one-page reference of all 10 common cognitive distortions with examples and reframes. Print this and keep it beside your thought records so you can quickly identify which distortion you are using.

The 10 distortions:

  • All-or-Nothing — "If I'm not perfect, I'm a failure"
  • Overgeneralization — "I always mess up"
  • Mental Filter — focusing only on the negative
  • Disqualifying Positive — "That doesn't count"
  • Mind Reading — "They think I'm incompetent"
  • Fortune Telling — "It's going to go wrong"
  • Catastrophizing — "If I fail, my life is over"
  • Emotional Reasoning — "I feel guilty, so I must be guilty"
  • Should Statements — "I should never make mistakes"
  • Personalization — "It's my fault they're upset"

How to print:

  1. Open the Cognitive Distortion Checker
  2. Press Ctrl+P or Cmd+P
  3. Print as a reference card — keep it visible while doing thought records

4. Core Belief Worksheet Print: A4/Letter portrait

Core beliefs are the deep assumptions that drive automatic thoughts. This printable worksheet walks you through the downward arrow technique to surface your core beliefs, then helps you evaluate and modify them.

Layout (3 sections):

  1. Downward Arrow — "If that thought were true, what would it mean about me?" (repeat 4-5 times to reach core belief)
  2. Evidence Review — list evidence for and against the core belief
  3. New Core Belief — craft a balanced, realistic alternative belief

How to print:

  1. Open the Core Belief Detector
  2. Press Ctrl+P or Cmd+P
  3. Print portrait, one sheet per core belief you are examining

Print Tips for Best Results

  1. Use landscape for wide worksheets (thought record) and portrait for tall ones (mood diary)
  2. Set margins to minimum in your browser print dialog to maximize writing space
  3. Disable headers and footers in print options (browser adds URL/date by default)
  4. Use a pen, not pencil — CBT thought records benefit from commitment. Cross out and rewrite rather than erase.
  5. Print multiple copies — thought records work best with daily practice. Print 7-14 copies at once.
  6. Use a clipboard or folder if you fill them out away from a desk
  7. Review weekly — look back at your completed worksheets to spot patterns
