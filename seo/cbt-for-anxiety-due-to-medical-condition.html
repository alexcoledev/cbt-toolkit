What is Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition?

Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition (DSM-5 293.84) is an Anxiety Disorder where the anxiety is the direct physiological consequence of a medical condition. The DSM-5 criteria require: (a) prominent anxiety or panic attacks; (b) there is evidence from the history, physical examination, or laboratory findings that the disturbance is the direct pathophysiological consequence of a medical condition; (c) the disturbance is not better explained by another mental disorder (e.g., Panic Disorder, GAD, where the anxiety is not attributable to a medical condition); (d) the disturbance does not occur exclusively during delirium; (e) the disturbance causes clinically significant distress or impairment in social, occupational, or other important areas of functioning. The critical feature is the causal medical link: the anxiety is not coincidental with a medical condition but is PRODUCED BY the medical condition through a known physiological mechanism. For example, in hyperthyroidism, elevated thyroid hormones (T3/T4) directly increase sympathetic nervous system activity and adrenergic receptor sensitivity, producing palpitations, tremor, restlessness, and panic-like episodes that are the physiological consequence of the hormone excess — not a coincidental anxiety disorder. In pheochromocytoma, the adrenal tumor episodically releases catecholamines (epinephrine, norepinephrine), producing sudden panic-like attacks with hypertension, palpitations, and sweating that are the direct consequence of the catecholamine surge. In COPD, the dyspnea and hypoxia produce air hunger and sympathetic activation that manifest as anxiety and panic — the anxiety is the physiological consequence of the respiratory compromise. The distinction from a primary anxiety disorder is essential because the treatment sequence differs: the medical condition must be treated FIRST, and the anxiety often resolves or substantially improves as the medical condition is managed. CBT is an adjunct that addresses residual symptoms, adherence, and relapse prevention — not a replacement for medical treatment.

The 3-Component CBT Protocol for Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition

CBT for Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition (DSM-5 293.84) combines three evidence-based components. Each component targets a different maintaining factor — the psychiatric-dismissal and condition-fatalism distortions that block medical workup and treatment engagement, the monocausal-reductionism and treatment-hopelessness distortions that dismiss CBT and block psychological treatment, the dichotomous-attribution and brokenness-fusion distortions that block integrated care and recovery identity. CBT with medical-psychological integration is an evidence-based adjunctive treatment that addresses residual anxiety, adherence, and relapse prevention alongside medical management of the underlying condition.

1. Psychoeducation, Medical-Anxiety Link Education, Medical Condition Management & Anxiety Monitoring

The foundation of CBT for Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition is understanding the medical-anxiety link, identifying the cognitive distortions that maintain the anxiety and interfere with medical adherence, ensuring the underlying medical condition is being treated, and learning anxiety monitoring skills. Psychoeducation: learn the DSM-5 criteria (prominent anxiety/'panic attacks, direct pathophysiological consequence of a medical condition, not better explained by another mental disorder, not during delirium, clinically significant impairment), the common medical conditions that produce anxiety (endocrine: hyperthyroidism, pheochromocytoma, hypoglycemia; cardiovascular: arrhythmias, CHF, MVP; respiratory: COPD, asthma, hyperventilation; neurological: encephalitis, TLE, TBI; metabolic: anemia, B12 deficiency), the specifiers (with panic attacks, with generalized anxiety, with obsessive-compulsive symptoms), the treatment sequence (medical condition treated FIRST, CBT as adjunct), and the good prognosis with integrated care. Medical-anxiety link education is the cornerstone: many people with medically-caused anxiety have never understood that their anxiety is the DIRECT physiological consequence of a medical condition — the It-Is-Just-The-Anxiety distortion frames the anxiety as purely psychiatric, the My-Symptoms-Are-All-Medical-Or-All-Psychological distortion splits symptoms dichotomously, and the Treating-The-Medical-Condition-Is-Enough distortion dismisses the psychological treatment that addresses residual symptoms. CBT helps the person understand the specific mechanism by which their medical condition produces anxiety (e.g., hyperthyroidism increases adrenergic receptor sensitivity producing sympathetic activation; pheochromocytoma releases catecholamines producing panic-like surges; COPD produces hypoxia and dyspnea producing air hunger and sympathetic activation), the medical-psychological collaboration (treating both the condition AND the residual anxiety), and the role of CBT (cognitive restructuring + behavioral regulation + medical adherence + relapse prevention). Medical condition management: ensure the underlying medical condition is being treated by the appropriate specialist (endocrinologist for hyperthyroidism, cardiologist for arrhythmia, pulmonologist for COPD, neurologist for neurological conditions), the medical treatment plan is being followed (medication adherence, lab monitoring, follow-up appointments), and the medical team is aware of the anxiety symptoms. Anxiety monitoring: daily anxiety log tracking anxiety intensity (0-10 SUDS), timing relative to medical symptoms and medication schedule, triggers (medical flares, medication timing, stress), and the cognitive distortions active in each episode.

2. Cognitive Restructuring of Psychiatric-Dismissal, Condition-Fatalism, Monocausal-Reductionism, Treatment-Hopelessness, Dichotomous-Attribution & Brokenness-Fusion Distortions + Medical-Psychological Integration

Identify and challenge the six cognitive distortions that maintain the condition. Keep an anxiety-medical belief log: each day, write down the distressing thoughts (it is just the anxiety, my anxiety is permanent because my condition is, treating the medical condition is enough, the anxiety will never improve, my symptoms are all medical or all psychological, I am broken at the root), identify the distortion, examine the evidence (the anxiety is the direct pathophysiological consequence of a medical condition requiring medical workup — the symptoms have a physiological mechanism, they correlate with medical status, and they require medical treatment; CBT reduces residual anxiety even when the medical condition is chronic — the nervous system sensitization and learned anxiety patterns respond to CBT even when the medical condition persists; residual anxiety and adherence-interfering distortions require psychological treatment — the anxiety that persists after medical treatment, the distortions that interfere with medication adherence, and the relapse anxiety when the condition flares all require CBT; CBT produces meaningful improvement in medically-caused anxiety — cognitive restructuring reduces symptom amplification, behavioral regulation reduces sympathetic activation, and medical adherence improves outcomes; symptoms are produced by both medical and psychological factors requiring integrated care — the medical condition produces the physiological anxiety, the psychological interpretation amplifies it, and both must be addressed; the condition is a medical event and the person is a person with a medical condition, not a broken person — the condition is treatable, the person is not defective, and the identity is separate from the diagnosis), and generate a reframe. Use Socratic questioning: If it is just the anxiety, why do the symptoms correlate with medical status, why does treating the medical condition reduce them, and why is there a known physiological mechanism? If my anxiety is permanent because my condition is, why does CBT reduce residual anxiety in chronic conditions, why do people with managed hyperthyroidism report reduced anxiety, and why does the nervous system desensitize with treatment? If treating the medical condition is enough, why does residual anxiety persist after medical treatment, why do adherence-interfering distortions worsen outcomes, and why does integrated care outperform medical-only care? Use medical-psychological integration: coordinate with the medical team (the CBT clinician and the medical specialist collaborate), align the CBT schedule with the medical treatment schedule (CBT intensifies during medical flares, maintains during stable periods), and address both the medical condition AND the psychological factors in each session. Use behavioral experiments: test the It-Is-Just-The-Anxiety distortion by tracking anxiety symptoms before and after a medical intervention (e.g., anxiety before thyroid medication adjustment vs. after — if anxiety drops with the medical change, the medical etiology is confirmed); test the Treating-The-Medical-Condition-Is-Enough distortion by measuring residual anxiety after the medical condition is well-managed (if residual anxiety persists, the psychological treatment is necessary).

3. Behavioral Regulation, Medical Adherence, Anxiety Management Skills & Relapse Prevention

Behavioral regulation: establish regular sleep (sleep deprivation amplifies anxiety and worsens medical conditions), regular meals (hypoglycemia triggers anxiety and medical flares), regular activity (moderate exercise reduces sympathetic activation and improves medical conditions), and substance reduction (caffeine, alcohol, nicotine all interact with anxiety and medical conditions). Medical adherence: medication adherence tracking (pill diary, medication reminder system, adherence barriers identified and addressed), lab monitoring and follow-up attendance (the distortions that interfere with follow-up — the I-Am-Broken-At-The-Root that produces avoidance of medical settings, the It-Is-Just-The-Anxiety that dismisses the medical workup — are restructured), and medical communication skills (tracking symptoms to report to the medical team, preparing questions for appointments, advocating for integrated care). Anxiety management skills: breathing retraining (slow diaphragmatic breathing reduces sympathetic activation — particularly helpful for respiratory conditions where hyperventilation amplifies anxiety), progressive muscle relaxation (reduces the muscle tension that both medical conditions and anxiety produce), mindfulness (non-judgmental observation of symptoms reduces the catastrophizing that amplifies them), and grounding (present-moment focus reduces the anticipatory anxiety about medical flares). Relapse prevention plan: a written plan that includes the early warning signs of anxiety returning (increased anxiety intensity, increased medical symptom-anxiety coupling, increased avoidance of medical settings, declining medication adherence, increased catastrophizing about symptoms), the action steps for each warning sign (return to CBT skills, contact the medical team about the medical flare, increase anxiety monitoring, restart the belief log, contact the CBT clinician), the medical flare plan (what to do when the medical condition flares — contact the medical specialist, follow the medical flare protocol, intensify CBT skills, track the anxiety-medical coupling), and the ongoing maintenance schedule (periodic CBT practice, regular medical follow-up, integrated care coordination). Generalization: the anxiety reduction and adherence skills transfer to new medical situations (new specialists, hospital settings, medical procedures) through the same cognitive restructuring and behavioral regulation principles.

The 6 Cognitive Distortions in Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition

Each distortion maintains the condition by blocking medical workup, dismissing CBT, blocking integrated care, or preventing recovery identity. Each has a reframe backed by evidence.

1. It-Is-Just-The-Anxiety (psychiatric-dismissal / medical-etiology-dismissal): "It is just anxiety. It is all in my head. I just need to relax. The anxiety is not physical, it is psychological. I do not need a medical workup." The person dismisses the medical etiology, framing the anxiety as purely psychiatric and blocking the medical investigation that would identify the underlying condition.
Reframe: "The anxiety is the direct pathophysiological consequence of a medical condition requiring medical workup. The It-Is-Just-The-Anxiety distortion is a psychiatric-dismissal: the person has dismissed the medical etiology, treating the anxiety as purely psychological when it is the direct physiological consequence of a medical condition. But the anxiety symptoms in this disorder have a known physiological mechanism — hyperthyroidism produces anxiety through elevated thyroid hormones increasing adrenergic receptor sensitivity, pheochromocytoma produces panic-like attacks through episodic catecholamine release, COPD produces anxiety through hypoxia and dyspnea, arrhythmia produces anxiety through cardiac sympathetic activation. The symptoms correlate with medical status (anxiety worsens when the medical condition flares, improves when the medical condition is treated), they have a physiological mechanism (not just psychological), and they require medical treatment (treating the medical condition reduces the anxiety). The distortion is dangerous because it blocks the medical workup that would identify the underlying condition — the person attributes the symptoms to stress or anxiety and never gets the thyroid panel, the catecholamine testing, the cardiac monitoring, or the pulmonary function testing that would reveal the cause. The antidote is medical-anxiety link education: understand the specific mechanism by which medical conditions produce anxiety, recognize that the symptom pattern (correlation with medical status, physiological mechanism, response to medical treatment) indicates medical etiology, and pursue the medical workup that the symptoms warrant. The key question: if it is just the anxiety, why do the symptoms correlate with medical status, why is there a known physiological mechanism, and why does treating the medical condition reduce them?"
2. My-Anxiety-Is-Permanent-Because-My-Condition-Is (condition-fatalism / chronicity-transfer): "My medical condition is chronic, so my anxiety is chronic. Nothing will help the anxiety because the condition is permanent. I am stuck with this anxiety forever because the condition is forever." The person fatalizes the anxiety as permanent by transferring the chronicity of the medical condition to the anxiety, blocking CBT engagement.
Reframe: "CBT reduces residual anxiety even when the medical condition is chronic. The My-Anxiety-Is-Permanent-Because-My-Condition-Is distortion is a condition-fatalism: the person has transferred the chronicity of the medical condition to the anxiety, treating the anxiety as equally permanent. But even when the medical condition is chronic (COPD is chronic, hyperthyroidism may require lifelong management, arrhythmia may recur), the anxiety is NOT equally permanent — the anxiety has two components: (1) the direct physiological anxiety from the medical condition (which fluctuates with medical status and is managed by medical treatment), and (2) the residual anxiety from nervous system sensitization and learned patterns (which is reduced by CBT). The residual anxiety responds to CBT even when the medical condition persists — cognitive restructuring reduces the catastrophizing that amplifies symptoms, behavioral regulation reduces sympathetic activation, and exposure to medical settings reduces the avoidance anxiety. People with chronic medical conditions who receive CBT report lower anxiety than those who receive medical treatment alone, even with the same medical status. The distortion is harmful because it blocks CBT engagement — if the anxiety is permanent, treatment is futile. But the evidence shows CBT produces meaningful anxiety reduction in chronic medical conditions. The antidote is recognizing that the anxiety and the medical condition are not equally chronic — the medical condition may persist, but the anxiety component is treatable. The key question: if my anxiety is permanent because my condition is, why does CBT reduce residual anxiety in chronic conditions, why do people with managed conditions report lower anxiety, and why does the nervous system desensitize with treatment?"
3. Treating-The-Medical-Condition-Is-Enough (monocausal-reductionism / psychological-treatment-dismissal): "I just need the right medication for my medical condition. Therapy is not for me. The medical fix will solve everything. Once the medical condition is treated, the anxiety will go away on its own." The person dismisses CBT as unnecessary, treating the medical treatment as sufficient and blocking the psychological treatment that addresses residual anxiety and adherence.
Reframe: "Residual anxiety and adherence-interfering distortions require psychological treatment. The Treating-The-Medical-Condition-Is-Enough distortion is a monocausal-reductionism: the person has reduced the anxiety to a single cause (the medical condition) and dismissed the psychological treatment, treating the medical fix as sufficient. But three problems require CBT even after the medical condition is treated: (1) residual anxiety symptoms persist — the nervous system has been sensitized, the anxiety has become a learned response, and the symptom interpretation patterns amplify residual symptoms; (2) adherence-interfering distortions — the It-Is-Just-The-Anxiety blocks medical workup, the I-Am-Broken-At-The-Root produces avoidance of medical settings, and these distortions interfere with the ongoing medical management; (3) relapse anxiety — when the medical condition flares, the anxiety flares with it, and without CBT skills the person catastrophizes the flare, the anxiety amplifies, and the recovery is slower. Integrated care (medical treatment + CBT) outperforms medical-only care for anxiety in medical conditions — the CBT addresses the psychological factors that medical treatment cannot reach. The distortion is harmful because it blocks the psychological treatment that would address residual symptoms, improve adherence, and prevent relapse. The antidote is recognizing that the medical treatment is necessary but not sufficient — the anxiety has both medical and psychological components, and both require treatment. The key question: if treating the medical condition is enough, why does residual anxiety persist after medical treatment, why do adherence-interfering distortions worsen outcomes, and why does integrated care outperform medical-only care?"
4. The-Anxiety-Will-Never-Improve (treatment-hopelessness / treatment-resistance-fatalism): "Nothing works for my anxiety. I have tried everything. My case is different. The anxiety will never get better because it is caused by a medical condition." The person fatalizes the anxiety as treatment-resistant, using the medical etiology as evidence that psychological treatment cannot help.
Reframe: "CBT produces meaningful improvement in medically-caused anxiety. The The-Anxiety-Will-Never-Improve distortion is a treatment-hopelessness: the person has fatalized the anxiety as treatment-resistant, using the medical etiology as evidence that CBT cannot help. But CBT produces meaningful improvement in anxiety due to medical conditions through four mechanisms: (1) cognitive restructuring reduces the symptom amplification that the catastrophizing produces — even when the medical condition causes the physiological anxiety, the person's interpretation of the symptoms (the The-Anxiety-Will-Never-Improve distortion itself) amplifies and prolongs them, and restructuring reduces the amplification; (2) behavioral regulation reduces the sympathetic activation that both the medical condition and the anxiety produce — breathing retraining, relaxation, and activity regulation reduce the sympathetic tone that amplifies anxiety; (3) medical adherence improves — the distortions that interfere with adherence are restructured, and better adherence improves the medical condition which reduces the anxiety; (4) relapse prevention skills reduce the impact of medical flares — early warning signs, coping plans, and medical contact plans reduce the anxiety duration and intensity of each flare. The distortion is a self-fulfilling prophecy — if the anxiety will never improve, the person does not engage with CBT, and the anxiety does not improve. But the evidence shows CBT produces meaningful anxiety reduction in medical conditions. The antidote is recognizing that the medical etiology does not make the anxiety treatment-resistant — the psychological components of the anxiety respond to psychological treatment. The key question: if the anxiety will never improve, why does CBT reduce symptom amplification, why does behavioral regulation reduce sympathetic activation, and why do integrated care outcomes exceed medical-only outcomes?"
5. My-Symptoms-Are-All-Medical-Or-All-Psychological (dichotomous-attribution / medical-psychological-split): "My symptoms are either physical or mental, not both. Either it is the medical condition or it is the anxiety. I need to figure out which is which and treat them separately." The person splits symptoms dichotomously into all-medical or all-psychological, blocking the integrated care that addresses both.
Reframe: "Symptoms are produced by both medical and psychological factors requiring integrated care. The My-Symptoms-Are-All-Medical-Or-All-Psychological distortion is a dichotomous-attribution: the person has split symptoms into mutually exclusive categories (medical OR psychological), blocking the recognition that symptoms are produced by BOTH. But the anxiety in Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition is inherently both — the medical condition produces the physiological anxiety (palpitations, tremor, sympathetic activation), AND the psychological interpretation amplifies it (catastrophizing about the symptoms increases their severity and duration). The palpitation is medical (the arrhythmia produces it) AND psychological (the interpretation "this palpitation means I am dying" amplifies the anxiety about it). The dyspnea is medical (the COPD produces it) AND psychological (the interpretation "I cannot breathe" amplifies the panic about it). The dichotomous split is harmful because it blocks integrated care — if symptoms are all-medical, only the medical team treats them; if all-psychological, only the CBT clinician treats them. But the symptoms require BOTH — the medical team treats the medical condition that produces the physiological anxiety, and the CBT clinician treats the psychological interpretation that amplifies it. Integrated care (medical + CBT) outperforms either alone. The antidote is recognizing that symptoms are produced by both factors simultaneously — the medical condition produces the physiological substrate, the psychological interpretation amplifies it, and both must be addressed in an integrated plan. The key question: if symptoms are all medical or all psychological, why does the psychological interpretation amplify medical symptoms, why does treating the medical condition not fully resolve the anxiety, and why does integrated care outperform either alone?"
6. I-Am-Broken-At-The-Root (brokenness-fusion / condition-as-identity): "I am defective. My body is fundamentally wrong. I am broken at the root. The medical condition means I am a broken person, not a person with a medical condition." The person fuses the medical condition with personal brokenness, treating a medical event as evidence of fundamental defectiveness.
Reframe: "The condition is a medical event and the person is a person with a medical condition, not a broken person. The I-Am-Broken-At-The-Root distortion is a brokenness-fusion: the person has fused the medical condition with their identity, treating the condition as evidence of fundamental brokenness rather than a medical event that happened to a person. But a medical condition is a medical event — hyperthyroidism is a thyroid disorder, not a character defect; arrhythmia is a cardiac rhythm disorder, not a personal failing; COPD is a respiratory condition, not a brokenness. The person is a person who has a medical condition, not a broken person. The condition is treatable (medical treatment manages the condition, CBT manages the anxiety), the person is not defective (having a medical condition is a human experience, not a defect), and the identity is separate from the diagnosis (the person is a person, the condition is a condition, and the two are distinct). The distortion is harmful because it blocks recovery — if the person is broken at the root, treatment is futile (you cannot fix brokenness), medical settings are avoided (they confirm the brokenness), and the identity collapses into the diagnosis (I am my condition). But the evidence shows that people with medical conditions recover, manage, and live full lives — the condition is a medical event, not an identity. The antidote is recognizing that the condition is a medical event that happened to a person, that the person is not the condition, and that treatment addresses the condition while the person remains a whole person. The key question: if I am broken at the root, why is the condition treatable, why do people with the condition recover and live full lives, and why is the condition a medical event rather than an identity?"

When to seek professional help

CBT tools on this page are educational, not a substitute for professional care. Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition requires coordinated care from both a medical specialist (for the underlying medical condition) and a CBT-trained clinician (for the anxiety), especially when the anxiety is severe or the medical condition is complex. Seek help if: the anxiety symptoms correlate with medical status (worsen when the medical condition flares, improve when it is treated — this is the hallmark of medical etiology); you have a known medical condition that produces anxiety symptoms (hyperthyroidism, pheochromocytoma, COPD, arrhythmia, neurological conditions) and the anxiety is causing significant distress or impairment; you have anxiety symptoms and have NOT had a medical workup (the It-Is-Just-The-Anxiety distortion may be blocking the investigation that would identify an underlying condition — a medical workup including thyroid panel, cardiac monitoring, pulmonary function, and neurological evaluation may be warranted); the anxiety is producing panic attacks, significant generalized anxiety, or obsessive-compulsive symptoms that interfere with daily functioning; the cognitive distortions are interfering with medical adherence (the I-Am-Broken-At-The-Root produces avoidance of medical settings, the It-Is-Just-The-Anxiety dismisses the medical workup, the Treating-The-Medical-Condition-Is-Enough dismisses the CBT); you have been treating the medical condition but residual anxiety persists (the residual anxiety requires CBT — the Treating-The-Medical-Condition-Is-Enough distortion may be blocking the psychological treatment); the anxiety is producing secondary problems (depression, relationship strain, occupational limitation, avoidance of medical settings that worsens the medical condition); the medical condition flares and the anxiety flares with it (relapse prevention skills can reduce the anxiety impact of each flare); you have co-occurring depression or substance use (the anxiety, medical condition, and co-occurring conditions interact — integrated care addresses all); you have never tried CBT and the The-Anxiety-Will-Never-Improve distortion is blocking engagement; the anxiety is severe enough to require medication (a psychiatrist can evaluate anxiety medication that is compatible with the medical condition treatment). A CBT-trained clinician with experience in medical psychology or behavioral medicine is ideal — they understand the medical-psychological integration that this condition requires.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition different from Health Anxiety?

Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition (DSM-5 293.84) and Health Anxiety (illness anxiety) both involve anxiety and medical conditions, but the mechanism differs fundamentally. In Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition, the anxiety is the DIRECT PATHOPHYSIOLOGICAL CONSEQUENCE of a medical condition — the medical condition (e.g., hyperthyroidism) is PRODUCING the anxiety symptoms through a known physiological mechanism (elevated thyroid hormones increasing sympathetic activation). The anxiety is a symptom of the medical condition. In Health Anxiety, the anxiety is ABOUT having or acquiring a medical condition — the person is preoccupied with having an illness, but the anxiety is not caused by a medical condition's physiological effects. A person with hyperthyroidism producing palpitations and panic has Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition (the hyperthyroidism is causing the anxiety). A person without a medical condition who is preoccupied with the fear of having hyperthyroidism has Health Anxiety (the anxiety is about the condition, not caused by it). The treatments differ: Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition requires treating the underlying medical condition first with CBT as adjunct; Health Anxiety is treated primarily with CBT (cognitive restructuring of illness beliefs, behavioral experiments, exposure to health-related triggers) without a medical workup as the primary intervention.

What medical conditions most commonly cause anxiety symptoms?

The medical conditions that most commonly produce anxiety symptoms (Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition, DSM-5 293.84) fall into five categories. Endocrine disorders: hyperthyroidism/thyrotoxicosis (elevated T3/T4 produce sympathetic activation, palpitations, tremor, panic-like episodes — one of the most common medical causes of anxiety), pheochromocytoma (adrenal tumor releasing catecholamines produces episodic panic-like attacks with hypertension), hypoglycemia (low blood sugar produces adrenaline release and panic-like symptoms), hyperparathyroidism, carcinoid syndrome. Cardiovascular disorders: cardiac arrhythmias (atrial fibrillation, SVT produce palpitations and panic-like episodes), congestive heart failure, mitral valve prolapse. Respiratory disorders: COPD (hypoxia and dyspnea produce air hunger and sympathetic activation), asthma, hyperventilation syndrome, pulmonary embolism. Neurological disorders: encephalitis, temporal lobe epilepsy (seizures can produce anxiety auras), brain tumors, traumatic brain injury, multiple sclerosis. Metabolic/toxic conditions: anemia, vitamin B12 deficiency, caffeine intoxication, substance withdrawal (alcohol, benzodiazepine, opioid withdrawal produce prominent anxiety). The key diagnostic feature is that the anxiety symptoms correlate with the medical condition status (worsen when the condition flares, improve when it is treated) and have a known physiological mechanism. A medical workup including thyroid panel (TSH, free T3, free T4), cardiac monitoring (ECG, Holter), pulmonary function testing, and basic metabolic panel is warranted when anxiety symptoms may have a medical etiology.

If the anxiety is caused by a medical condition, do I still need CBT?

Yes, CBT is an evidence-based adjunctive treatment for Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition, even though the medical condition is the primary cause. Three reasons. First, residual anxiety symptoms often PERSIST after the medical condition is treated — the nervous system has been sensitized, the anxiety has become a learned response, and the symptom interpretation patterns (catastrophizing about symptoms) amplify residual symptoms. CBT extinguishes the residual through cognitive restructuring and behavioral regulation. Second, the cognitive distortions (the It-Is-Just-The-Anxiety that blocks medical workup, the I-Am-Broken-At-The-Root that produces avoidance of medical settings, the Treating-The-Medical-Condition-Is-Enough that dismisses CBT) interfere with MEDICAL ADHERENCE — the person does not follow up with the medical team, does not take medication consistently, does not attend monitoring appointments. CBT corrects these distortions, improving adherence and medical outcomes. Third, medical conditions FLUCTUATE (hyperthyroidism relapses, COPD exacerbates, arrhythmia recurs), and the anxiety flares with each medical flare. CBT provides relapse prevention skills (early warning signs, coping plan, medical contact plan) that reduce the anxiety duration and intensity of each flare. Integrated care (medical treatment + CBT) outperforms medical-only care for anxiety in medical conditions. CBT is not a replacement for medical treatment but a critical adjunct that addresses the psychological factors that maintain and amplify the medically-caused anxiety.

Can I have both Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition AND a primary anxiety disorder?

Yes. A person can have both Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition (DSM-5 293.84) AND a primary anxiety disorder (e.g., Panic Disorder 300.01, GAD 300.02). For example, a person with hyperthyroidism may have anxiety symptoms directly caused by the thyroid hormone excess (the 293.84 diagnosis) AND additional excessive worry about multiple topics that is not attributable to the thyroid (the GAD diagnosis). The DSM-5 allows both diagnoses when each set of symptoms meets full criteria and is not better explained by the other. The treatment addresses both: the medical condition is treated (reducing the physiologically-caused anxiety), the primary anxiety disorder is treated with CBT and/or medication (reducing the primary anxiety), and the integrated plan coordinates the medical and psychological treatment. The cognitive distortions may overlap (the catastrophizing amplifies both the medical and primary anxiety) but the etiology differs (the 293.84 anxiety is physiologically caused, the primary anxiety is not). A thorough diagnostic evaluation by a clinician who understands both medical and primary anxiety disorders is essential to distinguish the components and design the integrated treatment.

What if my medical condition is chronic and will not go away?

A chronic medical condition (COPD, managed hyperthyroidism, recurrent arrhythmia) does not mean chronic untreatable anxiety. The anxiety has two components: (1) the direct physiological anxiety from the medical condition, which fluctuates with medical status and is managed by medical treatment, and (2) the residual anxiety from nervous system sensitization and learned patterns, which is reduced by CBT. Even when the medical condition is chronic, the residual anxiety responds to CBT — cognitive restructuring reduces the catastrophizing that amplifies symptoms, behavioral regulation reduces sympathetic activation, medical adherence improves with distortion restructuring, and relapse prevention skills reduce the anxiety impact of medical flares. People with chronic medical conditions who receive CBT report lower anxiety than those who receive medical treatment alone, even with the same medical status. The My-Anxiety-Is-Permanent-Because-My-Condition-Is distortion transfers the chronicity of the medical condition to the anxiety, but the two are not equally chronic — the medical condition may persist, but the anxiety component is treatable. CBT is not a cure for the medical condition, but it is a meaningful treatment for the anxiety that the medical condition produces.
