What is Doomscrolling?

Doomscrolling is the act of spending an excessive amount of time on digital content that elicit negative emotions. The concept was coined around 2018, and became more widespread in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic. The World Health Organization (WHO) observed that the pandemic was accompanied by widespread misleading information, conspiracy theories, and false reports, which it referred to as an "infodemic".

Why Doomscrolling & News Anxiety Needs Specific CBT

General anxiety tools say "manage the worry." General OCD tools address the compulsion. General stress tools address the overload. None of them address the specifics: the i-must-check-every-update produces a specific compulsion-check loop that general OCD tools cannot frame because the compulsion is information-seeking not a ritual, the world-is-ending produces a specific catastrophizing that general anxiety tools cannot address because the catastrophe is headline-anchored not generalized, the if-i-stop-i-am-complicit produces a specific information-guilt that general guilt tools cannot address because the guilt is about staying informed not a moral act, the everything-is-getting-worse produces a specific hopelessness that general depression tools cannot frame because the hopelessness is trend-extrapolated not endogenous, the one-more-refresh produces a specific control illusion that general anxiety tools cannot address because the illusion is about information-as-control not a generalized worry, and the news-defines-reality produces a specific identity fusion that general identity tools cannot frame because the fusion is with the feed not a general identity question. This produces specific, identifiable thought patterns that CBT for doomscrolling can target directly:

The "I Must Check Every Update" Compulsion-Check Loop Distortion

Distortion: "I must check every update. If I do not check I will miss something. If I miss something something bad will happen and I will not know. The refresh is not a choice, it is a need. I cannot stop. I have tried to stop and I cannot. The moment I put the phone down the anxiety spikes, which proves I need to check. If I could just check one more time I would feel better. One more refresh. One more article. One more scroll. I will stop after the next one. I will stop after I am caught up. But I am never caught up. The news never stops so neither can I. Checking is the only thing that brings the anxiety down, even if only for a moment. I am behind on everything. I have to know."
Reframe: "I am examining the evidence and challenging the compulsion. The i-must-check-every-update (I must check) was a learned pattern: I learned to check because the first checks reduced anxiety, the checking was reinforced by the relief, and the relief trained my brain to crave the check. The compulsion (I cannot stop) is the distortion: the urge to check is real but the check is not required, and the anxiety spike when I stop is withdrawal from the relief-reinforcement cycle, not evidence of need. The intolerance-of-uncertainty (if I do not check I will miss something) is the trap: I will miss something, but most of what I miss is not actionable and not relevant to my life, and the cost of catching everything is the anxiety I am trying to reduce. CBT helps by examining the evidence (does checking actually give me control or just more information, what percentage of what I check is actionable), challenging the compulsion (the urge is real but the check is a choice), and building a calibrated relationship with information that holds uncertainty without the compulsive check. The news anxiety is real; the conclusion that I must check is the distortion."

The "World Is Ending" News Catastrophizing Distortion

Distortion: "The world is ending. Every headline confirms it. The fires, the floods, the wars, the collapse — this is it. This is the apocalypse. It is only going to get worse. There is no future. Everything is falling apart. I can feel it in my chest when I read the headlines. The dread is the truth. The people who are not worried are the ones who are wrong. Every news cycle is worse than the last. The trend is clear and the trend is down. There is no point planning for a future that will not exist. The headlines are the reality and the reality is doom. I have to keep reading because I have to know how it ends, even though I already know how it ends."
Reframe: "I am examining the evidence and challenging the catastrophizing. The world-is-ending (this is the apocalypse) was a learned pattern: I learned to escalate because the worst-case felt like the safest preparation, the catastrophizing was a way to brace against hope, and the doom felt like the most honest assessment. The catastrophizing (the world is ending) is the distortion: the events are real but the end-of-the-world interpretation is not supported by the evidence, and the dread in my chest is a stress response to the framing, not evidence of the conclusion. The fortune-telling (it will only get worse) is the trap: the trend is real but the extrapolation to permanent doom is not supported, and news selection amplifies the negative because catastrophe drives engagement. CBT helps by examining the evidence (is this headline an apocalypse or a reported event, what is the actual probability this affects me, what is the full picture beyond the selected sample), challenging the catastrophizing (the event is real but the end-of-the-world interpretation is the distortion), and building a calibrated assessment that holds the reality without the apocalypse. The news anxiety is real; the conclusion that the world is ending is the distortion."

The "Everything Is Getting Worse" Hopelessness Distortion

Distortion: "Everything is getting worse. Every metric is down. Every trend is negative. The world I am leaving for my children is worse than the one I inherited. Nothing is improving. The good news is a lie, the bad news is the truth. The people who see progress are delusional. The data is clear and the data is doom. I have given up. There is no point. Nothing I do matters because the trajectory is fixed. Hope is naive. Hope is denial. The only honest position is despair. The news proves it, day after day, headline after headline. It is not going to get better. It has never gotten better. The brief periods of calm were the exception, the crisis is the rule."
Reframe: "I am examining the evidence and challenging the hopelessness. The everything-is-getting-worse (the trajectory is fixed and downward) was a learned pattern: I learned to extrapolate because the negative trends were salient and the positive trends were invisible, the hopelessness felt like the most honest assessment, and the despair was a way to stop hoping and stop being disappointed. The hopelessness (nothing will improve) is the distortion: some metrics are down and some are up, and the long-term data on human welfare (health, poverty, violence, life expectancy) shows real progress alongside real problems. The overgeneralization (every trend is negative) is the trap: I am weighting the visible negative trends and discounting the invisible positive ones, and the news selection bias amplifies the negative because crisis drives engagement. CBT helps by examining the evidence (what does the long-term data actually show, am I weighting negative and positive trends equally, what is the full picture beyond the feed), challenging the hopelessness (the problems are real but the permanent-doom conclusion is the distortion), and building a calibrated assessment that holds the problems without the despair. The news anxiety is real; the conclusion that everything is getting worse is the distortion."

When to See a Professional

These tools are self-help based on CBT for cognitive distortion principles, useful for mild to moderate distress from news consumption, not a substitute for professional support. The cognitive distortions that doomscrolling produces can lead to depression, anxiety, sleep disruption, relationship strain, and in some cases the news anxiety is the surface of deeper patterns, generalized anxiety disorder, or co-occurring conditions. These tools are a self-help starting point, not a replacement for professional support, especially if the distress is affecting your physical health, if it is accompanied by thoughts of self-harm, if it is complicated by depression that meets clinical criteria, or if it is layered on a history of trauma, adverse experiences, or an anxiety disorder. If you are experiencing severe distress, thoughts of self-harm, or any condition affecting your safety, please contact a licensed mental health professional or a crisis line immediately. In the US, call or text 988 for the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline. In the UK and Ireland, Samaritans (samaritans.org, 116 123) offers 24/7 support. NAMI (nami.org) offers mental health support and education. ADAA (adaa.org, the Anxiety and Depression Association of America) offers anxiety-specific resources and support. Seeking help is a sign of good judgment, not a failure.

I feel guilty when I stop checking the news. Can CBT help with the information-guilt?

Yes, and the if-i-stop-i-am-complicit information-guilt is one of the most insidious patterns because it moralizes not-checking, converting a boundary into a betrayal. CBT helps by examining the evidence (is staying informed a moral obligation or a choice, does knowing about every catastrophe actually help anyone) and challenging the assumption that not-checking is complicity. How is doomscrolling different from general anxiety?

Doomscrolling is compulsive news consumption driven by intolerance-of-uncertainty and reinforced by the relief of checking. General anxiety is not check-anchored or consumption-driven. The distinction matters: general anxiety tools address the worry without the consumption trigger; doomscrolling tools address the specific cognitive distortions that compulsive news consumption installs (the compulsion-check loop, the news catastrophizing, the information-guilt, the hopelessness, the control illusion, the identity fusion). General anxiety says "manage the worry"; doomscrolling CBT says "the worry is about the feed, the compulsion is to check, and the reframes must engage the information-seeking behavior." Someone can experience both — the generalized anxiety plus the doomscrolling — but the doomscrolling-specific distortions require doomscrolling-specific CBT.

How is doomscrolling different from climate anxiety or social media anxiety?

Doomscrolling is compulsive news consumption across all topics driven by the need to stay informed. Climate anxiety is topic-specific to the climate crisis. Social media anxiety is platform-specific to social comparison and validation. The distinction matters: climate anxiety tools address the existential threat of one domain; social media anxiety tools address the comparison and validation loops of one platform type; doomscrolling tools address the compulsion-check loop and catastrophizing that cuts across all news topics and all platforms. Someone can experience all three — but the doomscrolling distortions are about the consumption behavior itself, not the specific topic or platform.
