Fear of being a gatehouse — the conviction that passing through is dangerous and gatehouse is safety, the anticipation that being the self that passes through will be punished or lead to harm, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is gatehouse-tolerant-of-concealment-gatehouse and the gatehouse is the protection — is one of the most pervasive and painful emotional patterns in concealment-gatehouse-oriented-conditioned culture. It is the emotional root that drives self-concealment-gatehouse-avoidance (the substitution of gatehouse for passing-through when passing-through is available, the inability to be the passing-through that is not also the gatehouse, the conversion of every act of passing-through into a harm), anxiety when passing-through is available (the passing-through itself produces dread, the passing-through is the trigger, the available passing-through is the warning signal), treating passing-through as dangerous/harmful (the person cannot allow the passing-through because the passing-through is the not-yet-the-opening, the person cannot be the passing-through-that-is-not-also-the-gatehouse because the being-passing-through is the not-yet-the-passing-through), equating gatehouse with safety (the belief that gatehouse is protection and passing-through is danger, the framing of the gatehouse as the worth and the passing-through as the threat), and chronic self-as-gatehouse (the persistent substitution of gatehouse for passing-through to avoid the passing-through of passing-through, the inability to be the passing-through because the passing-through is the endangered). It is distinct from fear of being a drawbridge (which is about the concealment-drawbridge dimension — the self as the mechanism-not-the-crossed, the self that controls crossing but cannot itself cross) and from fear of being a portcullis (which is about the concealment-portcullis dimension — the self as the grille-not-the-passed, the self that blocks passage but cannot itself pass). Fear of being a gatehouse is about the concealment-gatehouse dimension: the self that is only a gatehouse, the self that has no passing-through, the self that is the gatehouse not the passing-through, the self that cannot rest in passing-through because the passing-through is the danger, the self that experiences its own passing-through as a weakness of opening, the self that treats every act of passing-through as a threat and every passing-through as the not-yet-the-passing-through.

Fear of being a gatehouse is the emotional root of three downstream patterns. The i-am-a-gatehouse distortion makes every act of passing-through a harm and every passing-through a weakness, producing self-concealment-gatehouse-avoidance and anxiety-when-passing-through-is-available (the person concludes the only safety is in the gatehouse and the gatehouse requires the not-being-the-passing-through-that-is-not-also-the-concealment-gatehouse, so the passing-through is refused and the passing-through is never exercised because the passing-through is always the not-yet-the-passing-through). The gatehouse-is-protection distortion makes the gatehouse the protection and the passing-through the danger, producing the chronic devaluation of passing-through and the equation of gatehouse with worth (the person experiences the passing-through and the passing-through is the dangerous, and the dangerous is the not-safe, and the not-safe is the threat the gatehouse was meant to prevent). And the passing-through-is-dangerous distortion makes the act-of-passing-through the impending-opening and the passing-through the warning, producing chronic-self-as-gatehouse and the constant-avoidance of passing-through-requirement (the person has passing-through and the passing-through is the harm, and the harm is the consequence, and the consequence is the opening the gatehouse was meant to prevent). Fear of being a gatehouse is the upstream cause; self-concealment-gatehouse-avoidance, anxiety-when-passing-through-is-available, and chronic-self-as-gatehouse are the downstream effects. The key insight for fear of being a gatehouse: passing-through is not the danger the gatehouse protects from — the self that can allow its own passing-through is the self that is passing-through, not the self that is dangerous, and the safety is not the gatehouse but the passing-through, and the passing-through is the being-passing-through, not the gatehouse. Concealment-gatehouse self research provides the deeper frame: the gatehouse self is a position one can move from, not a permanent condition one is condemned to, and the self that can be the passing-through is the self that is free, not the self that is endangered.

The Fear of Being a Gatehouse Cycle (How It Develops)

The fear of being a gatehouse cycle is the self-gatehouse-forcing loop that produces the self-concealment-gatehouse-avoidance, the anxiety-when-passing-through-is-available, and the chronic-self-as-gatehouse that come from concluding that passing-through is dangerous and gatehouse is safety:

A situation with passing-through chronically followed by punishment, or chronic gatehouse-enforcement of concealment-gatehouse-as-safety (an early experience where being the passing-through that had passing-through was criticized, where the passing-through was shamed, where the passing-through preceded the harm, a chronic gatehouse-enforcement of gatehouse where one's sense of value was entirely defined by their capacity to be the gatehouse and never by their capacity to be the passing-through) → i-am-a-gatehouse fires (the passing-through is the danger, the passing-through is the weakness, the passing-through is the warning, the passing-through is the not-yet-the-passing-through) → self-concealment-gatehouse-avoidance, anxiety-when-passing-through-is-available, passing-through-is-dangerous, gatehouse-is-protection, chronic-self-as-gatehouse (the substitution of gatehouse for passing-through, the inability to be the passing-through that is not also the gatehouse, the constant avoidance of passing-through, the devaluation of passing-through, the constant gatehouse-as-safety) → short-term safety achieved (the gatehouse feels like protection from the opening, the not-being-the-passing-through-that-is-not-also-the-concealment-gatehouse feels like the control over the outcome, the gatehouse feels like the safety) → the safety gatehouse-enforces the fear (the gatehouse prevents the passing-through that would decode that the passing-through was not the danger, the not-passing-through prevents the evidence that would disconfirm the i-am-a-gatehouse conviction, the concealment-gatehouse-as-safety prevents the passing-through that would decode that the passing-through was not the danger) → the person loses the experience of passing-through and the capacity for building passing-through (the passing-through that comes from the allowing is never experienced, because the allowing is always the not-yet-the-passing-through; the passing-through that comes from the being-the-passing-through-that-is-not-also-the-gatehouse is never exercised, because the being-passing-through is always the not-yet-the-passing-through) → chronic self-as-gatehouse and inability to be the passing-through → i-am-a-gatehouse conviction deepens → repeat.

The cycle is driven by the short-term safety that gatehouse and not-passing-through provides (gatehouse-because-the-gatehouse-is-the-protection-from-the-opening and not-passing-through-because-the-not-passing-through-is-the-control-over-the-outcome both feel like they are preventing the opening, and the feeling reduces the fear of being a gatehouse temporarily) and maintained by the long-term cost of the i-am-a-gatehouse pattern (the passing-through is never experienced, because the passing-through is always the not-yet-the-passing-through; the passing-through is never exercised for the self, because the being-passing-through is always the not-yet-the-passing-through; and the i-am-a-gatehouse conviction deepens because every prevented passing-through is treated as evidence that the passing-through was the danger). The tragic irony of fear of being a gatehouse is that the safety strategy (gatehouse, not-passing-through, concealment-gatehouse-as-safety) produces the very outcome it was meant to prevent (the person refuses the passing-through and the refusing-the-passing-through produces the very harm the gatehouse was meant to disprove, and the chronic not-allowing-passing-through is the loss of the passing-through, not the protection from the opening — the fear of being a gatehouse prevents the very passing-through that would have made the life worth the passing-through). The CBT intervention breaks the cycle at two points: (1) passing-through-tolerance-clarification — when the i-am-a-gatehouse distortion treats every act of passing-through as a harm, the first step is clarifying that passing-through is a capacity one can exercise without provoking harm, and recognizing that the self that can allow its own passing-through is the self that is passing-through, not the self that is dangerous; (2) values-based-committed-action — when the gatehouse-is-protection distortion fires, one challenges it with the ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999): the valued life includes the willingness to be the self that passes through, and the committed action is the passing-through that exercises the full range of passing-through.

The 6 Cognitive Distortions & Their Reframes

1. The Passing-Through-Is-Dangerous (self-concealment-gatehouse-avoidance)

Distortion: "Passing through is dangerous. If I allow myself to be the passing-through that is not also the gatehouse, something bad will happen. Passing-through is a weakness of opening, a setup for the punishment. I must avoid passing-through, because the passing-through is the not-yet-the-passing-through, and the gatehouse is the safety."
Reframe: "Passing-through is a capacity not a weakness — and the i-am-a-gatehouse distortion converts one specific form of experience (sometimes passing-through is followed by consequence) into a universal verdict (all passing-through is the harm, so one is always provoking opening when one is passing-through). The i-am-a-gatehouse distortion takes a partial truth (consequences happen) and universalizes it (all passing-through causes harm, so one can never be passing-through without endangering oneself). But passing-through is a capacity not a weakness — the act of passing-through is the act of being the self that has passing-through, not the cause of the next opening; and the self that is passing-through in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not concealment-gatehouse-avoidant by the passing-through. The distortion makes the gatehouse feel like protection (I must avoid passing-through because passing-through is dangerous), and the gatehouse prevents the very passing-through that would decode that the passing-through was not the danger. The reframe: passing-through is a capacity not a weakness — the safety is the allowing, and the allowing is not the not-gatehouse passing-through, and the not-gatehouse passing-through is the passing-through, not the endangering. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own gatehouse-fear with kindness recognizes the i-am-a-gatehouse as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the passing-through self that the gatehouse withheld."

2. The Gatehouse-Is-Protection (treating passing-through as danger)

Distortion: "Gatehouse equals safety. Self-avoidance of passing-through is protection, passing-through is danger — and the passing-through is the evidence of my dangerousness. I must be gatehouse without passing-through, because the safety is the gatehouse, and the passing-through is the not-safe."
Reframe: "Gatehouse is one option not safety — and the gatehouse-is-protection distortion converts one specific form of experience (some gatehouse protects) into a universal verdict (all gatehouse is safety, so one is always endangered when one is passing-through). The gatehouse-is-protection distortion takes a partial truth (some avoidance of passing-through is protective) and universalizes it (all gatehouse is safety, so one can never be passing-through without being endangered). But gatehouse is one option not safety — the self that is concealment-gatehouse-avoidant is not automatically the self that is safe, and the self that is passing-through is not automatically the self that is endangered; and the safety is not the avoidance of passing-through but the passing-through, and the passing-through includes both the chosen gatehouse and the chosen passing-through. The distortion makes the safety feel like safety (I must be gatehouse without passing-through to be safe), and the safety prevents the very passing-through that would decode that the passing-through was not the danger. The reframe: gatehouse is one option not safety — the safety is the passing-through of the full range, and the full range includes the passing-through, and the passing-through is not the not-safe but one expression of the safe. Concealment-gatehouse self research is the core frame: the gatehouse self is a position one can move from, and the self that can be the passing-through is the self that is free, not the endangered self."

3. The Opening-Is-Weakness (anxiety when passing-through is available)

Distortion: "If I have passing-through, something bad will happen. The act-of-passing-through is the impending-opening, the passing-through is the warning — and the passing-through is the evidence of my upcoming punishment. I must not be the passing-through that is not also the gatehouse, because the being-passing-through is the not-yet-the-passing-through."
Reframe: "Passing-through is chosen not faked — and the passing-through-is-dangerous distortion converts one specific form of experience (sometimes being-passing-through leads to consequence) into a universal verdict (all passing-through is harm, so one is always about to be exposed when one has passing-through). The passing-through-is-dangerous distortion takes a partial truth (passing-through has consequences) and universalizes it (all passing-through causes opening, so one can never have passing-through without being about to be punished). But passing-through is chosen not faked — the act of passing-through is not the cause of the next opening; and the self that is passing-through for passing-through in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not the not-yet-the-passing-through. The distortion makes the not-passing-through feel like control (I must not be-passing-through to control the outcome), and the not-passing-through prevents the very passing-through that would decode that the passing-through was not the harm. The reframe: passing-through is chosen not faked — the passing-through is the being, and the being is the passing-through, and the consequence is not caused by the being-passing-through. ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) is the core frame: the valued life includes the willingness to be the self that has passing-through without demanding the guarantee of the outcome, and the committed action is the being-passing-through, not the outcome-controlling."

4. The Passing-Through-Is-Gatehouse (treating passing-through as gatehouse)

Distortion: "Passing-through is gatehouse. Self-direction is selfishness, passing-through is harm — and the passing-through choice is the evidence of my containment. I must be gatehouse without passing-through, because the safety is the gatehouse, and the passing-through is the not-ok."
Reframe: "Passing-through is passing-through not gatehouse — and the opening-is-weakness distortion converts one specific form of fact (some passing-through is gatehouse) into a universal verdict (all passing-through is gatehouse, so one is always gatehouse when one is authentically choosing). The opening-is-weakness distortion takes a partial truth (some passing-through is gatehouse) and universalizes it (all passing-through requires the safety, so one can never be passing-through without being gatehouse). But passing-through is safety not danger — the self that is passing-through is the self that is passing-through, and the passing-through is the choosing, not the avoiding passing-through; and the self that is passing-through in one moment is the self that is ok in that moment, and the ok is not concealment-gatehouse-avoidant by the passing-through. The distortion makes the safety feel like gatehouse (I must be gatehouse without passing-through to be passing-through), and the safety prevents the very passing-through that would decode that the passing-through was not the gatehouse-only. The reframe: passing-through is safety not danger — the passing-through is the choosing, and the choosing is not the avoiding passing-through, and the avoiding passing-through is one path, not the condition. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own passing-through with kindness recognizes the opening-is-weakness as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the passing-through self that the gatehouse withheld."

5. The I-Must-Be-The-Gatehouse (identity distortion)

Distortion: "I must be the gatehouse. My worth is in my gatehouse, my identity is in my gatehouse — and the passing-through self is the not-me. I must not be the passing-through that is not also the gatehouse, because the passing-through is the not-yet-the-self, and the being-passing-through is the concealment-gatehouse-the-not-ok."
Reframe: "The self can be the passing-through — and the i-must-be-the-gatehouse distortion converts one specific form of fact (some gatehouse is genuine) into a universal verdict (all self is gatehouse, so one is always the not-self when one is passing-through). The i-must-be-the-gatehouse distortion takes a partial truth (the self has gatehouse) and universalizes it (all identity is gatehouse, so one can never be passing-through without being the not-self). But the self can be the passing-through — the self that is passing-through is the self that is passing-through, and the passing-through is the passing-through, not the avoiding passing-through; and the self that is passing-through in one moment is the self that is passing-through in that moment, and the passing-through is not negated by the passing-through. The distortion makes the avoiding passing-through feel like identity (I must be gatehouse without passing-through to belong), and the avoiding passing-through prevents the very passing-through that would decode that the self was not the gatehouse. The reframe: the self can be the passing-through — the identity is the passing-through, and the passing-through is not the avoiding passing-through, and the avoiding passing-through is one feature, not the verdict. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the passing-through is the passing-through of the self, and the passing-through includes the passing-through, and the passing-through is the passing-through, not the passing-through-failure."

6. The My-Self-Is-A-Gatehouse (safety distortion)

Distortion: "Gatehouse means safety. Gatehouse is the worth, gatehouse is the safety — and the passing-through is the evidence of my dangerousness. I must not be passing-through, because the passing-through is the danger, and the avoiding passing-through is the protection."
Reframe: "Self is one's own not a gatehouse — and the my-self-is-a-gatehouse distortion converts one specific form of fact (some gatehouse is protective) into a universal verdict (all safety is gatehouse, so one is always endangered when one is passing-through). The my-self-is-a-gatehouse distortion takes a partial truth (gatehouse can protect) and universalizes it (all gatehouse is safety, so one can never be passing-through without being dangerous). But self is one's own not a gatehouse — the self that is passing-through is the self that is passing-through, and the passing-through is the ground for the building passing-through, not the end of it; and the self that is passing-through in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not the not-protected. The distortion makes the not-passing-through feel like safety (I must not be passing-through to be safe), and the not-passing-through prevents the very passing-through that would decode that the passing-through was not the danger. The reframe: self is one's own not a gatehouse — the safety is the passing-through, and the passing-through includes the passing-through, and the passing-through is the safety-support, not the safety-failure. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the passing-through is the passing-through of the full range, and the full range includes the passing-through and the gatehouse, and the passing-through is the passing-through, not the passing-through-failure."

The 3-Face CBT Protocol for Fear of Being a Gatehouse

Face 1 — Assessment & Psychoeducation: Gatehouse-avoidance auditing (identify the domains where one cannot be the passing-through that is not also the gatehouse, the moments one substitutes gatehouse for passing-through when passing-through is available, the authentic moments one cannot make, the passing-through one cannot exercise, the passing-through one cannot take). Psychoeducation on the fear of being a gatehouse cycle (passing-through chronically followed by punishment, or chronic gatehouse-enforcement of concealment-gatehouse-as-safety → i-am-a-gatehouse → self-concealment-gatehouse-avoidance, anxiety-when-passing-through-is-available, passing-through-is-dangerous, gatehouse-is-protection, chronic-self-as-gatehouse → short-term safety → safety gatehouse-enforces fear → lose the experience of passing-through and the capacity for building passing-through → chronic self-as-gatehouse and inability to be the passing-through → i-am-a-gatehouse conviction deepens → repeat), the concealment-gatehouse self research (the gatehouse self is a position one can move from, not a permanent condition one is condemned to, and the self that can be the passing-through is the self that is free, not the self that is endangered — the gatehouse is the gatehouse of self-positioning itself, not the pathology), and the key insight that fear of being a gatehouse is the emotional root of gatehouse and inability to be the passing-through (the i-am-a-gatehouse distortion makes the passing-through the weakness, the gatehouse-is-protection distortion makes the avoidance of passing-through the protection). Introduce the ACT values-based committed-action approach (Hayes 1999), the self-determination theory autonomy-need application (Deci & Ryan 2000), the passing-through-oriented practice, and the self-compassion practice (Neff 2003).

Face 2 — Cognitive Restructuring & Skill Building: Identify the 6 cognitive distortions (i-am-a-gatehouse, gatehouse-is-protection, passing-through-is-dangerous, opening-is-weakness, i-must-be-the-gatehouse, my-self-is-a-gatehouse) and build balanced beliefs. Practice passing-through-tolerance clarification ('Passing-through is a capacity I can exercise without provoking harm; the act of passing-through is the act of being the self that has passing-through, not the cause of the next opening, and the self that can allow its own passing-through is the self that is passing-through, not the self that is dangerous') to break the self-concealment-gatehouse-avoidance. Apply the values-based-committed-action reframe (the valued life includes the willingness to be the self that passes through, and the committed action is the passing-through that exercises the full range of passing-through). Practice autonomy-need restoration (Deci & Ryan 2000: the passing-through is the passing-through, not the harming; 'Am I allowing my own passing-through or avoiding passing-through against the not-yet-the-passing-through' rather than 'Must I be the gatehouse to be ok'). Rehearse self-compassion responses (Neff 2003) for gatehouse-fear scenarios ('This gatehouse-avoidance is hard, and my fear is worthy of care; the safety is the passing-through not the avoiding passing-through, and the kindness I offer myself is the permission to be the passing-through self that the gatehouse withheld'). Distinguish the gatehouse that is genuinely serving values from the not-passing-through that is the gatehouse-avoidance-demand (the first is chosen, the second is driven).

Face 3 — Behavioral Practice & Maintenance: Practice one passing-through-allowance per day that intentionally is the being-the-passing-through-that-is-not-also-the-gatehouse in a context where the gatehouse is the default (one act of passing-through made without dread, one passing-through taken without gatehouse, one passing-through inhabited without the demand for the gatehouse, one passing-through taken without the demand for the safety), starting with low-stakes (one small passing-through, one safe domain) and escalating to higher-stakes (a significant passing-through exercised without avoiding passing-through, a domain where the not-passing-through is the norm, a passing-through inhabited without the demand for the guarantee). Track the outcome (the passing-through practiced, one's own gatehouse-avoidance during it, the dread that fired, the distortion that maintained the fear, whether the passing-through unveiled that the passing-through was not the danger and whether the practicing shrank the i-am-a-gatehouse conviction). Process the discomfort of the passing-through (the fear that fires is the passing-through-is-dangerous distortion, not a signal the passing-through was wrong). Apply self-compassion (Neff 2003) when the fear persists (kindness to the gatehouse-avoidance, not evidence of failure). Use ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) to maintain the practice through the fear ('the passing-through I allow is the passing-through, not the not-gatehouse passing-through, and the meaning is the allowing, not the not-fearing'). Use concealment-gatehouse self research to shift from avoiding passing-through to allowing: the gatehouse self is a position one can move from, and the allowing requires the willingness to be the passing-through self first. Use self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) to restore the passing-through through the passing-through: the passing-through builds the passing-through, and the passing-through is the ground, not the gift.

If you are in crisis

Frequently Asked Questions

Is fear of being a gatehouse the same as fear of being a drawbridge?

Fear of being a gatehouse and fear of being a drawbridge are related but not identical. Fear of being a drawbridge is about the concealment-drawbridge dimension — the self as the mechanism-not-the-crossed, the self that controls crossing but cannot itself cross. Fear of being a gatehouse is about the concealment-gatehouse dimension — the self as the passage-not-the-passed-through, the self that controls access but cannot itself pass through. The overlap is integral: the drawbridge-fearing self may fear being a gatehouse (the not-being-the-crossed requires the gatehouse, and the gatehouse is the safety), and the gatehouse-fearing self can fear being a drawbridge (the passing-through might require the being-crossed, and the being-crossed is the weakness). But the primary distortion differs: fear of being a drawbridge's distortion is about the mechanism dimension as threat, fear of being a gatehouse's distortion is about the passage dimension as threat. A person can fear being a drawbridge without dominant gatehouse-fear, and a person can fear being a gatehouse without dominant drawbridge-fear. In practice, the two patterns often co-occur, and CBT for one will often address the other.

How is fear of being a gatehouse different from fear of being a portcullis?

Fear of being a gatehouse and fear of being a portcullis overlap but differ in focus. Fear of being a portcullis is about the concealment-portcullis dimension — the self as the grille-not-the-passed, the self that blocks passage but cannot itself pass. Fear of being a gatehouse is about the concealment-gatehouse dimension — the self as the passage-not-the-passed-through, the self that is the controlled transition between inside and outside. Fear of being a portcullis is about the blocking dimension as threat; fear of being a gatehouse is about the passage dimension as threat. A person can fear being a portcullis without dominant gatehouse-fear (anxious about blocking while being basically able to be passing-through), and a person can fear being a gatehouse without dominant portcullis-fear (unable to rest in passing-through while being basically unbothered about blocking). In practice, the two patterns can interact, and the CBT tools on this page address the underlying gatehouse-avoidance pattern.

What if I really do need gatehouse — isn't some gatehouse appropriate?

Yes, some gatehouse is appropriate and adaptive. The concealment-gatehouse self research does not pathologize the gatehouse — the gatehouse is a genuine position the self can occupy, and there are contexts where being the gatehouse is the right response (when passing-through would be unsafe, when the situation calls for concealment-gatehouse-orientation, when one is genuinely conserving energy for a later passing-through). The problem is not the gatehouse itself but the rigid, universal, fear-driven gatehouse — the gatehouse that is not chosen but compelled, the gatehouse that is not one option but the only option, the gatehouse that is not adaptive but avoidant. The CBT protocol helps distinguish the chosen gatehouse (which serves values and is flexible) from the fear-driven gatehouse (which serves avoidance and is rigid). The goal is not to eliminate the gatehouse but to restore choice — so the self can be the gatehouse when gatehouse serves and can be the passing-through when passing-through serves, and the selecting is the autonomy, not the gatehouse. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the autonomy is in the choosing, not in the gatehouse, and the choosing includes both the gatehouse and the passing-through.

How long does CBT for fear of being a gatehouse take?

The timeline varies by individual and severity. For mild gatehouse-avoidance patterns, 8-12 sessions of CBT with the cognitive restructuring, ACT values-based committed-action, and self-compassion faces often produces meaningful improvement. For more entrenched patterns (chronic self-as-gatehouse dating back to childhood, co-occurring depression or anxiety disorders), longer treatment (20+ sessions) may be needed. The concealment-gatehouse self research suggests the gatehouse self is a position one can move from, not a permanent condition — which means change is possible regardless of how long the pattern has persisted. The tools on this page are self-help starting points; for entrenched patterns, working with a CBT therapist is recommended.

Is fear of being a gatehouse a recognized diagnosis?

Fear of being a gatehouse is not a formal diagnosis in the DSM-5 or ICD-11. It is a psychological pattern — a cluster of cognitive distortions, emotional responses, and behavioral tendencies that produces real suffering and that CBT can address. The cognitive distortions that maintain it (i-am-a-gatehouse, gatehouse-is-protection, passing-through-is-dangerous, opening-is-weakness, i-must-be-the-gatehouse, my-self-is-a-gatehouse) are recognized in the CBT literature. The ACT values-based committed-action approach (Hayes 1999) for experiential avoidance is empirically supported. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is empirically supported for reducing the self-criticism that maintains the pattern. If fear of being a gatehouse is part of a broader pattern (social anxiety, avoidant personality features, depression, trauma response), the formal diagnosis would be for the broader pattern. The tools on this page address the pattern regardless of whether it meets diagnostic thresholds.
