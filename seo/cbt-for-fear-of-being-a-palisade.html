Fear of being a palisade — the conviction that escaping is dangerous and palisade is safety, the anticipation that being the self that has escaping will be punished or lead to harm, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is palisade-tolerant-of-concealment-palisade and the palisade is the protection — is one of the most pervasive and painful emotional patterns in concealment-palisade-oriented-conditioned culture. It is the emotional root that drives self-concealment-palisade-avoidance (the substitution of palisade for escaping when escaping is available, the inability to be the escaping that is not also the palisade, the conversion of every act of escaping into a harm), anxiety when escaping is available (the escaping itself produces dread, the escaping is the trigger, the available escaping is the warning signal), treating escaping as dangerous/harmful (the person cannot allow the escaping because the escaping is the not-yet-the-loosening, the person cannot be the escaping-that-is-not-also-the-palisade because the being-escaping is the not-yet-the-escaping), equating palisade with safety (the belief that palisade is protection and escaping is danger, the framing of the palisade as the worth and the escaping as the threat), and chronic self-as-palisade (the persistent substitution of palisade for escaping to avoid the escaping of escaping, the inability to be the escaping because the escaping is the endangered). It is distinct from fear of being an enigma (which is about the concealment-palisade dimension — the self as the trap-not-the-escaped, the self that hides by trapping) and from fear of being a snare (which is about the concealment-snare dimension — the self as the snare-not-the-escaped, the self that hides by snaring). Fear of being a palisade is about the concealment-palisade dimension: the self that is only a palisade, the self that has no escaping, the self that is the palisade not the escaping, the self that is the palisade not the snaring, the self that cannot rest in escaping because the escaping is the danger, the self that experiences its own escaping as a weakness of loosening, the self that treats every act of escaping as a threat and every escaping as the not-yet-the-escaping.

Fear of being a palisade is the emotional root of three downstream patterns. The i-am-a-palisade distortion makes every act of escaping a harm and every escaping a weakness, producing self-concealment-palisade-avoidance and anxiety-when-escaping-is-available (the person concludes the only safety is in the palisade and the palisade requires the not-being-the-escaping-that-is-not-also-the-concealment-palisade, so the escaping is refused and the escaping is never exercised because the escaping is always the not-yet-the-escaping). The palisade-is-protection distortion makes the palisade the protection and the escaping the danger, producing the chronic devaluation of escaping and the equation of palisade with worth (the person experiences the escaping and the escaping is the dangerous, and the dangerous is the not-safe, and the not-safe is the threat the palisade was meant to prevent). And the escaping-is-dangerous distortion makes the act-of-escaping the impending-loosening and the escaping the warning, producing chronic-self-as-palisade and the constant-avoidance of escaping-requirement (the person has escaping and the escaping is the harm, and the harm is the consequence, and the consequence is the loosening the palisade was meant to prevent). Fear of being a palisade is the upstream cause; self-concealment-palisade-avoidance, anxiety-when-escaping-is-available, and chronic-self-as-palisade are the downstream effects. The key insight for fear of being a palisade: escaping is not the danger the palisade protects from — the self that can allow its own escaping is the self that is escaping, not the self that is dangerous, and the safety is not the palisade but the escaping, and the escaping is the being-escaping, not the palisade. Concealment-palisade self research provides the deeper frame: the palisade self is a position one can move from, not a permanent condition one is condemned to, and the self that can be the escaping is the self that is filled, not the self that is endangered.

The Fear of Being a Palisade Cycle (How It Develops)

The fear of being a palisade cycle is the self-palisadeforcing loop that produces the self-concealment-palisade-avoidance, the anxiety-when-escaping-is-available, and the chronic-self-as-palisade that come from concluding that escaping is dangerous and palisade is safety:

A situation with escaping chronically followed by punishment, or chronic palisadeforcement of concealment-palisade-as-safety (an early experience where being the escaping that had escaping was criticized, where the escaping was shamed, where the escaping preceded the harm, a chronic palisadeforcement of palisade where one's sense of value was entirely defined by their capacity to be the palisade and never by their capacity to be the escaping) → i-am-a-palisade fires (the escaping is the danger, the escaping is the weakness, the escaping is the warning, the escaping is the not-yet-the-escaping) → self-concealment-palisade-avoidance, anxiety-when-escaping-is-available, escaping-is-dangerous, palisade-is-protection, chronic-self-as-palisade (the substitution of palisade for escaping, the inability to be the escaping that is not also the palisade, the constant avoidance of escaping, the devaluation of escaping, the constant palisade-as-safety) → short-term safety achieved (the palisade feels like protection from the loosening, the not-being-the-escaping-that-is-not-also-the-concealment-palisade feels like the control over the outcome, the palisade feels like the safety) → the safety palisadeforces the fear (the palisade prevents the escaping that would decode that the escaping was not the danger, the not-escaping prevents the evidence that would disconfirm the i-am-a-palisade conviction, the concealment-palisade-as-safety prevents the escaping that would decode that the escaping was not the danger) → the person loses the experience of escaping and the capacity for building escaping (the escaping that comes from the allowing is never experienced, because the allowing is always the not-yet-the-escaping; the escaping that comes from the being-the-escaping-that-is-not-also-the-palisade is never exercised, because the being-escaping is always the not-yet-the-escaping) → chronic self-as-palisade and inability to be the escaping → i-am-a-palisade conviction deepens → repeat

The cycle is driven by the short-term safety that palisade and not-escaping provides (palisade-because-the-palisade-is-the-protection-from-the-loosening and not-escaping-because-the-not-escaping-is-the-control-over-the-outcome both feel like they are preventing the loosening, and the feeling reduces the fear of being a palisade temporarily) and maintained by the long-term cost of the i-am-a-palisade pattern (the escaping is never experienced, because the escaping is always the not-yet-the-escaping; the escaping is never exercised for the self, because the being-escaping is always the not-yet-the-escaping; and the i-am-a-palisade conviction deepens because every prevented escaping is treated as evidence that the escaping was the danger). The tragic irony of fear of being a palisade is that the safety strategy (palisade, not-escaping, concealment-palisade-as-safety) produces the very outcome it was meant to prevent (the person refuses the escaping and the refusing-the-escaping produces the very harm the palisade was meant to disprove, and the chronic not-allowing-escaping is the loss of the escaping, not the protection from the loosening — the fear of being a palisade prevents the very escaping that would have made the life worth the escaping). The CBT intervention breaks the cycle at two points: (1) escaping-tolerance-clarification — when the i-am-a-palisade distortion treats every act of escaping as a harm, the first step is clarifying that escaping is a capacity one can exercise without provoking harm, and recognizing that the self that can allow its own escaping is the self that is escaping, not the self that is dangerous; (2) values-based-committed-action — when the palisade-is-protection distortion fires, one challenges it with the ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999): the valued life includes the willingness to be the self that has escaping, and the committed action is the being-escaping that exercises the full range of escaping.

The 6 Cognitive Distortions & Their Reframes

1. The Escaping-Is-Dangerous (self-concealment-palisade-avoidance)

Distortion: "Escaping is dangerous. If I allow myself to be the escaping that is not also the palisade, something bad will happen. Escaping is a weakness of loosening, a setup for the punishment. I must avoid escaping, because the escaping is the not-yet-the-escaping, and the palisade is the safety."
Reframe: "Escaping is a capacity not a weakness — and the i-am-a-palisade distortion converts one specific form of experience (sometimes escaping is followed by consequence) into a universal verdict (all escaping is the harm, so one is always provoking loosening when one is escaping). The i-am-a-palisade distortion takes a partial truth (consequences happen) and universalizes it (all escaping causes harm, so one can never be escaping without endangering oneself). But escaping is a capacity not a weakness — the act of escaping is the act of being the self that has escaping, not the cause of the next loosening; and the self that is escaping in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not concealment-palisade-avoidant by the escaping. The distortion makes the palisade feel like protection (I must avoid escaping because escaping is dangerous), and the palisade prevents the very escaping that would decode that the escaping was not the danger. The reframe: escaping is a capacity not a weakness — the safety is the allowing, and the allowing is not the not-palisade escaping, and the not-palisade escaping is the escaping, not the endangering. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own palisade-fear with kindness recognizes the i-am-a-palisade as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the escaping self that the palisade withheld."

2. The Palisade-Is-Protection (treating escaping as danger)

Distortion: "Palisade equals safety. Self-avoidance of escaping is protection, escaping is danger — and the escaping is the evidence of my dangerousness. I must be palisade without escaping, because the safety is the palisade, and the escaping is the not-safe."
Reframe: "Palisade is one option not safety — and the palisade-is-protection distortion converts one specific form of experience (some palisade protects) into a universal verdict (all palisade is safety, so one is always endangered when one is escaping). The palisade-is-protection distortion takes a partial truth (some avoidance of escaping is protective) and universalizes it (all palisade is safety, so one can never be escaping without being endangered). But palisade is one option not safety — the self that is concealment-palisade-avoidant is not automatically the self that is safe, and the self that is escaping is not automatically the self that is endangered; and the safety is not the avoidance of escaping but the escaping, and the escaping includes both the chosen palisade and the chosen escaping. The distortion makes the safety feel like safety (I must be palisade without escaping to be safe), and the safety prevents the very escaping that would decode that the escaping was not the danger. The reframe: palisade is one option not safety — the safety is the escaping of the full range, and the full range includes the escaping, and the escaping is not the not-safe but one expression of the safe. Concealment-palisade self research is the core frame: the palisade self is a position one can move from, and the self that can be the escaping is the self that is filled, not the endangered self."

3. The Weakness-Is-Weakness (anxiety when escaping is available)

Distortion: "If I have escaping, something bad will happen. The act-of-escaping is the impending-loosening, the escaping is the warning — and the escaping is the evidence of my upcoming punishment. I must not be the escaping that is not also the palisade, because the being-escaping is the not-yet-the-escaping."
Reframe: "Escaping is chosen not faked — and the escaping-is-dangerous distortion converts one specific form of experience (sometimes being-escaping leads to consequence) into a universal verdict (all escaping is harm, so one is always about to be exposed when one has escaping). The escaping-is-dangerous distortion takes a partial truth (escaping has consequences) and universalizes it (all escaping causes loosening, so one can never have escaping without being about to be punished). But escaping is chosen not faked — the act of escaping is not the cause of the next loosening; and the self that is escaping for escaping in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not the not-yet-the-escaping. The distortion makes the not-escaping feel like control (I must not be-escaping to control the outcome), and the not-escaping prevents the very escaping that would decode that the escaping was not the harm. The reframe: escaping is chosen not faked — the escaping is the being, and the being is the escaping, and the consequence is not caused by the being-escaping. ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) is the core frame: the valued life includes the willingness to be the self that has escaping without demanding the guarantee of the outcome, and the committed action is the being-escaping, not the outcome-controlling."

4. The Escaping-Is-Danger (treating escaping as palisade)

Distortion: "Escaping is palisade. Self-direction is selfishness, escaping is harm — and the escaping choice is the evidence of my containment. I must be palisade without escaping, because the safety is the palisade, and the escaping is the not-ok."
Reframe: "Escaping is escaping not palisade — and the loosening-is-weakness distortion converts one specific form of fact (some escaping is palisade) into a universal verdict (all escaping is palisade, so one is always palisade when one is authentically choosing). The loosening-is-weakness distortion takes a partial truth (some escaping is palisade) and universalizes it (all escaping requires the safety, so one can never be escaping without being palisade). But escaping is safety not danger — the self that is escaping is the self that is escaping, and the escaping is the choosing, not the avoiding escaping; and the self that is escaping in one moment is the self that is ok in that moment, and the ok is not concealment-palisade-avoidant by the escaping. The distortion makes the safety feel like palisade (I must be palisade without escaping to be escaping), and the safety prevents the very escaping that would decode that the escaping was not the palisade-only. The reframe: escaping is safety not danger — the escaping is the choosing, and the choosing is not the avoiding escaping, and the avoiding escaping is one path, not the condition. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own escaping with kindness recognizes the loosening-is-weakness as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the escaping self that the palisade withheld."

5. The I-Must-Be-The-Palisade (identity distortion)

Distortion: "I must be the palisade. My worth is in my palisade, my identity is in my palisade — and the escaping self is the not-me. I must not be the escaping that is not also the palisade, because the escaping is the not-yet-the-self, and the being-escaping is the concealment-palisade-the-not-ok."
Reframe: "The self can be the escaping — and the i-must-be-the-palisade distortion converts one specific form of fact (some palisade is genuine) into a universal verdict (all self is palisade, so one is always the not-self when one is escaping). The i-must-be-the-palisade distortion takes a partial truth (the self has palisade) and universalizes it (all identity is palisade, so one can never be escaping without being the not-self). But the self can be the escaping — the self that is escaping is the self that is escaping, and the escaping is the escaping, not the avoiding escaping; and the self that is escaping in one moment is the self that is escaping in that moment, and the escaping is not negated by the escaping. The distortion makes the avoiding escaping feel like identity (I must be palisade without escaping to belong), and the avoiding escaping prevents the very escaping that would decode that the self was not the palisade. The reframe: the self can be the escaping — the identity is the escaping, and the escaping is not the avoiding escaping, and the avoiding escaping is one feature, not the verdict. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the escaping is the escaping of the self, and the escaping includes the escaping, and the escaping is the escaping, not the escaping-failure."

6. The My-Self-Is-A-Palisade (safety distortion)

Distortion: "Palisade means safety. Palisade is the worth, palisade is the safety — and the escaping is the evidence of my dangerousness. I must not be escaping, because the escaping is the danger, and the avoiding escaping is the protection."
Reframe: "Self is one's own not a palisade — and the my-self-is-a-palisade distortion converts one specific form of fact (some palisade is protective) into a universal verdict (all safety is palisade, so one is always endangered when one is escaping). The my-self-is-a-palisade distortion takes a partial truth (palisade can protect) and universalizes it (all palisade is safety, so one can never be escaping without being dangerous). But self is one's own not a palisade — the self that is escaping is the self that is escaping, and the escaping is the ground for the building escaping, not the end of it; and the self that is escaping in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not the not-protected. The distortion makes the not-escaping feel like safety (I must not be escaping to be safe), and the not-escaping prevents the very escaping that would decode that the escaping was not the danger. The reframe: self is one's own not a palisade — the safety is the escaping, and the escaping includes the escaping, and the escaping is the safety-support, not the safety-failure. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the escaping is the escaping of the full range, and the full range includes the escaping and the palisade, and the escaping is the escaping, not the escaping-failure."

The 3-Face CBT Protocol for Fear of Being a Palisade

Face 1 — Assessment & Psychoeducation: Palisade-avoidance auditing (identify the domains where one cannot be the escaping that is not also the palisade, the moments one substitutes palisade for escaping when escaping is available, the authentic moments one cannot make, the escaping one cannot exercise, the escaping one cannot take). Psychoeducation on the fear of being a palisade cycle (escaping chronically followed by punishment, or chronic palisadeforcement of concealment-palisade-as-safety → i-am-a-palisade → self-concealment-palisade-avoidance, anxiety-when-escaping-is-available, escaping-is-dangerous, palisade-is-protection, chronic-self-as-palisade → short-term safety → safety palisadeforces fear → lose the experience of escaping and the capacity for building escaping → chronic self-as-palisade and inability to be the escaping → i-am-a-palisade conviction deepens → repeat), the concealment-palisade self research (the palisade self is a position one can move from, not a permanent condition one is condemned to, and the self that can be the escaping is the self that is filled, not the self that is endangered — the palisade is the palisade of self-positioning itself, not the pathology), and the key insight that fear of being a palisade is the emotional root of palisade and inability to be the escaping (the i-am-a-palisade distortion makes the escaping the weakness, the palisade-is-protection distortion makes the avoidance of escaping the protection). Introduce the ACT values-based committed-action approach (Hayes 1999), the self-determination theory autonomy-need application (Deci & Ryan 2000), the escaping-oriented practice, and the self-compassion practice (Neff 2003).

Face 2 — Cognitive Restructuring & Skill Building: Identify the 6 cognitive distortions (i-am-a-palisade, palisade-is-protection, escaping-is-dangerous, loosening-is-weakness, i-must-be-the-palisade, my-self-is-a-palisade) and build balanced beliefs. Practice escaping-tolerance clarification ('Escaping is a capacity I can exercise without provoking harm; the act of escaping is the act of being the self that has escaping, not the cause of the next loosening, and the self that can allow its own escaping is the self that is escaping, not the self that is dangerous') to break the self-concealment-palisade-avoidance. Apply the values-based-committed-action reframe (the valued life includes the willingness to be the self that has escaping, and the committed action is the escaping that exercises the full range of escaping). Practice autonomy-need restoration (Deci & Ryan 2000: the escaping is the escaping, not the harming; 'Am I allowing my own escaping or avoiding escaping against the not-yet-the-escaping' rather than 'Must I be the palisade to be ok'). Rehearse self-compassion responses (Neff 2003) for palisade-fear scenarios ('This palisade-avoidance is hard, and my fear is worthy of care; the safety is the escaping not the avoiding escaping, and the kindness I offer myself is the permission to be the escaping self that the palisade withheld'). Distinguish the palisade that is genuinely serving values from the not-escaping that is the palisade-avoidance-demand (the first is chosen, the second is driven).

Face 3 — Behavioral Practice & Maintenance: Practice one escaping-allowance per day that intentionally is the being-the-escaping-that-is-not-also-the-palisade in a context where the palisade is the default (one act of escaping made without dread, one escaping taken without palisade, one escaping inhabited without the demand for the palisade, one escaping taken without the demand for the safety), starting with low-stakes (one small escaping, one safe domain) and escalating to higher-stakes (a significant escaping exercised without avoiding escaping, a domain where the not-escaping is the norm, a escaping inhabited without the demand for the guarantee). Track the outcome (the escaping practiced, one's own palisade-avoidance during it, the dread that fired, the distortion that maintained the fear, whether the escaping unveiled that the escaping was not the danger and whether the practicing shrank the i-am-a-palisade conviction). Process the discomfort of the escaping (the fear that fires is the escaping-is-dangerous distortion, not a signal the escaping was wrong). Apply self-compassion (Neff 2003) when the fear persists (kindness to the palisade-avoidance, not evidence of failure). Use ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) to maintain the practice through the fear ('the escaping I allow is the escaping, not the not-palisade escaping, and the meaning is the allowing, not the not-fearing'). Use concealment-palisade self research to shift from avoiding escaping to allowing: the palisade self is a position one can move from, and the allowing requires the willingness to be the escaping self first. Use self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) to restore the escaping through the escaping: the escaping builds the escaping, and the escaping is the ground, not the gift.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is fear of being a palisade the same as fear of being an enigma?

Fear of being a palisade and fear of being a shell are related but not identical. Fear of being an enigma is the conviction that escaping is dangerous and enigma is safety — the anticipation that being the self that has holding will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is enigma-tolerant-of-concealment-palisade. Fear of being a palisade is the conviction that escaping is dangerous and palisade is safety — the anticipation that being the self that has escaping will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is palisade-tolerant-of-concealment-palisade. The overlap is integral: the enigma-fearing self may fear being a palisade (the not-being-the-content requires the palisade, and the palisade is the safety, so the only safety is in the palisade), and the palisade-fearing self can fear being a shell (the escaping might require the being-content, and the being-content is the weakness, so the only safety is in the shell). But the primary distortion differs: fear of being an enigma's distortion is about the concealment-palisade dimension as threat (is the self that is only the hider safe), fear of being a palisade's distortion is about the concealment-palisade dimension as threat (is the self that is only the palisade safe). A person can fear being an enigma without dominant palisade-fear (anxious about being the hider while being basically able to be escaping), and a person can fear being a palisade without dominant enigma-fear (unable to rest in escaping while being basically unbothered about hider). In practice, the two patterns often co-occur, and CBT for one will often address the other. The self-compassion (Neff 2003), ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999), and concealment-palisade self research tools on this page address the underlying palisade-avoidance pattern.

How is fear of being a palisade different from fear of being a cipher?

Fear of being a palisade and fear of being a hollow overlap but differ in focus. Fear of being a cipher is the conviction that readability is dangerous and cipher is safety — the anticipation that being the self that has filling will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is cipher-tolerant-of-concealment-encoding. Fear of being a palisade is the conviction that escaping is dangerous and palisade is safety — the anticipation that being the self that has escaping will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is palisade-tolerant-of-concealment-palisade. Fear of being a cipher is about the concealment-encoding dimension as threat (is the self that is only empty safe); fear of being a palisade is about the concealment-palisade dimension as threat (is the self that is only the palisade safe). A person can fear being a cipher without dominant palisade-fear (anxious about being empty while being basically able to be escaping), and a person can fear being a palisade without dominant cipher-fear (unable to rest in escaping while being basically ok with not being empty). In practice, the two patterns can interact (the cipher-fearing self may fear being a palisade — the substance might decode the escaping, and the escaping is the weakness, so the only safety is in the palisade; and the palisade-fearing self may fear being a cipher — the escaping is the escaping, and the escaping is the dangerous), and the CBT tools on this page address the underlying palisade-avoidance pattern. The self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the unifying frame: both patterns involve a threat to the self's acceptability (the substance self and the escaping self both threaten the palisade-self), and the restoration — through acceptance of the full range of experience rather than demand for the self to be palisade — is the core intervention.

What if I really do need palisade — isn't some palisade appropriate?

Yes, some palisade is appropriate and adaptive. The concealment-palisade self research does not pathologize the palisade — the palisade is a genuine position the self can occupy, and there are contexts where being the palisade is the right response (when escaping would be unsafe, when the situation calls for concealment-palisade-orientation, when one is genuinely conserving energy for a later escaping). The problem is not the palisade itself but the rigid, universal, fear-driven palisade — the palisade that is not chosen but compelled, the palisade that is not one option but the only option, the palisade that is not adaptive but avoidant. The CBT protocol helps distinguish the chosen palisade (which serves values and is flexible) from the fear-driven palisade (which serves avoidance and is rigid). The goal is not to eliminate the palisade but to restore choice — so the self can be the palisade when palisade serves and can be the escaping when escaping serves, and the selecting is the autonomy, not the palisade. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the autonomy is in the choosing, not in the palisade, and the choosing includes both the palisade and the escaping.

How long does CBT for fear of being a palisade take?

The timeline varies by individual and severity. For mild palisade-avoidance patterns, 8-12 sessions of CBT with the cognitive restructuring, ACT values-based committed-action, and self-compassion faces often produces meaningful improvement. For more entpalisadeed patterns (chronic self-as-palisade dating back to childhood, co-occurring depression or anxiety disorders), longer treatment (20+ sessions) may be needed, and integration with other evidence-based approaches (e.g., loosening-based practices for the escaping-avoidance, schema therapy for the early palisade-palisadeforcement patterns) can be helpful. The concealment-palisade self research suggests the palisade self is a position one can move from, not a permanent condition — which means change is possible regardless of how long the pattern has persisted, but the depth of the pattern informs the intensity of the work. The tools on this page are self-help starting points; for entpalisadeed patterns, working with a CBT therapist who can tailor the protocol to your specific palisade-avoidance patterns is recommended.

Is fear of being a palisade a recognized diagnosis?

Fear of being a palisade is not a formal diagnosis in the DSM-5 or ICD-11. It is a psychological pattern — a cluster of cognitive distortions, emotional responses, and behavioral tendencies that produces real suffering and that CBT can address. The cognitive distortions that maintain it (i-am-a-palisade, palisade-is-protection, escaping-is-dangerous, loosening-is-weakness, i-must-be-the-palisade, my-self-is-a-palisade) are recognized in the CBT literature. The ACT values-based committed-action approach (Hayes 1999) for experiential avoidance is empirically supported. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is empirically supported for reducing the self-criticism that maintains the pattern. If fear of being a palisade is part of a broader pattern (social anxiety, avoidant personality features, depression, trauma response), the formal diagnosis would be for the broader pattern, and the palisade-avoidance would be a target within that treatment. The tools on this page address the pattern regardless of whether it meets diagnostic thresholds.
