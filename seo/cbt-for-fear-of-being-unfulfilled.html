Fear of being unfulfilled — the conviction that the current self is not the possible self and the gap is the failure, the anticipation that the present is the not-yet and the not-yet is the evidence of an unfulfilled life, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is the possible self and the possible is the fulfilled — is one of the most pervasive and painful emotional patterns in self-actualization-driven culture. It is the emotional root that drives potential-gap (the belief that the current is the not-yet-the-possible, the conversion of every present-self into a deficit, the treating of the now as the not-yet-the-real), not-yet-achieved (the conclusion that the not-yet-achieved is the failure, the pathologizing of the in-process that treats the becoming as the not-yet-the-arrived, the framing of the present as the betrayal of the possible), comparison-to-possible (the compulsive measuring of the current against the possible, the conversion of being into benchmarking, the treating of the present-self as the entry in a ledger that must close), chronic-dissatisfaction (the persistent not-enough that comes from the gap, the inability to rest in the current because the current is the not-yet, the chronic not-quite-that defines the relation to the present), treating-current-as-provisional (the framing of the present self as the not-yet-the-real-self, the treating of the current as the draft and the possible as the final, the conclusion that the self one is now is not the self one really is), and equating fulfillment with worth (the demand that one must be fulfilled to be ok, the equation of the not-fulfilled with the not-ok, the conclusion that the self is only legitimate insofar as the self is the possible). It is distinct from fear of wasting life (which is about the life as a whole — the self that fears the life is the not-optimal-life) and from fear of being ordinary (which is about status/achievement level — the self that fears being average) and from fear of not being enough (which is about person-level adequacy — the self that fears being inadequate as a person). Fear of being unfulfilled is about the conviction that the current self is not the possible self and the gap is the failure: the self that cannot be in the present because the present is the not-yet-the-possible and the not-yet is the failure, the self that refuses the current self because the current self is the provisional and the provisional is the not-real, the self that treats every present-action as the not-yet-the-fulfilled-action and every not-fulfilled action as the evidence of an unfulfilled self.

Fear of being unfulfilled is the emotional root of three downstream patterns. The current-is-not-the-possible distortion makes every present-self a deficit and every now a not-yet-the-real, producing potential-gap and comparison-to-possible (the person concludes the only safety is in the becoming and the becoming requires the comparing, so the present is refused and the current self is never inhabited because the current self is always the not-yet-the-possible). The not-yet-achieved-is-failure distortion makes the becoming the obligation and the in-process the failure, producing not-yet-achieved and treating-current-as-provisional (the person acts and the acting is the not-yet-arrived, and the not-yet-arrived is the failure, and the failure is the not-fulfilled — but the fear of the not-fulfilled prevents the present-action, and the not-present-action is the not-becoming, and the not-becoming is the not-fulfilled, and the cycle deepens). And the fulfillment-equals-worth distortion makes the not-fulfilled the not-ok and the fulfillment the worth, producing chronic-dissatisfaction and the constant-becoming-requirement (the person gets a self and the self is the not-fulfilled, and the not-fulfilled is the not-ok, and the not-ok is the threat the becoming was meant to prevent). Fear of being unfulfilled is the upstream cause; potential-gap, comparison-to-possible, and not-yet-achieved are the downstream effects. The key insight for fear of being unfulfilled: the present action is the fulfilled action when it is the valued action — the self that can act from values in the present is the self that is fulfilled, not the self that is unfulfilled, and the fulfillment is the engaged present-action from values, not the comparison to the possible self. The fulfillment research provides the deeper frame: fulfillment is not found in the arrival at the possible self but in the engaged present-action from values — the fulfilled life is the one lived in valued-action now, not the one lived in the benchmarking of the current against the possible.

The Fear of Being Unfulfilled Cycle (How It Develops)

The fear of being unfulfilled cycle is the self-reinforcing loop that produces the potential-gap, the comparison-to-possible, and the not-yet-achieved that come from concluding that the current self is not the possible self and the gap is the failure:

A situation with the possible chronically invoked as the standard, or chronic reinforcement of becoming-as-worth (an early experience where the current was never enough because the possible demanded more, where the not-yet-achieved was treated as the failure, where the self's worth was entirely defined by the gap between the current and the possible, a chronic reinforcement of self-actualization where one's sense of value was entirely defined by the self one could become) → current-is-not-the-possible fires (the present is the not-yet, the now is the deficit, the not-fulfilled is the failure, the comparison is the living, the not-arrived is the verdict) → potential-gap, not-yet-achieved, comparison-to-possible, chronic-dissatisfaction, treating-current-as-provisional, fulfillment-worth-equation (the anxious becoming, the refusing to be the current self, the compulsive measuring against the possible, the pathologizing of the in-process, the equation of not-fulfilled with not-ok, the demand that the self be the possible) → short-term safety achieved (the becoming feels like protection from the unfulfilled, the comparison feels like the control over the gap, the not-present avoids the confrontation with the not-possible) → the safety reinforces the fear (the becoming prevents the presence that would reveal that the present was the fulfilled-when-valued, the comparison prevents the living that would reveal that the living was not the benchmarking, the not-present prevents the evidence that would disconfirm the unfulfilled-self conviction) → the person loses the experience of presence and the capacity for being the current self (the presence that comes from the present-action is never experienced, because the present-action is always the not-yet-the-fulfilled; the living that comes from the valued-action is never experienced, because the valued-action is the not-arrived and the not-arrived is the not-fulfilled; and the unfulfilled-self conviction deepens because every prevented presence is treated as evidence that the self was the unfulfilled).

The cycle is driven by the short-term safety that becoming and comparing provides (becoming-because-the-becoming-is-the-protection-from-the-unfulfilled and comparing-because-the-comparing-is-the-control-over-the-gap both feel like they are preventing the unfulfilled self, and the feeling reduces the fear of being unfulfilled temporarily) and maintained by the long-term cost of the current-is-not-the-possible pattern (the presence is never experienced, because the present is always the not-yet-the-possible; the current self is never inhabited, because the current is always the provisional; and the unfulfilled-self conviction deepens because every prevented presence is treated as evidence that the self was the unfulfilled). The tragic irony of fear of being unfulfilled is that the safety strategy (becoming, comparing, not-present) produces the very outcome it was meant to prevent (the person refuses the present-action and the refusing-the-present-action produces the very unfulfilled self the becoming was meant to disprove, and the chronic not-present is the self-abandonment, not the self-actualization — the fear of being unfulfilled prevents the very present-action that would have made the self fulfilled). The CBT intervention breaks the cycle at two points: (1) present-action-is-the-fulfilled-action-clarification — when the current-is-not-the-possible distortion treats every present-action as the not-yet, the first step is clarifying that the present action is the fulfilled action when it is the valued action (the possible is one frame, not the measure, and the present-action from values is the fulfillment, not the not-yet), and recognizing that the self that can act from values in the present is the self that is fulfilled; (2) values-based-present-action — when the fulfillment-equals-worth distortion fires, one challenges it with the ACT values-based present-action (Hayes 1999): the fulfilled life is the one lived in valued-action in the present, not the one lived in the comparison to the possible for worth.

The 6 Cognitive Distortions & Their Reframes

1. The Current-Is-Not-The-Possible (potential-gap)

Distortion: "The current is not the possible. There is a gap between who I am and who I could be, and the gap is the failure — not a fact but a verdict. Whatever I am, I am not-yet, because the current is the not-yet-the-possible, and the present is the deficit. I will be unfulfilled, because the current is the not-possible, and the not-possible is the threat."
Reframe: "The present is the fulfilled when it is the valued — and the current-is-not-the-possible distortion converts one specific form of fact (there is a possible self one could become) into a universal verdict (all present-selves are the deficit, so one is always unfulfilled when one is present). The current-is-not-the-possible distortion takes a partial truth (one can grow) and universalizes it (all presence is the not-yet, so one can never be present without being unfulfilled). But the present is the fulfilled when it is the valued — the worth is the living, and the living is the present-action from values, and the present-action from values is the fulfillment, not the not-yet; and the possible is one frame on the self, not the measure of it. The distortion makes the becoming feel like protection (I must become because I am not the possible), and the becoming prevents the very present-action that would reveal that the present was the fulfilled. The reframe: the present is the fulfilled when it is the valued — the worth is the living, and the living is not the comparing, and the possible is the frame, not the verdict. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own unfulfilled-anxiety with kindness recognizes the current-is-not-the-possible as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the current self that the becoming withheld."

2. The Not-Yet-Achieved-Is-Failure (not-yet-achieved)

Distortion: "The not-yet-achieved is the failure. The in-process is the not-arrived, the becoming is the not-yet, the not-achieved is the verdict — and the not-yet-achieved is the evidence of my failure. I must achieve, because the not-yet-achieved is the failure, and the achieved is the worth."
Reframe: "The becoming is the living — and the not-yet-achieved-is-failure distortion converts one specific form of fact (some things are not yet achieved) into a universal verdict (all not-yet-achieved is the failure, so one is always failing when one is in-process). The not-yet-achieved-is-failure distortion takes a partial truth (one has not yet achieved everything) and universalizes it (all worth requires the all-achieved, so one can never be in-process without failing). But the becoming is the living — the becoming is the engaged present-action from values, and the engaged present-action is the living, and the living is not diminished by the not-yet-arrived; and the self that is in-process in one domain is the self that is living in that domain, and the living is not diminished by the in-process. The distortion makes the achieving feel like truth (I must achieve to be ok), and the achieving prevents the very present-action that would reveal that the becoming was not the failure. The reframe: the becoming is the living — the becoming is the engaged present-action, and the engaged present-action is the living, and the failure is the distortion, not the in-process. ACT values-based present-action (Hayes 1999) is the core frame: the person who meets their own in-process with values recognizes the not-yet-achieved-is-failure as a distortion, not a verdict, and the valued-action is the fulfillment, not the not-yet-arrived."

3. The I-Must-Compare-To-The-Possible (comparison-to-possible)

Distortion: "I must compare to the possible. The not-compared is the not-known, the not-measured is the verdict — and the not-compared is the evidence of my failure. I must measure myself against the possible, because the not-compared is the unfulfilled, and the compared is the worth."
Reframe: "Comparison is not living — and the i-must-compare-to-the-possible distortion converts one specific form of awareness (some comparison can inform growth) into a universal verdict (all moments must be compared, so one is always unfulfilled when one is not measuring). The i-must-compare-to-the-possible distortion takes a partial truth (comparison can support reflection) and universalizes it (all worth requires the all-compared, so one can never have an unmeasured present without being unfulfilled). But comparison is not living — the worth is the living, and the living is the present-action from values, and the present-action from values is not the benchmarking; and the self that is present in an unmeasured action is the self that is living in that action, and the living is not diminished by the unmeasured. The distortion makes the comparing feel like control (I must compare to control the gap), and the comparing prevents the very present-action that would reveal that the living was not the benchmarking. The reframe: comparison is not living — the worth is the living, and the living is not the comparing, and the comparing is one tool, not the life. Self-determination theory competence need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the competence is built through engaged present-action, not assessed by comparison to the possible, and the engaged present-action is the competence-building, not the competence-measuring."

4. The The-Current-Is-Provisional (provisional-current)

Distortion: "The current is provisional. The present self is the not-yet-the-real-self, the now is the draft, the not-final is the verdict — and the provisional is the evidence of my failure. I must become the real self, because the current is the provisional, and the real is the worth."
Reframe: "The current is the real — and the the-current-is-provisional distortion converts one specific form of becoming (one can grow into new selves) into a universal verdict (all present-selves are the provisional, so one is always not-yet-real when one is present). The the-current-is-provisional distortion takes a partial truth (the self evolves) and universalizes it (all present-selves are the not-yet-the-real, so one can never be the current self without being the not-real). But the current is the real — the self one is now is the self one is, and the self one is is the real self, and the real self is not diminished by the not-yet-the-possible; and the self that is present in the current is the self that is real in that moment, and the real is not diminished by the becoming. The distortion makes the becoming feel like real-self-seeking (I must become the real self), and the becoming prevents the very presence that would reveal that the current was the real. The reframe: the current is the real — the self one is now is the real self, and the real self is not the not-yet, and the becoming is one expression of the real, not the replacement of it. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own current self with kindness recognizes the the-current-is-provisional as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the current self that the becoming withheld."

5. The Fulfillment-Equals-Worth (fulfillment-worth-equation)

Distortion: "Fulfillment equals worth. The not-fulfilled is the not-ok, the not-possible is the verdict — and the not-fulfilled is the evidence of my failure. I must be fulfilled, because the not-fulfilled is the not-ok, and the fulfilled is the worth."
Reframe: "Fulfillment is not the worth — and the fulfillment-equals-worth distortion converts one specific form of aspiration (one can seek fulfillment) into a universal verdict (all worth requires the fulfilled, so one is always failing when one is not fulfilled). The fulfillment-equals-worth distortion takes a partial truth (fulfillment is worth seeking) and universalizes it (all worth requires the all-fulfilled, so one can never be not-fulfilled without failing). But fulfillment is not the worth — the worth is the being and the choosing and the valuing in the present, and the fulfillment is one expression of the worth, not the condition of it; and the self that is not-fulfilled in one moment is the self that is worth in that moment, and the worth is not diminished by the not-fulfilled. The distortion makes the fulfilling feel like worth-seeking (I must be fulfilled to be ok), and the fulfilling prevents the very present-action that would reveal that the worth was not the fulfillment. The reframe: fulfillment is not the worth — the worth is the living, and the living is not the fulfilling, and the fulfilling is one orientation, not the condition. Fulfillment research is the core frame: the fulfillment is found in engaged present-action from values, not in the arrival at the possible self, and the worth is the engaged present-action, not the fulfilled-self. Self-determination theory competence need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core tool: the competence is built through engaged present-action, and the engaged present-action is the worth, and the worth is the present-action, not the fulfilled-self."

6. The I-Must-Always-Be-Becoming (constant-becoming-requirement)

Distortion: "I must always be becoming. The not-becoming is the not-living, the not-growing is the verdict — and the not-becoming is the evidence of my failure. I must always be becoming, because the not-becoming is the unfulfilled, and the becoming is the worth."
Reframe: "The becoming is the living not the demand — and the i-must-always-be-becoming distortion converts one specific form of growth (one can grow) into a universal verdict (all moments must be becoming, so one is always failing when one is not growing). The i-must-always-be-becoming distortion takes a partial truth (growth is part of living) and universalizes it (all worth requires the all-becoming, so one can never rest in the present without being unfulfilled). But the becoming is the living not the demand — the becoming is one expression of the living, and the living includes the present and the rest and the being, and the being is not diminished by the not-becoming; and the self that is present in a not-becoming moment is the self that is living in that moment, and the living is not diminished by the not-becoming. The distortion makes the becoming feel like worth-protection (I must always be becoming to be worth), and the becoming prevents the very presence that would reveal that the worth was not the becoming. The reframe: the becoming is the living not the demand — the worth is the living, and the living is not the all-becoming, and the all-becoming is the self-depletion, not the self-actualization. ACT values-based present-action (Hayes 1999) is the core frame: the valued life includes both becoming and being, and the being is not the not-becoming but the ground from which the becoming comes. Self-determination theory competence need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core tool: the competence is built through engaged present-action, and the engaged present-action can include the becoming and the being, and the being is the competence-support, not the competence-failure."

The 3-Component CBT Protocol for Fear of Being Unfulfilled

Component 1 — Assessment & Psychoeducation: Unfulfilled auditing (identify the domains where one cannot be the current self, the moments one cannot stop comparing, the selves one cannot accept as the not-yet-the-possible, the present one cannot inhabit as the real, the actions one cannot take as the fulfilled-when-valued). Psychoeducation on the fear of being unfulfilled cycle (the possible chronically invoked as the standard, or chronic reinforcement of becoming-as-worth → current-is-not-the-possible → potential-gap, not-yet-achieved, comparison-to-possible, chronic-dissatisfaction, treating-current-as-provisional, fulfillment-worth-equation → short-term safety → safety reinforces fear → lose the experience of presence and the capacity for being the current self → chronic self-actualization-anxiety and inability to be the current self → current-is-not-the-possible conviction deepens → repeat), the fulfillment research (fulfillment is found in engaged present-action from values, not in the arrival at the possible self), and the key insight that fear of being unfulfilled is the emotional root of self-actualization-anxiety and inability to be the current self (the current-is-not-the-possible distortion makes the present the deficit, the fulfillment-equals-worth distortion makes the becoming the worth). Introduce the ACT values-based present-action approach (Hayes 1999), the self-determination theory competence-need application (Deci & Ryan 2000), the fulfillment-oriented practice, and the self-compassion practice (Neff 2003).

Component 2 — Cognitive Restructuring & Skill Building: Identify the 6 cognitive distortions (current-is-not-the-possible, not-yet-achieved-is-failure, i-must-compare-to-the-possible, the-current-is-provisional, fulfillment-equals-worth, i-must-always-be-becoming) and build balanced beliefs. Practice present-action-is-the-fulfilled-action clarification ('The present action is the fulfilled action when it is the valued action; the possible is one frame, not the measure, and the present-action from values is the fulfillment, not the not-yet') to break the potential-gap. Apply the values-based-present-action reframe (the fulfilled life is the one lived in valued-action in the present, not the one lived in the comparison to the possible for worth). Practice competence-need restoration (Deci & Ryan 2000: the competence is built through engaged present-action, not assessed by comparison to the possible; 'Am I acting from values in this present or demanding the possible to be ok' rather than 'Must I become to be ok'). Rehearse self-compassion responses (Neff 2003) for unfulfilled-self scenarios ('This self-actualization-anxiety is hard, and my fear is worthy of care; the worth is the living not the fulfilling, and the kindness I offer myself is the permission to be the current self that the becoming withheld'). Distinguish the becoming that is genuinely serving values from the comparing that is the potential-gap-demand (the first is chosen, the second is driven).

Component 3 — Behavioral Practice & Maintenance: Practice one present-action per day that intentionally is the current self in a context where the becoming is the default (one action taken as the current self, one present inhabited without comparing, one choice made without the demand for the possible, one activity done as the real self), starting with low-stakes (one present-action, one safe domain) and escalating to higher-stakes (a significant action taken as the current self, a domain where the comparing is the norm, a present inhabited without the demand for the possible). Track the outcome (the present-action practiced, one's own self-actualization-anxiety during it, the unfulfilled-fear that fired, the distortion that maintained the fear, whether the present-action revealed that the present was the fulfilled-when-valued and whether the practicing shrank the current-is-not-the-possible conviction). Process the discomfort of the presence (the fear that fires is the potential-gap distortion, not a signal the presence was wrong). Apply self-compassion (Neff 2003) when the fear persists (kindness to the self-actualization-anxiety, not evidence of failure). Use ACT values-based present-action (Hayes 1999) to maintain the practice through the fear ('the present-action I take is the fulfilled-action, not the not-yet-action, and the fulfilled is the acting from values, not the arriving at the possible'). Use fulfillment research to shift from comparing to engaged present-action: the fulfilled life is the engaged present-action from values, and the engagement requires the willingness to be the current self first. Use self-determination theory competence need (Deci & Ryan 2000) to restore the competence through the present-action: the present-action builds the competence, and the competence is the engaged present-action, not the arrived-self. Generalize to higher-stakes presence as the capacity to act from values strengthens and the current-is-not-the-possible conviction loosens.

If you are in crisis

If fear of being unfulfilled is part of a pattern that includes self-harm, suicidal thoughts, or an inability to function — if the self-actualization-anxiety is so total that you cannot access any presence, or if the chronic unfulfilled-fear is leading to thoughts of harming yourself — this is not a self-help problem, it is a crisis. Frequently Asked Questions

Is fear of being unfulfilled the same as fear of wasting life?

Fear of being unfulfilled and fear of wasting life are related but not identical. Fear of wasting life is the conviction that the life one is living is not the life one could be living and the gap is the waste — the anticipation that the present is the not-enough and the not-enough is the evidence of a wasted life, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is living the optimal life. Fear of being unfulfilled is the conviction that the current self is not the possible self and the gap is the failure — the anticipation that the present is the not-yet and the not-yet is the evidence of an unfulfilled life, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is the possible self. The overlap is substantial: the unfulfilled-fearing self may fear wasting life (the unfulfilled self is the wasted life, so the only safety is in the fulfilled), and the wasting-life-fearing self can fear being unfulfilled (the wasted life includes the unfulfilled self, so the only safety is in the never-unfulfilled). But the primary distortion differs: fear of wasting life's distortion is about the life as a whole (is this life the optimal life), fear of being unfulfilled's distortion is about the self in the present (is this self the possible self). A person can fear wasting life without dominant unfulfilled-fear (anxious about the life as a whole while being basically able to accept their current self as not-unfulfilled), and a person can fear being unfulfilled without dominant wasting-life-fear (unable to accept their current self as not-unfulfilled while being basically unbothered by the life as a whole). In practice, the two patterns often co-occur, and CBT for one will often address the other. The self-compassion (Neff 2003), ACT values-based present-action (Hayes 1999), and fulfillment research tools on this page serve both patterns: they build the capacity to be the current self (fear of being unfulfilled) and the capacity to live the present life (fear of wasting life), because both capacities come from the same reframe: the worth is the living in the present, not the arriving at the possible.

How is fear of being unfulfilled different from fear of not being enough?

Fear of being unfulfilled and fear of not being enough overlap but differ in focus. Fear of not being enough is the conviction that one is inadequate as a person — the anticipation that the self is the not-enough and the not-enough is the verdict, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is the enough. Fear of being unfulfilled is the conviction that the current self is not the possible self and the gap is the failure — the anticipation that the present is the not-yet and the not-yet is the evidence of an unfulfilled life, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is the possible self. Fear of not being enough is about person-level adequacy (am I enough as a person); fear of being unfulfilled is about self-vs-possible-self gap (am I the self I could be). A person can fear not being enough without dominant unfulfilled-fear (anxious about being inadequate as a person while being basically able to accept their current self as not-unfulfilled relative to the possible), and a person can fear being unfulfilled without dominant not-enough-fear (unable to accept their current self as not-unfulfilled while being basically ok with their person-level adequacy). In practice, the two patterns can interact (the unfulfilled-fearing self may fear not being enough — the not-possible self is the not-enough, so the only being-ok is the fulfilled, and the not-enough-fearing self may fear being unfulfilled — the not-enough self is the not-possible, so the being-ok is the enough-which-is-the-fulfilled), and the CBT tools on this page address the underlying self-actualization pattern. The self-determination theory competence need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the unifying frame: both patterns involve a threat to the competence (the unfulfilled self and the not-enough self both threaten the competence), and the restoration — through engaged present-action from values rather than comparison to the possible or demand for the enough — is the path through both.

What if I really do want to grow and become — isn't some self-actualization appropriate?

Yes — and this is a crucial nuance. Fear of being unfulfilled is not the claim that all growth is bad or that one should never become. The fulfilled life is the becoming life for many people, and the becoming that serves one's values is the valued-action, not the potential-gap-driven becoming. The diagnostic question is not 'am I becoming' but 'am I acting from values or from the fear of being unfulfilled'. The becoming that comes from the current-is-not-the-possible distortion has a specific signature: it is driven (the person cannot be the current self, the present produces anxiety, the now feels like a deficit), not chosen (the person could be the current self, the becoming is welcome, the present is a gift). The becoming that serves values is chosen and can include presence; the becoming that serves the potential-gap distortion is driven and cannot include presence. The ACT values-based present-action (Hayes 1999) framework makes this explicit: the valued life includes both becoming and presence, and the presence is not the not-becoming but the ground from which the becoming comes. The goal of CBT for fear of being unfulfilled is not to eliminate becoming but to liberate it — to move from becoming that is driven by the fear of being unfulfilled (and therefore cannot pause, and therefore anxiously compares, and therefore is unsustainable) to becoming that is chosen from values (and therefore can pause, and therefore is sustainable, and therefore is more effective). The self-determination theory competence need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the competence is built through engaged present-action, and the engaged present-action can include the becoming and the being, and the being is the competence-support, not the competence-failure.

What does CBT for Fear of Being Unfulfilled involve and does it work?

CBT for Fear of Being Unfulfilled involves present-action-is-the-fulfilled-action clarification, cognitive restructuring of the unfulfilled distortions, ACT values-based present-action (Hayes 1999), self-determination theory competence-need application (Deci & Ryan 2000), fulfillment-oriented practice, and self-compassion practice (Neff 2003). The core components: (1) present-action-is-the-fulfilled-action clarification — the current-is-not-the-possible distortion treats every present-action as the not-yet and every not-fulfilled action as the failure; the first step is clarifying that the present action is the fulfilled action when it is the valued action (the possible is one frame, not the measure, and the present-action from values is the fulfillment, not the not-yet), and recognizing that the self that can act from values in the present is the self that is fulfilled, not the self that is unfulfilled; (2) cognitive restructuring — identifying the 6 cognitive distortions (current-is-not-the-possible, not-yet-achieved-is-failure, i-must-compare-to-the-possible, the-current-is-provisional, fulfillment-equals-worth, i-must-always-be-becoming) and building balanced beliefs (the present is the fulfilled when it is the valued, the becoming is the living, comparison is not living, the current is the real, fulfillment is not the worth, and the becoming is the living not the demand); (3) ACT values-based present-action — the Hayes (1999) approach: the fulfilled life is the one lived in valued-action in the present, not the one lived in the comparison to the possible for worth; acting from one's values includes the willingness to be this self in this moment, and the present-action is the fulfilled-action, not the not-yet-action. Yes, CBT for fear of being unfulfilled works — the cognitive distortions are well-established in the CBT literature, the ACT values-based present-action approach has strong empirical support for self-actualization and existential patterns, and the fulfillment research confirms that fulfillment is found in engaged present-action from values, not in the comparison to the possible self.
