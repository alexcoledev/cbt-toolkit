Fear of being an imitation — the conviction that the original self is dangerous and the imitation is the safety, the anticipation that originality will be punished or lead to harm, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is imitation-avoidant-of-originality and the copying is the protection — is one of the most pervasive and painful emotional patterns in imitation-conditioned culture. It is the emotional root that drives self-originality-avoidance (the substitution of a copy for one's own originality when originality is available, the inability to inhabit one's own originality, the conversion of every act of originality into a harm), anxiety when originality is available (the originality itself produces dread, the originality is the trigger, the available originality is the warning signal), treating originality as dangerous/harmful (the person cannot allow the originality because the originality is the not-yet-the-rejection, the person cannot inhabit the true self because the true self is the not-yet-the-rejected), equating imitation with safety (the belief that copying is protection and originality is danger, the framing of the imitation as the worth and the originality as the threat), and chronic unoriginality (the persistent substitution of imitation for originality to avoid the originality of originality, the inability to be original because the original is the endangered). It is distinct from fear of being different (which is about the social dimension — standing out from the group) and from fear of being authentic (which is about real self vs performance). Fear of being an imitation is about the creative dimension: the self that cannot rest in originality because the originality is the danger, the self that experiences its own creativity as a vulnerability of harm, the self that treats every original act as a derivative and every originality as the not-yet-the-rejected.

Fear of being an imitation is the emotional root of three downstream patterns. The originality-is-dangerous distortion makes every act of originality a harm and every originality a vulnerability, producing self-originality-avoidance and anxiety-when-originality-is-available (the person concludes the only safety is in the imitation and the imitation requires the not-being-original, so the originality is refused and the originality is never exercised because the originality is always the not-yet-the-rejected). The imitation-is-safety distortion makes the copying the protection and the originality the danger, producing the chronic devaluation of originality and the equation of imitation with worth (the person experiences the originality and the originality is the dangerous, and the dangerous is the not-safe, and the not-safe is the threat the imitation was meant to prevent). And the creativity-is-vulnerability distortion makes the act-of-originality the impending-rejection and the originality the warning, producing chronic-unoriginality and the constant-avoidance of originality-requirement (the person has originality and the originality is the harm, and the harm is the consequence, and the consequence is the rejection the imitation was meant to prevent). Fear of being an imitation is the upstream cause; self-originality-avoidance, anxiety-when-originality-is-available, and chronic-unoriginality are the downstream effects. The key insight for fear of being an imitation: originality is not the danger the imitation protects from — the self that can allow its own originality is the self that is whole, not the self that is dangerous, and the safety is not the imitation but the living, and the living is the being, not the copying. The creativity research (Kaufman & Beghetto 2012) provides the deeper frame: original living is associated with greater well-being, lower anxiety, and more fulfilling creative engagement — the self that allows originality is the self that is whole, not the self that is endangered.

The Fear of Being an Imitation Cycle (How It Develops)

The fear of being an imitation cycle is the self-reinforcing loop that produces the self-originality-avoidance, the anxiety-when-originality-is-available, and the chronic-unoriginality that come from concluding that the original self is dangerous and the imitation is the safety:

A situation with originality chronically followed by rejection, or chronic reinforcement of imitation-as-safety (an early experience where being original was criticized, where the true creation was shamed, where the self-expression preceded the harm, a chronic reinforcement of copying where one's sense of value was entirely defined by their imitation and never by their originality) → originality-is-dangerous fires (the originality is the danger, the creativity is the vulnerability, the originality is the warning, the originality is the not-yet-the-rejected) → self-originality-avoidance, anxiety-when-originality-is-available, creativity-is-vulnerability, imitation-is-safety, chronic-unoriginality (the substitution of a copy for one's own originality, the inability to inhabit originality, the constant avoidance of originality, the devaluation of originality, the constant imitation) → short-term safety achieved (the imitation feels like protection from the rejection, the not-being-original feels like the control over the outcome, the copying feels like the safety) → the safety reinforces the fear (the imitation prevents the living that would reveal that the originality was not the danger, the not-being-original prevents the evidence that would disconfirm the originality-is-dangerous conviction, the copying-as-safety prevents the originality that would reveal that the originality was not the danger) → the person loses the experience of originality and the capacity for inhabiting creativity (the originality that comes from the allowing is never experienced, because the allowing is always the not-yet-the-safe; the originality that comes from the being-original is never experienced, because the being-original is always the not-yet-the-rejected) → chronic unoriginality and inability to inhabit originality → originality-is-dangerous conviction deepens → repeat

The cycle is driven by the short-term safety that imitation and not-being-original provides (imitation-because-the-imitation-is-the-protection-from-the-rejection and not-being-original-because-the-not-being-original-is-the-control-over-the-outcome both feel like they are preventing the rejection, and the feeling reduces the fear of being an imitation temporarily) and maintained by the long-term cost of the originality-is-dangerous pattern (the originality is never experienced, because the originality is always the not-yet-the-safe; the originality is never exercised for the self, because the being is always the not-yet-the-rejected; and the originality-is-dangerous conviction deepens because every prevented originality is treated as evidence that the originality was the danger). The tragic irony of fear of being an imitation is that the safety strategy (imitation, not-being-original, copying-as-safety) produces the very outcome it was meant to prevent (the person refuses the originality and the refusing-the-originality produces the very harm the imitation was meant to disprove, and the chronic not-allowing-originality is the loss of the creation, not the protection from the rejection — the fear of being an imitation prevents the very originality that would have made the life worth the living). The CBT intervention breaks the cycle at two points: (1) originality-tolerance-clarification — when the originality-is-dangerous distortion treats every act of originality as a harm, the first step is clarifying that originality is a capacity one can exercise without provoking harm, and recognizing that the self that can allow its own originality is the self that is whole, not the self that is dangerous; (2) values-based-committed-action — when the imitation-is-safety distortion fires, one challenges it with the ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999): the valued life includes the willingness to be original, and the committed action is the living that exercises the full range of original self-expression.

The 6 Cognitive Distortions & Their Reframes

1. The Originality-Is-Dangerous (self-originality-avoidance)

Distortion: "Originality is dangerous. If I allow myself to be original, something bad will happen. Creativity is a vulnerability of harm, a setup for the rejection. I must avoid originality myself, because the originality is the not-yet-the-rejected, and the imitation is the safety."
Reframe: "Originality is a capacity not a vulnerability — and the originality-is-dangerous distortion converts one specific form of experience (sometimes originality is followed by consequence) into a universal verdict (all originality is the harm, so one is always provoking rejection when one is original). The originality-is-dangerous distortion takes a partial truth (consequences happen) and universalizes it (all originality causes harm, so one can never be original without endangering oneself). But originality is a capacity not a vulnerability — the act of originality is the act of originality, not the cause of the next rejection; and the self that is original in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not imitation-avoidant by the originality. The distortion makes the imitation feel like protection (I must avoid originality myself because originality is dangerous), and the imitation prevents the very originality that would reveal that the originality was not the danger. The reframe: originality is a capacity not a vulnerability — the safety is the allowing, and the allowing is not the not-copying originality, and the not-copying originality is the living, not the endangering. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own originality-fear with kindness recognizes the originality-is-dangerous as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the original self that the imitation withheld."

2. The Imitation-Is-Safety (treating originality as danger)

Distortion: "Imitation equals safety. Self-avoidance of originality is protection, originality is danger — and the originality is the evidence of my dangerousness. I must be a copy, because the safety is the safety, and the originality is the not-safe."
Reframe: "Imitation is one option not safety — and the imitation-is-safety distortion converts one specific form of experience (some copying protects) into a universal verdict (all imitation is safety, so one is always endangered when one is original). The imitation-is-safety distortion takes a partial truth (some avoidance of originality is protective) and universalizes it (all imitation is safety, so one can never be original without being endangered). But imitation is one option not safety — the self that is imitation-avoidant is not automatically the self that is safe, and the self that is original is not automatically the self that is endangered; and the safety is not the avoidance of originality but the living, and the living includes both the chosen imitation and the chosen originality. The distortion makes the safety feel like safety (I must be a copy to be safe), and the safety prevents the very originality that would reveal that the originality was not the danger. The reframe: imitation is one option not safety — the safety is the living of the full range, and the full range includes the originality, and the originality is not the not-safe but one expression of the safe. Creativity research (Kaufman & Beghetto 2012) is the core frame: original living is associated with greater well-being, and the original self is the whole self, not the endangered self."

3. The Creativity-Is-Trap (anxiety when originality is available)

Distortion: "If I have originality, something bad will happen. The act-of-originality is the impending-rejection, the originality is the warning — and the originality is the evidence of my upcoming rejection. I must not act on originality, because the acting is the not-yet-the-rejected."
Reframe: "Creativity is contribution not harm — and the creativity-is-vulnerability distortion converts one specific form of experience (sometimes being-original leads to consequence) into a universal verdict (all originality is harm, so one is always about to be rejected when one has originality). The creativity-is-vulnerability distortion takes a partial truth (originality has consequences) and universalizes it (all originality causes rejection, so one can never have originality without being about to be rejected). But creativity is contribution not trap — the act of originality is not the cause of the next rejection; and the self that is present for originality in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not the not-yet-the-rejected. The distortion makes the not-being-original feel like control (I must not act to control the outcome), and the not-being-original prevents the very presence that would reveal that the originality was not the harm. The reframe: creativity is contribution not trap — the present is the present, and the originality is the originality, and the consequence is not caused by the having-of-originality. ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) is the core frame: the valued life includes the willingness to be original without demanding the guarantee of the outcome, and the committed action is the being-original, not the outcome-controlling."

4. The Originality-Is-Danger (treating originality as derivative)

Distortion: "Originality is derivative. Self-direction is selfishness, originality is harm — and the original choice is the evidence of my unworthiness. I must be a copy, because the safety is the imitation, and the self-expression is the not-ok."
Reframe: "Originality is originality not derivative — and the copying-is-protection distortion converts one specific form of fact (some originality is derivative) into a universal verdict (all self-expression is derivative, so one is always unworthy when one is originally choosing). The copying-is-protection distortion takes a partial truth (some originality is derivative) and universalizes it (all originality requires the safety, so one can never be original without being derivative). But originality is safety not danger — the self that is original is the self that is original, and the originality is the choosing, not the avoiding originality; and the self that is original in one moment is the self that is ok in that moment, and the ok is not imitation-avoidant by the self-expression. The distortion makes the safety feel like imitation (I must be a copy to be original), and the safety prevents the very originality that would reveal that the originality was not the derivative. The reframe: originality is safety not danger — the originality is the choosing, and the choosing is not the avoiding originality, and the avoiding originality is one path, not the condition. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own originality with kindness recognizes the copying-is-protection as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the original self that the imitation withheld."

5. The I-Must-Be-A-Copy (identity distortion)

Distortion: "I must be a copy. My worth is in my imitation, my identity is in my copy — and the original self is the not-me. I must not act on originality, because the originality is the not-yet-the-self, and the acting is the becoming-the-not-ok."
Reframe: "The self can be original — and the i-must-be-a-copy distortion converts one specific form of fact (some copies are genuine) into a universal verdict (all self is copy, so one is always the not-self when one is original). The i-must-be-a-copy distortion takes a partial truth (the self has copies) and universalizes it (all identity is imitation, so one can never be original without being the not-self). But the self can be original — the self that is original is the self that is whole, and the wholeness is the living, not the avoiding originality; and the self that is original in one moment is the self that is original in that moment, and the original is not negated by the originality. The distortion makes the avoiding originality feel like identity (I must be a copy to belong), and the avoiding originality prevents the very originality that would reveal that the self was not the imitation. The reframe: the self can be original — the identity is the living, and the living is not the avoiding originality, and the avoiding originality is one feature, not the verdict. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the originality is the living of the self, and the living includes the originality, and the originality is the originality-expression, not the originality-failure."

6. The Originality-Means-Derivative (safety distortion)

Distortion: "Copied means role. Copying is the worth, self-imitation is the safety — and the originality is the evidence of my dangerousness. I must not be original, because the originality is the danger, and the avoiding originality is the protection."
Reframe: "Identity is one's own not copy — and the originality-means-derivative distortion converts one specific form of fact (some imitation is protective) into a universal verdict (all safety is imitation, so one is always endangered when one is original). The originality-means-derivative distortion takes a partial truth (imitation can protect) and universalizes it (all copying is safety, so one can never be original without being dangerous). But identity is one's own not copy — the self that is original is the self that is whole, and the originality is the ground for the genuine creation, not the end of it; and the self that is original in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not the not-protected. The distortion makes the not-original feel like safety (I must not be original to be safe), and the not-original prevents the very originality that would reveal that the originality was not the danger. The reframe: identity is one's own not copy — the safety is the living, and the living includes the originality, and the originality is the safety-support, not the safety-failure. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the originality is the living of the full range, and the full range includes the originality and the imitation, and the originality is the originality-expression, not the originality-failure."

The 3-Component CBT Protocol for Fear of Being an Imitation

Component 1 — Assessment & Psychoeducation: Imitation-avoidance auditing (identify the domains where one cannot inhabit originality, the moments one substitutes a copy for one's own originality when originality is available, the originalities one cannot allow, the originality one cannot exercise, the self-expression one cannot take). Psychoeducation on the fear of being an imitation cycle (originality chronically followed by rejection, or chronic reinforcement of imitation-as-safety → originality-is-dangerous → self-originality-avoidance, anxiety-when-originality-is-available, creativity-is-vulnerability, imitation-is-safety, chronic-unoriginality → short-term safety → safety reinforces fear → lose the experience of originality and the capacity for inhabiting creativity → chronic unoriginality and inability to inhabit originality → originality-is-dangerous conviction deepens → repeat), the creativity research (Kaufman & Beghetto 2012: original living is associated with greater well-being, lower anxiety, and more fulfilling creative engagement), and the key insight that fear of being an imitation is the emotional root of copying and inability to inhabit originality (the originality-is-dangerous distortion makes the originality the vulnerability, the imitation-is-safety distortion makes the avoidance of originality the protection). Introduce the ACT values-based committed-action approach (Hayes 1999), the self-determination theory autonomy-need application (Deci & Ryan 2000), the originality-oriented practice, and the self-compassion practice (Neff 2003).

Component 2 — Cognitive Restructuring & Skill Building: Identify the 6 cognitive distortions (originality-is-dangerous, imitation-is-safety, creativity-is-vulnerability, copying-is-protection, i-must-be-a-copy, originality-means-derivative) and build balanced beliefs. Practice originality-tolerance clarification ('Originality is a capacity I can exercise without provoking harm; the act of originality is the act of originality, not the cause of the next rejection, and the self that can allow its own originality is the self that is whole, not the self that is dangerous') to break the self-originality-avoidance. Apply the values-based-committed-action reframe (the valued life includes the willingness to be original, and the committed action is the living that exercises the full range of original self-expression). Practice autonomy-need restoration (Deci & Ryan 2000: the originality is the living, not the harming; 'Am I allowing my own originality or avoiding originality against the not-yet-the-rejected' rather than 'Must I be a copy to be ok'). Rehearse self-compassion responses (Neff 2003) for originality-fear scenarios ('This imitation-avoidance is hard, and my fear is worthy of care; the safety is the living not the avoiding originality, and the kindness I offer myself is the permission to be the original self that the imitation withheld'). Distinguish the imitation that is genuinely serving values from the not-being-original that is the originality-avoidance-demand (the first is chosen, the second is driven).

Component 3 — Behavioral Practice & Maintenance: Practice one originality-allowance per day that intentionally is the inhabiting-of-originality in a context where the imitation is the default (one act of originality made without dread, one originality exercised without self-imitation, one originality inhabited without the demand for the copying, one self-expression taken without the demand for the safety), starting with low-stakes (one small originality, one safe domain) and escalating to higher-stakes (a significant originality exercised without avoiding originality, a domain where the not-being-original is the norm, an originality inhabited without the demand for the guarantee). Track the outcome (the originality practiced, one's own imitation-avoidance during it, the dread that fired, the distortion that maintained the fear, whether the originality revealed that the originality was not the danger and whether the practicing shrank the originality-is-dangerous conviction). Process the discomfort of the originality (the fear that fires is the creativity-is-vulnerability distortion, not a signal the originality was wrong). Apply self-compassion (Neff 2003) when the fear persists (kindness to the imitation-avoidance, not evidence of failure). Use ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) to maintain the practice through the fear ('the originality I allow is the living, not the not-copying originality, and the meaning is the allowing, not the not-fearing'). Use creativity research (Kaufman & Beghetto 2012) to shift from avoiding originality to allowing: original living is associated with greater well-being, and the allowing requires the willingness to be the original self first. Use self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) to restore the originality through the originality: the originality builds the originality, and the originality is the living, not the imitation-avoidant-self. Generalize to higher-stakes domains as the originality-is-dangerous conviction shrinks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is fear of being an imitation the same as fear of being different?

Fear of being an imitation and fear of being different are related but not identical. Fear of being different is the conviction that the different self is dangerous and the conformity is the safety — the anticipation that difference will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is conformity-avoidant-of-individuality. Fear of being an imitation is the conviction that the original self is dangerous and the imitation is the safety — the anticipation that originality will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is imitation-avoidant-of-originality. The overlap is substantial: the different-fearing self may fear being an imitation (the originality is the different, and the different is the dangerous, so the only safety is in the imitation), and the imitation-fearing self can fear being different (the difference might require the originality, and the originality is the vulnerability, so the only safety is in the copying). But the primary distortion differs: fear of being different's distortion is about the social dimension as threat (is standing out from the group safe), fear of being an imitation's distortion is about the creative dimension as threat (is originality safe). A person can fear being different without dominant imitation-fear (anxious about standing out while being basically able to rest in chosen originality), and a person can fear being an imitation without dominant difference-fear (unable to rest in originality while being basically unbothered by standing out in domains that don't produce originality). In practice, the two patterns often co-occur, and CBT for one will often address the other. The self-compassion (Neff 2003), ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999), and creativity research (Kaufman & Beghetto 2012) tools on this page serve both patterns: they build the capacity to allow originality (fear of being an imitation) and the capacity to allow the difference (fear of being different) through the same allowing-of-the-full-range-of-experience frame.

How is fear of being an imitation different from fear of being authentic?

Fear of being an imitation and fear of being authentic overlap but differ in focus. Fear of being authentic is the conviction that the real self is dangerous and the performance is the safety — the anticipation that authenticity will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is performance-avoidant. Fear of being an imitation is the conviction that the original self is dangerous and the imitation is the safety — the anticipation that originality will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is imitation-avoidant-of-originality. Fear of being authentic is about real self vs performance as threat (is authenticity safe); fear of being an imitation is about originality as threat (is originality safe). A person can fear being authentic without dominant imitation-fear (anxious about real-self-vs-performance while being basically able to rest in chosen originality), and a person can fear being an imitation without dominant authenticity-fear (unable to rest in originality while being basically ok with authenticity in domains that don't produce originality). In practice, the two patterns can interact (the authentic-fearing self may fear being an imitation — the originality might reveal the authenticity, and the authenticity is the vulnerability, so the only safety is in the imitation; and the imitation-fearing self may fear being authentic — the authenticity is the original, and the original is the dangerous), and the CBT tools on this page address the underlying originality-avoidance pattern. The self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the unifying frame: both patterns involve a threat to the self's acceptability (the authentic self and the original self both threaten the performed-self), and the restoration — through acceptance of the full range of experience rather than demand for the self to be performed — is the core intervention.

What if I really do need imitation — isn't some copying appropriate?

Yes — and this is a crucial nuance. Fear of being an imitation is not the claim that all imitation is bad or that one should never adopt a copy. The performed self is the self that chooses many forms of imitation, and the imitation that serves one's values is the valued-action, not the originality-avoidance-driven avoiding originality. The diagnostic question is not 'am I being performed' but 'am I living my life or avoiding originality against the not-yet-the-rejected'. The avoiding originality that comes from the originality-is-dangerous distortion has a specific signature: it is driven (the person cannot inhabit originality, the originality produces dread, the originality feels like a harm), not chosen (the person could inhabit originality, the imitation is welcome, the originality is a gift). The imitation that serves values is chosen and can include originality; the avoiding originality that serves the originality-avoidance distortion is driven and cannot include originality. The ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) framework makes this explicit: the valued life includes both imitation and originality, and the originality is not the not-safety but the ground from which the imitation comes. The goal of CBT for fear of being an imitation is not to eliminate imitation but to liberate it — to move from avoiding originality that is driven by the fear of being an imitation (and therefore cannot pause, and therefore anxiously guards against originality, and therefore is unsustainable) to imitation that is chosen from values (and therefore can pause, and therefore is sustainable, and therefore is more effective). The self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the originality is the living of the full range, and the full range includes the imitation and the originality, and the originality is the originality-support, not the originality-failure.

What does CBT for Fear of Being an Imitation involve and does it work?

CBT for Fear of Being an Imitation involves originality-tolerance clarification, cognitive restructuring of the imitation-avoidance distortions, ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999), self-determination theory autonomy-need application (Deci & Ryan 2000), originality-oriented practice, and self-compassion practice (Neff 2003). The core components: (1) originality-tolerance clarification — the originality-is-dangerous distortion treats every act of originality as a harm; the first step is clarifying that originality is a capacity one can exercise without provoking harm, and recognizing that the self that can allow its own originality is the self that is whole, not the self that is dangerous; (2) cognitive restructuring — identifying the 6 cognitive distortions (originality-is-dangerous, imitation-is-safety, creativity-is-vulnerability, copying-is-protection, i-must-be-a-copy, originality-means-derivative) and building balanced beliefs (originality is a capacity not a vulnerability, imitation is one option not safety, creativity is contribution not trap, originality is safety not danger, the self can be original, and identity is one's own not copy); (3) ACT values-based committed-action — the Hayes (1999) approach: the valued life includes the willingness to be original, and the committed action is the living that exercises the full range of original self-expression. Yes, CBT for fear of being an imitation works — the cognitive distortions are well-established in the CBT literature, the ACT values-based committed-action approach has strong empirical support for experiential avoidance patterns, self-compassion (Neff 2003) is empirically supported for reducing the self-criticism that maintains the imitation-avoidance, and creativity research (Kaufman & Beghetto 2012) documents that original living is associated with greater well-being and lower anxiety.
