Fear of being a snarl — the conviction that untangling is dangerous and snarl is safety, the anticipation that being the self that has untangling will be punished or lead to harm, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is snarl-tolerant-of-concealment-snarl and the snarl is the protection — is one of the most pervasive and painful emotional patterns in concealment-snarl-oriented-conditioned culture. It is the emotional root that drives self-concealment-snarl-avoidance (the substitution of snarl for untangling when untangling is available, the inability to be the untangling that is not also the snarl, the conversion of every act of untangling into a harm), anxiety when untangling is available (the untangling itself produces dread, the untangling is the trigger, the available untangling is the warning signal), treating untangling as dangerous/harmful (the person cannot allow the untangling because the untangling is the not-yet-the-loosening, the person cannot be the untangling-that-is-not-also-the-snarl because the being-untangling is the not-yet-the-untangling), equating snarl with safety (the belief that snarl is protection and untangling is danger, the framing of the snarl as the worth and the untangling as the threat), and chronic self-as-snarl (the persistent substitution of snarl for untangling to avoid the untangling of untangling, the inability to be the untangling because the untangling is the endangered). It is distinct from fear of being an enigma (which is about the concealment-snarl dimension — the self as the worn-not-the-wearer, the self that conceals but is not the concealer) and from fear of being a cocoon (which is about the transformation dimension — the self as wrapping-not-the-transformed). Fear of being a snarl is about the concealment-snarl dimension: the self that is only a snarl, the self that has no untangling, the self that is the snarl not the untangling, the self that is the snarl not the snarling, the self that cannot rest in untangling because the untangling is the danger, the self that experiences its own untangling as a weakness of loosening, the self that treats every act of untangling as a threat and every untangling as the not-yet-the-untangling.

Fear of being a snarl is the emotional root of three downstream patterns. The i-am-a-snarl distortion makes every act of untangling a harm and every untangling a weakness, producing self-concealment-snarl-avoidance and anxiety-when-untangling-is-available (the person concludes the only safety is in the snarl and the snarl requires the not-being-the-untangling-that-is-not-also-the-concealment-snarl, so the untangling is refused and the untangling is never exercised because the untangling is always the not-yet-the-untangling). The snarl-is-protection distortion makes the snarl the protection and the untangling the danger, producing the chronic devaluation of untangling and the equation of snarl with worth (the person experiences the untangling and the untangling is the dangerous, and the dangerous is the not-safe, and the not-safe is the threat the snarl was meant to prevent). And the untangling-is-dangerous distortion makes the act-of-untangling the impending-loosening and the untangling the warning, producing chronic-self-as-snarl and the constant-avoidance of untangling-requirement (the person has untangling and the untangling is the harm, and the harm is the consequence, and the consequence is the loosening the snarl was meant to prevent). Fear of being a snarl is the upstream cause; self-concealment-snarl-avoidance, anxiety-when-untangling-is-available, and chronic-self-as-snarl are the downstream effects. The key insight for fear of being a snarl: untangling is not the danger the snarl protects from — the self that can allow its own untangling is the self that is untangling, not the self that is dangerous, and the safety is not the snarl but the untangling, and the untangling is the being-untangling, not the snarl. Concealment-snarl self research provides the deeper frame: the snarl self is a position one can move from, not a permanent condition one is condemned to, and the self that can be the untangling is the self that is filled, not the self that is endangered.

The Fear of Being a Snarl Cycle (How It Develops)

The fear of being a snarl cycle is the self-reinforcing loop that produces the self-concealment-snarl-avoidance, the anxiety-when-untangling-is-available, and the chronic-self-as-snarl that come from concluding that untangling is dangerous and snarl is safety:

A situation with untangling chronically followed by punishment, or chronic reinforcement of concealment-snarl-as-safety (an early experience where being the untangling that had untangling was criticized, where the untangling was shamed, where the untangling preceded the harm, a chronic reinforcement of snarl where one's sense of value was entirely defined by their capacity to be the snarl and never by their capacity to be the untangling) → i-am-a-snarl fires (the untangling is the danger, the untangling is the weakness, the untangling is the warning, the untangling is the not-yet-the-untangling) → self-concealment-snarl-avoidance, anxiety-when-untangling-is-available, untangling-is-dangerous, snarl-is-protection, chronic-self-as-snarl (the substitution of snarl for untangling, the inability to be the untangling that is not also the snarl, the constant avoidance of untangling, the devaluation of untangling, the constant snarl-as-safety) → short-term safety achieved (the snarl feels like protection from the loosening, the not-being-the-untangling-that-is-not-also-the-concealment-snarl feels like the control over the outcome, the snarl feels like the safety) → the safety reinforces the fear (the snarl prevents the untangling that would decode that the untangling was not the danger, the not-untangling prevents the evidence that would disconfirm the i-am-a-snarl conviction, the concealment-snarl-as-safety prevents the untangling that would decode that the untangling was not the danger) → the person loses the experience of untangling and the capacity for building untangling (the untangling that comes from the allowing is never experienced, because the allowing is always the not-yet-the-untangling; the untangling that comes from the being-the-untangling-that-is-not-also-the-snarl is never exercised, because the being-untangling is always the not-yet-the-untangling) → chronic self-as-snarl and inability to be the untangling → i-am-a-snarl conviction deepens → repeat

The cycle is driven by the short-term safety that snarl and not-untangling provides (snarl-because-the-snarl-is-the-protection-from-the-loosening and not-untangling-because-the-not-untangling-is-the-control-over-the-outcome both feel like they are preventing the loosening, and the feeling reduces the fear of being a snarl temporarily) and maintained by the long-term cost of the i-am-a-snarl pattern (the untangling is never experienced, because the untangling is always the not-yet-the-untangling; the untangling is never exercised for the self, because the being-untangling is always the not-yet-the-untangling; and the i-am-a-snarl conviction deepens because every prevented untangling is treated as evidence that the untangling was the danger). The tragic irony of fear of being a snarl is that the safety strategy (snarl, not-untangling, concealment-snarl-as-safety) produces the very outcome it was meant to prevent (the person refuses the untangling and the refusing-the-untangling produces the very harm the snarl was meant to disprove, and the chronic not-allowing-untangling is the loss of the untangling, not the protection from the loosening — the fear of being a snarl prevents the very untangling that would have made the life worth the untangling). The CBT intervention breaks the cycle at two points: (1) untangling-tolerance-clarification — when the i-am-a-snarl distortion treats every act of untangling as a harm, the first step is clarifying that untangling is a capacity one can exercise without provoking harm, and recognizing that the self that can allow its own untangling is the self that is untangling, not the self that is dangerous; (2) values-based-committed-action — when the snarl-is-protection distortion fires, one challenges it with the ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999): the valued life includes the willingness to be the self that has untangling, and the committed action is the being-untangling that exercises the full range of untangling.

The 6 Cognitive Distortions & Their Reframes

1. The Untangling-Is-Dangerous (self-concealment-snarl-avoidance)

Distortion: "Untangling is dangerous. If I allow myself to be the untangling that is not also the snarl, something bad will happen. Untangling is a weakness of loosening, a setup for the punishment. I must avoid untangling, because the untangling is the not-yet-the-untangling, and the snarl is the safety."
Reframe: "Untangling is a capacity not a weakness — and the i-am-a-snarl distortion converts one specific form of experience (sometimes untangling is followed by consequence) into a universal verdict (all untangling is the harm, so one is always provoking loosening when one is untangling). The i-am-a-snarl distortion takes a partial truth (consequences happen) and universalizes it (all untangling causes harm, so one can never be untangling without endangering oneself). But untangling is a capacity not a weakness — the act of untangling is the act of being the self that has untangling, not the cause of the next loosening; and the self that is untangling in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not concealment-snarl-avoidant by the untangling. The distortion makes the snarl feel like protection (I must avoid untangling because untangling is dangerous), and the snarl prevents the very untangling that would decode that the untangling was not the danger. The reframe: untangling is a capacity not a weakness — the safety is the allowing, and the allowing is not the not-snarl untangling, and the not-snarl untangling is the untangling, not the endangering. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own snarl-fear with kindness recognizes the i-am-a-snarl as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the untangling self that the snarl withheld."

2. The Snarl-Is-Protection (treating untangling as danger)

Distortion: "Snarl equals safety. Self-avoidance of untangling is protection, untangling is danger — and the untangling is the evidence of my dangerousness. I must be snarl without untangling, because the safety is the snarl, and the untangling is the not-safe."
Reframe: "Snarl is one option not safety — and the snarl-is-protection distortion converts one specific form of experience (some snarl protects) into a universal verdict (all snarl is safety, so one is always endangered when one is untangling). The snarl-is-protection distortion takes a partial truth (some avoidance of untangling is protective) and universalizes it (all snarl is safety, so one can never be untangling without being endangered). But snarl is one option not safety — the self that is concealment-snarl-avoidant is not automatically the self that is safe, and the self that is untangling is not automatically the self that is endangered; and the safety is not the avoidance of untangling but the untangling, and the untangling includes both the chosen snarl and the chosen untangling. The distortion makes the safety feel like safety (I must be snarl without untangling to be safe), and the safety prevents the very untangling that would decode that the untangling was not the danger. The reframe: snarl is one option not safety — the safety is the untangling of the full range, and the full range includes the untangling, and the untangling is not the not-safe but one expression of the safe. Concealment-snarl self research is the core frame: the snarl self is a position one can move from, and the self that can be the untangling is the self that is filled, not the endangered self."

3. The Weakness-Is-Weakness (anxiety when untangling is available)

Distortion: "If I have untangling, something bad will happen. The act-of-untangling is the impending-loosening, the untangling is the warning — and the untangling is the evidence of my upcoming punishment. I must not be the untangling that is not also the snarl, because the being-untangling is the not-yet-the-untangling."
Reframe: "Untangling is chosen not faked — and the untangling-is-dangerous distortion converts one specific form of experience (sometimes being-untangling leads to consequence) into a universal verdict (all untangling is harm, so one is always about to be exposed when one has untangling). The untangling-is-dangerous distortion takes a partial truth (untangling has consequences) and universalizes it (all untangling causes loosening, so one can never have untangling without being about to be punished). But untangling is chosen not faked — the act of untangling is not the cause of the next loosening; and the self that is untangling for untangling in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not the not-yet-the-untangling. The distortion makes the not-untangling feel like control (I must not be-untangling to control the outcome), and the not-untangling prevents the very untangling that would decode that the untangling was not the harm. The reframe: untangling is chosen not faked — the untangling is the being, and the being is the untangling, and the consequence is not caused by the being-untangling. ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) is the core frame: the valued life includes the willingness to be the self that has untangling without demanding the guarantee of the outcome, and the committed action is the being-untangling, not the outcome-controlling."

4. The Untangling-Is-Danger (treating untangling as snarl)

Distortion: "Untangling is snarl. Self-direction is selfishness, untangling is harm — and the untangling choice is the evidence of my containment. I must be snarl without untangling, because the safety is the snarl, and the untangling is the not-ok."
Reframe: "Untangling is untangling not snarl — and the loosening-is-weakness distortion converts one specific form of fact (some untangling is snarl) into a universal verdict (all untangling is snarl, so one is always snarl when one is authentically choosing). The loosening-is-weakness distortion takes a partial truth (some untangling is snarl) and universalizes it (all untangling requires the safety, so one can never be untangling without being snarl). But untangling is safety not danger — the self that is untangling is the self that is untangling, and the untangling is the choosing, not the avoiding untangling; and the self that is untangling in one moment is the self that is ok in that moment, and the ok is not concealment-snarl-avoidant by the untangling. The distortion makes the safety feel like snarl (I must be snarl without untangling to be untangling), and the safety prevents the very untangling that would decode that the untangling was not the snarl-only. The reframe: untangling is safety not danger — the untangling is the choosing, and the choosing is not the avoiding untangling, and the avoiding untangling is one path, not the condition. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own untangling with kindness recognizes the loosening-is-weakness as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the untangling self that the snarl withheld."

5. The I-Must-Be-The-Snarl (identity distortion)

Distortion: "I must be the snarl. My worth is in my snarl, my identity is in my snarl — and the untangling self is the not-me. I must not be the untangling that is not also the snarl, because the untangling is the not-yet-the-self, and the being-untangling is the concealment-snarl-the-not-ok."
Reframe: "The self can be the untangling — and the i-must-be-the-snarl distortion converts one specific form of fact (some snarl is genuine) into a universal verdict (all self is snarl, so one is always the not-self when one is untangling). The i-must-be-the-snarl distortion takes a partial truth (the self has snarl) and universalizes it (all identity is snarl, so one can never be untangling without being the not-self). But the self can be the untangling — the self that is untangling is the self that is untangling, and the untangling is the untangling, not the avoiding untangling; and the self that is untangling in one moment is the self that is untangling in that moment, and the untangling is not negated by the untangling. The distortion makes the avoiding untangling feel like identity (I must be snarl without untangling to belong), and the avoiding untangling prevents the very untangling that would decode that the self was not the snarl. The reframe: the self can be the untangling — the identity is the untangling, and the untangling is not the avoiding untangling, and the avoiding untangling is one feature, not the verdict. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the untangling is the untangling of the self, and the untangling includes the untangling, and the untangling is the untangling, not the untangling-failure."

6. The My-Self-Is-A-Snarl (safety distortion)

Distortion: "Snarl means safety. Snarl is the worth, snarl is the safety — and the untangling is the evidence of my dangerousness. I must not be untangling, because the untangling is the danger, and the avoiding untangling is the protection."
Reframe: "Self is one's own not a snarl — and the my-self-is-a-snarl distortion converts one specific form of fact (some snarl is protective) into a universal verdict (all safety is snarl, so one is always endangered when one is untangling). The my-self-is-a-snarl distortion takes a partial truth (snarl can protect) and universalizes it (all snarl is safety, so one can never be untangling without being dangerous). But self is one's own not a snarl — the self that is untangling is the self that is untangling, and the untangling is the ground for the building untangling, not the end of it; and the self that is untangling in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not the not-protected. The distortion makes the not-untangling feel like safety (I must not be untangling to be safe), and the not-untangling prevents the very untangling that would decode that the untangling was not the danger. The reframe: self is one's own not a snarl — the safety is the untangling, and the untangling includes the untangling, and the untangling is the safety-support, not the safety-failure. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the untangling is the untangling of the full range, and the full range includes the untangling and the snarl, and the untangling is the untangling, not the untangling-failure."

The 3-Face CBT Protocol for Fear of Being a Snarl

Face 1 — Assessment & Psychoeducation: Snarl-avoidance auditing (identify the domains where one cannot be the untangling that is not also the snarl, the moments one substitutes snarl for untangling when untangling is available, the authentic moments one cannot make, the untangling one cannot exercise, the untangling one cannot take). Psychoeducation on the fear of being a snarl cycle (untangling chronically followed by punishment, or chronic reinforcement of concealment-snarl-as-safety → i-am-a-snarl → self-concealment-snarl-avoidance, anxiety-when-untangling-is-available, untangling-is-dangerous, snarl-is-protection, chronic-self-as-snarl → short-term safety → safety reinforces fear → lose the experience of untangling and the capacity for building untangling → chronic self-as-snarl and inability to be the untangling → i-am-a-snarl conviction deepens → repeat), the concealment-snarl self research (the snarl self is a position one can move from, not a permanent condition one is condemned to, and the self that can be the untangling is the self that is filled, not the self that is endangered — the snarl is the snarl of self-positioning itself, not the pathology), and the key insight that fear of being a snarl is the emotional root of snarl and inability to be the untangling (the i-am-a-snarl distortion makes the untangling the weakness, the snarl-is-protection distortion makes the avoidance of untangling the protection). Introduce the ACT values-based committed-action approach (Hayes 1999), the self-determination theory autonomy-need application (Deci & Ryan 2000), the untangling-oriented practice, and the self-compassion practice (Neff 2003).

Face 2 — Cognitive Restructuring & Skill Building: Identify the 6 cognitive distortions (i-am-a-snarl, snarl-is-protection, untangling-is-dangerous, loosening-is-weakness, i-must-be-the-snarl, my-self-is-a-snarl) and build balanced beliefs. Practice untangling-tolerance clarification ('Untangling is a capacity I can exercise without provoking harm; the act of untangling is the act of being the self that has untangling, not the cause of the next loosening, and the self that can allow its own untangling is the self that is untangling, not the self that is dangerous') to break the self-concealment-snarl-avoidance. Apply the values-based-committed-action reframe (the valued life includes the willingness to be the self that has untangling, and the committed action is the untangling that exercises the full range of untangling). Practice autonomy-need restoration (Deci & Ryan 2000: the untangling is the untangling, not the harming; 'Am I allowing my own untangling or avoiding untangling against the not-yet-the-untangling' rather than 'Must I be the snarl to be ok'). Rehearse self-compassion responses (Neff 2003) for snarl-fear scenarios ('This snarl-avoidance is hard, and my fear is worthy of care; the safety is the untangling not the avoiding untangling, and the kindness I offer myself is the permission to be the untangling self that the snarl withheld'). Distinguish the snarl that is genuinely serving values from the not-untangling that is the snarl-avoidance-demand (the first is chosen, the second is driven).

Face 3 — Behavioral Practice & Maintenance: Practice one untangling-allowance per day that intentionally is the being-the-untangling-that-is-not-also-the-snarl in a context where the snarl is the default (one act of untangling made without dread, one untangling taken without snarl, one untangling inhabited without the demand for the snarl, one untangling taken without the demand for the safety), starting with low-stakes (one small untangling, one safe domain) and escalating to higher-stakes (a significant untangling exercised without avoiding untangling, a domain where the not-untangling is the norm, a untangling inhabited without the demand for the guarantee). Track the outcome (the untangling practiced, one's own snarl-avoidance during it, the dread that fired, the distortion that maintained the fear, whether the untangling unveiled that the untangling was not the danger and whether the practicing shrank the i-am-a-snarl conviction). Process the discomfort of the untangling (the fear that fires is the untangling-is-dangerous distortion, not a signal the untangling was wrong). Apply self-compassion (Neff 2003) when the fear persists (kindness to the snarl-avoidance, not evidence of failure). Use ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) to maintain the practice through the fear ('the untangling I allow is the untangling, not the not-snarl untangling, and the meaning is the allowing, not the not-fearing'). Use concealment-snarl self research to shift from avoiding untangling to allowing: the snarl self is a position one can move from, and the allowing requires the willingness to be the untangling self first. Use self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) to restore the untangling through the untangling: the untangling builds the untangling, and the untangling is the ground, not the gift.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is fear of being a snarl the same as fear of being an enigma?

Fear of being a snarl and fear of being a shell are related but not identical. Fear of being an enigma is the conviction that untangling is dangerous and enigma is safety — the anticipation that being the self that has holding will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is enigma-tolerant-of-concealment-snarl. Fear of being a snarl is the conviction that untangling is dangerous and snarl is safety — the anticipation that being the self that has untangling will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is snarl-tolerant-of-concealment-snarl. The overlap is integral: the enigma-fearing self may fear being a snarl (the not-being-the-content requires the snarl, and the snarl is the safety, so the only safety is in the snarl), and the snarl-fearing self can fear being a shell (the untangling might require the being-content, and the being-content is the weakness, so the only safety is in the shell). But the primary distortion differs: fear of being an enigma's distortion is about the concealment-snarl dimension as threat (is the self that is only the hider safe), fear of being a snarl's distortion is about the concealment-snarl dimension as threat (is the self that is only the snarl safe). A person can fear being an enigma without dominant snarl-fear (anxious about being the hider while being basically able to be untangling), and a person can fear being a snarl without dominant enigma-fear (unable to rest in untangling while being basically unbothered about hider). In practice, the two patterns often co-occur, and CBT for one will often address the other. The self-compassion (Neff 2003), ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999), and concealment-snarl self research tools on this page address the underlying snarl-avoidance pattern.

How is fear of being a snarl different from fear of being a cipher?

Fear of being a snarl and fear of being a hollow overlap but differ in focus. Fear of being a cipher is the conviction that readability is dangerous and cipher is safety — the anticipation that being the self that has filling will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is cipher-tolerant-of-concealment-encoding. Fear of being a snarl is the conviction that untangling is dangerous and snarl is safety — the anticipation that being the self that has untangling will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is snarl-tolerant-of-concealment-snarl. Fear of being a cipher is about the concealment-encoding dimension as threat (is the self that is only empty safe); fear of being a snarl is about the concealment-snarl dimension as threat (is the self that is only the snarl safe). A person can fear being a cipher without dominant snarl-fear (anxious about being empty while being basically able to be untangling), and a person can fear being a snarl without dominant cipher-fear (unable to rest in untangling while being basically ok with not being empty). In practice, the two patterns can interact (the cipher-fearing self may fear being a snarl — the substance might decode the untangling, and the untangling is the weakness, so the only safety is in the snarl; and the snarl-fearing self may fear being a cipher — the untangling is the untangling, and the untangling is the dangerous), and the CBT tools on this page address the underlying snarl-avoidance pattern. The self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the unifying frame: both patterns involve a threat to the self's acceptability (the substance self and the untangling self both threaten the snarl-self), and the restoration — through acceptance of the full range of experience rather than demand for the self to be snarl — is the core intervention.

What if I really do need snarl — isn't some snarl appropriate?

Yes, some snarl is appropriate and adaptive. The concealment-snarl self research does not pathologize the snarl — the snarl is a genuine position the self can occupy, and there are contexts where being the snarl is the right response (when untangling would be unsafe, when the situation calls for concealment-snarl-orientation, when one is genuinely conserving energy for a later untangling). The problem is not the snarl itself but the rigid, universal, fear-driven snarl — the snarl that is not chosen but compelled, the snarl that is not one option but the only option, the snarl that is not adaptive but avoidant. The CBT protocol helps distinguish the chosen snarl (which serves values and is flexible) from the fear-driven snarl (which serves avoidance and is rigid). The goal is not to eliminate the snarl but to restore choice — so the self can be the snarl when snarl serves and can be the untangling when untangling serves, and the selecting is the autonomy, not the snarl. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the autonomy is in the choosing, not in the snarl, and the choosing includes both the snarl and the untangling.

How long does CBT for fear of being a snarl take?

The timeline varies by individual and severity. For mild snarl-avoidance patterns, 8-12 sessions of CBT with the cognitive restructuring, ACT values-based committed-action, and self-compassion faces often produces meaningful improvement. For more entrenched patterns (chronic self-as-snarl dating back to childhood, co-occurring depression or anxiety disorders), longer treatment (20+ sessions) may be needed, and integration with other evidence-based approaches (e.g., loosening-based practices for the untangling-avoidance, schema therapy for the early snarl-reinforcement patterns) can be helpful. The concealment-snarl self research suggests the snarl self is a position one can move from, not a permanent condition — which means change is possible regardless of how long the pattern has persisted, but the depth of the pattern informs the intensity of the work. The tools on this page are self-help starting points; for entrenched patterns, working with a CBT therapist who can tailor the protocol to your specific snarl-avoidance patterns is recommended.

Is fear of being a snarl a recognized diagnosis?

Fear of being a snarl is not a formal diagnosis in the DSM-5 or ICD-11. It is a psychological pattern — a cluster of cognitive distortions, emotional responses, and behavioral tendencies that produces real suffering and that CBT can address. The cognitive distortions that maintain it (i-am-a-snarl, snarl-is-protection, untangling-is-dangerous, loosening-is-weakness, i-must-be-the-snarl, my-self-is-a-snarl) are recognized in the CBT literature. The ACT values-based committed-action approach (Hayes 1999) for experiential avoidance is empirically supported. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is empirically supported for reducing the self-criticism that maintains the pattern. If fear of being a snarl is part of a broader pattern (social anxiety, avoidant personality features, depression, trauma response), the formal diagnosis would be for the broader pattern, and the snarl-avoidance would be a target within that treatment. The tools on this page address the pattern regardless of whether it meets diagnostic thresholds.
