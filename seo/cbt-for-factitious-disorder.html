What is Factitious Disorder (Munchausen Syndrome)?

Factitious disorder (DSM-5 300.19, also called Munchausen syndrome when severe and chronic with repeated hospitalizations) is characterized by the falsification of physical or psychological symptoms with the intention of deceiving others to assume the sick role. The symptoms are intentionally produced or feigned. The motivation is the sick role itself (internal motivation), NOT external gain (which would be malingering). The deception is the core feature. Common presentations include feigning symptoms, falsifying medical history or records, tampering with tests (contaminating urine samples, altering medical documents), self-inducing injury or infection, and aggravating existing conditions. Factitious disorder imposed on another (formerly Munchausen syndrome by proxy) involves falsifying symptoms in another person (often a child or dependent) under one's care. More common in males, often with healthcare employment background, onset typically early adulthood. The key distinction from conversion disorder is that factitious symptoms ARE intentionally produced; from malingering is that factitious motivation is the sick role, not external gain; from somatic symptom disorder is that factitious involves deception, SSD does not.

Why Factitious Disorder Needs Specific CBT

General anxiety tools say "you worry, let's examine the worry." General somatic-symptom tools say "you catastrophize a symptom, let's examine the catastrophizing." General illness-anxiety tools say "you fear having a disease, let's examine the fear." General medical-seeking tools say "you seek medical care, let's reduce the seeking." None of them address the specifics: the I-Need-To-Be-Sick ties worth to the patient role in a way general anxiety tools cannot address because the worth-conditioning is about identity not worry, the No-One-Will-Care-If-I-Am-Well makes care conditional on illness in a way general somatic tools cannot address because the conditioning is about the relationship not the symptom, the My-Story-Is-Not-Enough makes real suffering insufficient in a way general self-criticism tools cannot address because the inadequacy is about the authenticity not the achievement, the I-Am-In-Control-Of-My-Illness makes symptom-manufacturing a source of control in a way general control tools cannot address because the control is about the illness not the life, the They-Want-Me-Sick confirms the sick role through others' expectations in a way general anxiety tools cannot address because the confirmation is about the role not the worry, and the One-More-Test-Will-Prove-It drives the medical-seeking loop in a way general health-anxiety tools cannot address because the loop is about the identity-validation not the disease-fear. This produces specific, identifiable thought patterns that CBT for factitious disorder can target directly:

The "I-Need-To-Be-Sick" Distortion (Sick-Role-Identity / Worth-Conditioning)

Distortion: "I need to be sick. The diagnosis is the identity, and the identity is the worth, and the worth is the one thing that means I matter. If I am well, I am no one; if I am no one, I am invisible; if I am invisible, I am abandoned. The patient is the only self I know how to be, and the being-well is the threat to the only self I have."
Reframe: "I am examining the evidence and challenging the I-Need-To-Be-Sick. The I-Need-To-Be-Sick (the diagnosis is the identity and the identity is the worth) was a sick-role-identity and a worth-conditioning: the mind takes the patient role and fuses it with worth, and because the fusion feels like evidence of mattering, the inference is that wellness means not mattering. The sick-role-identity (I am a patient) is the distortion: the patient is a role one occupies, not a self one is — the self exists before, during, and after the illness, and the role is one chapter, not the whole book. The worth-conditioning (I matter only if I am sick) is the distortion: worth is not conditional on illness — worth is intrinsic, and the people who care about you care about you, not your diagnosis. CBT helps by examining the evidence (what is the actual evidence that your worth depends on illness versus the evidence that worth is intrinsic, what would the people who love you say about your worth if you were well, what valued roles exist beyond the patient role), challenging the identity-fusion (the patient is a role, not the self), and reconstructing identity through valued roles (friend, partner, worker, hobbyist, community member) that exist independent of illness — so the mind learns that the self is more than the patient, and the worth is not the diagnosis. The diagnosis is real; the conclusion that it is the identity is the distortion.

The "No-One-Will-Care-If-I-Am-Well" Distortion (Abandonment-Fear / Care-Conditioning)

Distortion: "No one will care if I am well. The care is given to the diagnosis, not to the person, and the wellness is the loss of the care, and the loss of the care is the abandonment. The illness is the relationship, and the wellness is the end of the relationship, and the end of the relationship is the one thing I cannot survive."
Reframe: "I am examining the evidence and challenging the No-One-Will-Care-If-I-Am-Well. The No-One-Will-Care-If-I-Am-Well (care is given to the diagnosis not the person) was an abandonment-fear and a care-conditioning: the mind takes the care received during illness and concludes it is conditional on the illness, and because the conclusion feels like evidence of conditional love, the inference is that wellness produces abandonment. The abandonment-fear (wellness is abandonment) is the distortion: the care given during illness is given to the person who is ill, not to the illness itself — the person is the recipient, the illness is the context, and the care continues (in different form) when the illness resolves. The care-conditioning (care is conditional on illness) is the distortion: healthy people receive care, attention, and love — the form changes (celebration instead of concern, shared activity instead of bedside vigil, presence instead of worry), but the care does not end. CBT helps by examining the evidence (what is the actual evidence that care is conditional on illness versus the evidence that care is given to the person, what relationships in your life have continued through wellness, what would the people who care about you say about whether they would abandon you if you were well), challenging the conditioning (the care is for you, not the diagnosis), and building authentic connections that do not require illness — so the mind learns that care is for the person, and wellness is not the abandonment. The care during illness is real; the conclusion that it ends with wellness is the distortion.

The "I-Am-In-Control-Of-My-Illness" Distortion (Control-Illusion / Cost-Blindness)

Distortion: "I am in control of my illness. I produce the symptoms, I choose the tests, I direct the care, and the directing is the power. The power is the one thing that means I am not powerless, and the not-powerless is the proof that the manufacturing is the mastery, and the mastery is worth the cost."
Reframe: "I am examining the evidence and challenging the I-Am-In-Control-Of-My-Illness. The I-Am-In-Control-Of-My-Illness (I produce the symptoms so I am in control) was a control-illusion and a cost-blindness: the mind takes the ability to produce symptoms and inflates it into control over the illness, and because the inflation feels like evidence of mastery, the inference is that the manufacturing is power. The control-illusion (I am in control) is the distortion: the ability to produce symptoms is not control over the illness — it is control over the appearance of illness, and the appearance is not the illness, and the manufacturing creates a new problem (the factitious disorder itself) that the person does not control. The cost-blindness (the mastery is worth the cost) is the distortion: the cost is real — medical risk from unnecessary procedures, psychological cost of the deception, relational cost of the falsification, identity cost of the role-fusion — and the cost exceeds the benefit. CBT helps by examining the evidence (what is the actual evidence that you are in control versus the evidence that the manufacturing is a new problem you do not control, what is the actual cost of the manufacturing versus the actual benefit, what would a neutral observer say about whether the control is real), challenging the control-illusion (the appearance is not the illness, and the manufacturing is a new problem), and developing authentic sources of control (valued action, meaningful choices, real agency) that do not require falsification — so the mind learns that the control is illusory, and the cost is real. The ability to produce symptoms is real; the conclusion that it is control is the distortion.

If you are in crisis

Factitious disorder, especially when the deception is long-standing, the medical-seeking is consuming, the identity is fused with the patient role, or the No-One-Will-Care-If-I-Am-Well is producing despair, can coexist with severe distress, hopelessness, or suicidal ideation. The need for care is real; the conclusion that the care requires illness is the distortion. If you are having thoughts of suicide or self-harm, please reach out now:

  • Samaritans (UK/ROI): call 116 123
  • NAMI HelpLine (US): 1-800-950-6264 or text "HelpLine" to 62640

Frequently Asked Questions

Is factitious disorder the same as malingering?

No. Factitious disorder and malingering both involve the intentional production or feigning of symptoms, but they differ critically in motivation. Factitious disorder: the symptoms are intentionally produced to assume the sick role — the motivation is internal (the need to be a patient, to receive care, to be the focus of medical attention), and there is no external reward. Malingering: the symptoms are intentionally produced for external gain — the motivation is external (money, time off work, legal advantage, avoiding responsibility), and the reward is tangible. The I-Need-To-Be-Sick and the No-One-Will-Care-If-I-Am-Well are factitious-specific patterns that malingering does not produce because malingering does not seek the sick role, it seeks the reward. Factitious disorder is a mental disorder in DSM-5; malingering is NOT a mental disorder, it is a condition that may warrant legal or administrative response. CBT is used for factitious disorder; CBT is not the treatment for malingering.

How is factitious disorder different from somatic symptom disorder or illness anxiety?

Factitious disorder, somatic symptom disorder, and illness anxiety disorder all involve physical symptoms and medical seeking, but they differ critically in deception and symptom production. Factitious disorder: the symptoms ARE intentionally produced or feigned, and deception is present — the person is aware they are causing or faking the symptoms. Somatic symptom disorder: the symptoms are NOT intentionally produced, and deception is absent — the person has real symptoms (which may be medically explained or unexplained) and excessive distress about them. Illness anxiety disorder: the symptoms are NOT intentionally produced, and deception is absent — the person is preoccupied with having or acquiring a disease, with few or no somatic symptoms. The I-Am-In-Control-Of-My-Illness is factitious-specific because it reflects the actual intentional production of symptoms; somatic symptom disorder and illness anxiety do not produce this pattern because the symptoms are not under intentional control. Use the factitious tools if deception and intentional symptom production are present; use the somatic tools if distress about real symptoms is the problem; use the illness anxiety tools if fear of having a disease is the problem.

How is factitious disorder different from conversion disorder?

Factitious disorder and conversion disorder both involve symptoms that may not be explained by disease, but they differ critically in intention. Factitious disorder: the symptoms ARE intentionally produced or feigned — the person is deliberately causing or faking symptoms to assume the sick role, the symptom is not real and the person is aware they are producing it. Conversion disorder: the symptoms are NOT intentionally produced or faked — the brain is generating genuine neurological symptoms without structural disease, the person is not making it up, the symptom is real and the person is distressed by it. The I-Am-In-Control-Of-My-Illness is factitious-specific because it reflects the actual intentional production; conversion disorder does not produce this pattern because the symptoms are not under intentional control. The My-Body-Has-Betrayed-Me and the Its-All-In-My-Head are conversion-specific because they reflect the experience of real non-intentional symptoms; factitious disorder does not produce these patterns because the symptoms are intentional. Use the factitious tools if intentional symptom production is present; use the conversion tools if the symptoms are real and non-intentional.
