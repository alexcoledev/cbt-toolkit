Fear of being a fraud — the conviction that the genuine self is dangerous and the fraudulence is the safety, the anticipation that genuineness will be punished or lead to harm, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is maskulence-avoidant-of-genuineness and the mask is the protection — is one of the most pervasive and painful emotional patterns in fraudulence-conditioned culture. It is the emotional root that drives self-imposter-avoidance (the substitution of a mask for one's own genuineness when genuineness is available, the inability to inhabit one's own genuineness, the conversion of every act of genuineness into a harm), anxiety when genuineness is available (the genuineness itself produces dread, the genuineness is the trigger, the available genuineness is the warning signal), treating genuineness as dangerous/harmful (the person cannot allow the genuineness because the genuineness is the not-yet-the-rejection, the person cannot inhabit the true self because the true self is the not-yet-the-rejected), equating fraudulence with safety (the belief that exposure is inevitable and genuineness is danger, the framing of the fraudulence as the worth and the genuineness as the threat), and chronic impostor-feeling (the persistent substitution of maskulence for genuineness to avoid the genuineness of genuineness, the inability to be genuine because the original is the endangered). It is distinct from fear of being different (which is about the social dimension — being fundamentally fake) and from fear of being authentic (which is about real self vs performance). Fear of being a fraud is about the ontological dimension: the self that cannot rest in genuineness because the genuineness is the danger, the self that experiences its own achievement as a vulnerability of harm, the self that treats every original act as a derivative and every genuineness as the not-yet-the-rejected.

Fear of being a fraud is the emotional root of three downstream patterns. The i-am-a-fraud distortion makes every act of genuineness a harm and every genuineness a vulnerability, producing self-imposter-avoidance and anxiety-when-genuineness-is-available (the person concludes the only safety is in the fraudulence and the fraudulence requires the not-being-genuine, so the genuineness is refused and the genuineness is never exercised because the genuineness is always the not-yet-the-rejected). The fraudulence-is-safety distortion makes the mask the protection and the genuineness the danger, producing the chronic devaluation of genuineness and the equation of maskulence with worth (the person experiences the genuineness and the genuineness is the dangerous, and the dangerous is the not-safe, and the not-safe is the threat the fraudulence was meant to prevent). And the genuineness-is-dangerous distortion makes the act-of-genuineness the impending-rejection and the genuineness the warning, producing chronic-impostor-feeling and the constant-avoidance of genuineness-requirement (the person has genuineness and the genuineness is the harm, and the harm is the consequence, and the consequence is the rejection the fraudulence was meant to prevent). Fear of being a fraud is the upstream cause; self-imposter-avoidance, anxiety-when-genuineness-is-available, and chronic-impostor-feeling are the downstream effects. The key insight for fear of being a fraud: genuineness is not the danger the fraudulence protects from — the self that can allow its own genuineness is the self that is whole, not the self that is dangerous, and the safety is not the fraudulence but the living, and the living is the being, not the mask. The imposter-syndrome research (Clance & Imes 1978) provides the deeper frame: genuine living is associated with greater well-being, lower anxiety, and more fulfilling achieving engagement — the self that allows genuineness is the self that is whole, not the self that is endangered.

The Fear of Being a Fraud Cycle (How It Develops)

The fear of being a fraud cycle is the self-reinforcing loop that produces the self-imposter-avoidance, the anxiety-when-genuineness-is-available, and the chronic-impostor-feeling that come from concluding that the genuine self is dangerous and the fraudulence is the safety:

A situation with genuineness chronically followed by rejection, or chronic reinforcement of maskulence-as-safety (an early experience where being original was criticized, where the true creation was shamed, where the self-expression preceded the harm, a chronic reinforcement of mask where one's sense of value was entirely defined by their fraudulence and never by their genuineness) → i-am-a-fraud fires (the genuineness is the danger, the achievement is the vulnerability, the genuineness is the warning, the genuineness is the not-yet-the-rejected) → self-imposter-avoidance, anxiety-when-genuineness-is-available, genuineness-is-dangerous, fraudulence-is-safety, chronic-impostor-feeling (the substitution of a mask for one's own genuineness, the inability to inhabit genuineness, the constant avoidance of genuineness, the devaluation of genuineness, the constant fraudulence) → short-term safety achieved (the fraudulence feels like protection from the rejection, the not-being-genuine feels like the control over the outcome, the mask feels like the safety) → the safety reinforces the fear (the fraudulence prevents the living that would reveal that the genuineness was not the danger, the not-being-genuine prevents the evidence that would disconfirm the i-am-a-fraud conviction, the mask-as-safety prevents the genuineness that would reveal that the genuineness was not the danger) → the person loses the experience of genuineness and the capacity for inhabiting achievement (the genuineness that comes from the allowing is never experienced, because the allowing is always the not-yet-the-safe; the genuineness that comes from the being-genuine is never experienced, because the being-genuine is always the not-yet-the-rejected) → chronic impostor-feeling and inability to inhabit genuineness → i-am-a-fraud conviction deepens → repeat

The cycle is driven by the short-term safety that fraudulence and not-being-genuine provides (fraudulence-because-the-fraudulence-is-the-protection-from-the-rejection and not-being-genuine-because-the-not-being-genuine-is-the-control-over-the-outcome both feel like they are preventing the rejection, and the feeling reduces the fear of being a fraud temporarily) and maintained by the long-term cost of the i-am-a-fraud pattern (the genuineness is never experienced, because the genuineness is always the not-yet-the-safe; the genuineness is never exercised for the self, because the being is always the not-yet-the-rejected; and the i-am-a-fraud conviction deepens because every prevented genuineness is treated as evidence that the genuineness was the danger). The tragic irony of fear of being a fraud is that the safety strategy (fraudulence, not-being-genuine, mask-as-safety) produces the very outcome it was meant to prevent (the person refuses the genuineness and the refusing-the-genuineness produces the very harm the fraudulence was meant to disprove, and the chronic not-allowing-genuineness is the loss of the creation, not the protection from the rejection — the fear of being a fraud prevents the very genuineness that would have made the life worth the living). The CBT intervention breaks the cycle at two points: (1) genuineness-tolerance-clarification — when the i-am-a-fraud distortion treats every act of genuineness as a harm, the first step is clarifying that genuineness is a capacity one can exercise without provoking harm, and recognizing that the self that can allow its own genuineness is the self that is whole, not the self that is dangerous; (2) values-based-committed-action — when the fraudulence-is-safety distortion fires, one challenges it with the ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999): the valued life includes the willingness to be genuine, and the committed action is the living that exercises the full range of original self-expression.

The 6 Cognitive Distortions & Their Reframes

1. The Genuineness-Is-Dangerous (self-imposter-avoidance)

Distortion: "Genuineness is dangerous. If I allow myself to be genuine, something bad will happen. Achievement is a vulnerability of harm, a setup for the rejection. I must avoid genuineness myself, because the genuineness is the not-yet-the-rejected, and the fraudulence is the safety."
Reframe: "Genuineness is a capacity not a vulnerability — and the i-am-a-fraud distortion converts one specific form of experience (sometimes genuineness is followed by consequence) into a universal verdict (all genuineness is the harm, so one is always provoking rejection when one is original). The i-am-a-fraud distortion takes a partial truth (consequences happen) and universalizes it (all genuineness causes harm, so one can never be genuine without endangering oneself). But genuineness is a capacity not a vulnerability — the act of genuineness is the act of genuineness, not the cause of the next exposure; and the self that is original in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not maskulence-avoidant by the genuineness. The distortion makes the fraudulence feel like protection (I must avoid genuineness myself because genuineness is dangerous), and the fraudulence prevents the very genuineness that would reveal that the genuineness was not the danger. The reframe: genuineness is a capacity not a vulnerability — the safety is the allowing, and the allowing is not the not-mask genuineness, and the not-mask genuineness is the living, not the endangering. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own impostor-fear with kindness recognizes the i-am-a-fraud as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the genuine self that the fraudulence withheld."

2. The Fraudulence-Is-Safety (treating genuineness as danger)

Distortion: "Fraudulence equals safety. Self-avoidance of genuineness is protection, genuineness is danger — and the genuineness is the evidence of my dangerousness. I must hide, because the safety is the safety, and the genuineness is the not-safe."
Reframe: "Fraudulence is one option not safety — and the fraudulence-is-safety distortion converts one specific form of experience (some hiding protects) into a universal verdict (all fraudulence is safety, so one is always endangered when one is original). The fraudulence-is-safety distortion takes a partial truth (some avoidance of genuineness is protective) and universalizes it (all fraudulence is safety, so one can never be genuine without being endangered). But fraudulence is one option not safety — the self that is maskulence-avoidant is not automatically the self that is safe, and the self that is original is not automatically the self that is endangered; and the safety is not the avoidance of genuineness but the living, and the living includes both the chosen fraudulence and the chosen genuineness. The distortion makes the safety feel like safety (I must hide to be safe), and the safety prevents the very genuineness that would reveal that the genuineness was not the danger. The reframe: fraudulence is one option not safety — the safety is the living of the full range, and the full range includes the genuineness, and the genuineness is not the not-safe but one expression of the safe. Imposter-syndrome research (Clance & Imes 1978) is the core frame: genuine living is associated with greater well-being, and the genuine self is the whole self, not the endangered self."

3. The Achievement-Is-Trap (anxiety when genuineness is available)

Distortion: "If I have genuineness, something bad will happen. The act-of-genuineness is the impending-rejection, the genuineness is the warning — and the genuineness is the evidence of my upcoming rejection. I must not act on genuineness, because the acting is the not-yet-the-rejected."
Reframe: "Achievement is contribution not harm — and the genuineness-is-dangerous distortion converts one specific form of experience (sometimes being-genuine leads to consequence) into a universal verdict (all genuineness is harm, so one is always about to be rejected when one has genuineness). The genuineness-is-dangerous distortion takes a partial truth (genuineness has consequences) and universalizes it (all genuineness causes rejection, so one can never have genuineness without being about to be rejected). But achievement is earned not faked — the act of genuineness is not the cause of the next rejection; and the self that is present for genuineness in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not the not-yet-the-rejected. The distortion makes the not-being-genuine feel like control (I must not act to control the outcome), and the not-being-genuine prevents the very presence that would reveal that the genuineness was not the harm. The reframe: achievement is earned not faked — the present is the present, and the genuineness is the genuineness, and the consequence is not caused by the being-genuine. ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) is the core frame: the valued life includes the willingness to be genuine without demanding the guarantee of the outcome, and the committed action is the being-genuine, not the outcome-controlling."

4. The Genuineness-Is-Danger (treating genuineness as derivative)

Distortion: "Genuineness is derivative. Self-direction is selfishness, genuineness is harm — and the original choice is the evidence of my unworthiness. I must hide, because the safety is the fraudulence, and the self-expression is the not-ok."
Reframe: "Genuineness is genuineness not derivative — and the exposure-is-inevitable distortion converts one specific form of fact (some genuineness is derivative) into a universal verdict (all self-expression is derivative, so one is always unworthy when one is originally choosing). The exposure-is-inevitable distortion takes a partial truth (some genuineness is derivative) and universalizes it (all genuineness requires the safety, so one can never be genuine without being derivative). But genuineness is safety not danger — the self that is original is the self that is original, and the genuineness is the choosing, not the avoiding genuineness; and the self that is original in one moment is the self that is ok in that moment, and the ok is not maskulence-avoidant by the self-expression. The distortion makes the safety feel like fraudulence (I must hide to be genuine), and the safety prevents the very genuineness that would reveal that the genuineness was not the derivative. The reframe: genuineness is safety not danger — the genuineness is the choosing, and the choosing is not the avoiding genuineness, and the avoiding genuineness is one path, not the condition. Self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the core frame: the person who meets their own genuineness with kindness recognizes the exposure-is-inevitable as a distortion, not a verdict, and the kindness is the permission to be the genuine self that the fraudulence withheld."

5. The I-Must-Hide (identity distortion)

Distortion: "I must hide. My worth is in my maskulence, my identity is in my mask — and the genuine self is the not-me. I must not act on genuineness, because the genuineness is the not-yet-the-self, and the acting is the becoming-the-not-ok."
Reframe: "The self can be genuine — and the i-must-hide distortion converts one specific form of fact (some copies are genuine) into a universal verdict (all self is mask, so one is always the not-self when one is original). The i-must-hide distortion takes a partial truth (the self has copies) and universalizes it (all identity is maskulence, so one can never be genuine without being the not-self). But the self can be genuine — the self that is original is the self that is whole, and the wholeness is the living, not the avoiding genuineness; and the self that is original in one moment is the self that is original in that moment, and the original is not negated by the genuineness. The distortion makes the avoiding genuineness feel like identity (I must hide to belong), and the avoiding genuineness prevents the very genuineness that would reveal that the self was not the fraudulence. The reframe: the self can be genuine — the identity is the living, and the living is not the avoiding genuineness, and the avoiding genuineness is one feature, not the verdict. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the genuineness is the living of the self, and the living includes the genuineness, and the genuineness is the genuineness-expression, not the genuineness-failure."

6. The My-Achievements-Are-Unearned (safety distortion)

Distortion: "Copied means role. Hiding is the worth, self-maskulence is the safety — and the genuineness is the evidence of my dangerousness. I must not be genuine, because the genuineness is the danger, and the avoiding genuineness is the protection."
Reframe: "Identity is one's own not mask — and the my-achievements-are-unearned distortion converts one specific form of fact (some fraudulence is protective) into a universal verdict (all safety is maskulence, so one is always endangered when one is original). The my-achievements-are-unearned distortion takes a partial truth (fraudulence can protect) and universalizes it (all hiding is safety, so one can never be genuine without being dangerous). But identity is one's own not mask — the self that is original is the self that is whole, and the genuineness is the ground for the genuine creation, not the end of it; and the self that is original in one moment is the self that is alive in that moment, and the aliveness is not the not-protected. The distortion makes the not-original feel like safety (I must not be genuine to be safe), and the not-original prevents the very genuineness that would reveal that the genuineness was not the danger. The reframe: identity is one's own not mask — the safety is the living, and the living includes the genuineness, and the genuineness is the safety-support, not the safety-failure. Self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the genuineness is the living of the full range, and the full range includes the genuineness and the fraudulence, and the genuineness is the genuineness-expression, not the genuineness-failure."

The 3-Component CBT Protocol for Fear of Being a Fraud

Component 1 — Assessment & Psychoeducation: Fraudulence-avoidance auditing (identify the domains where one cannot inhabit genuineness, the moments one substitutes a mask for one's own genuineness when genuineness is available, the originalities one cannot allow, the genuineness one cannot exercise, the self-expression one cannot take). Psychoeducation on the fear of being a fraud cycle (genuineness chronically followed by rejection, or chronic reinforcement of maskulence-as-safety → i-am-a-fraud → self-imposter-avoidance, anxiety-when-genuineness-is-available, genuineness-is-dangerous, fraudulence-is-safety, chronic-impostor-feeling → short-term safety → safety reinforces fear → lose the experience of genuineness and the capacity for inhabiting achievement → chronic impostor-feeling and inability to inhabit genuineness → i-am-a-fraud conviction deepens → repeat), the imposter-syndrome research (Clance & Imes 1978: genuine living is associated with greater well-being, lower anxiety, and more fulfilling achieving engagement), and the key insight that fear of being a fraud is the emotional root of mask and inability to inhabit genuineness (the i-am-a-fraud distortion makes the genuineness the vulnerability, the fraudulence-is-safety distortion makes the avoidance of genuineness the protection). Introduce the ACT values-based committed-action approach (Hayes 1999), the self-determination theory autonomy-need application (Deci & Ryan 2000), the genuineness-oriented practice, and the self-compassion practice (Neff 2003).

Component 2 — Cognitive Restructuring & Skill Building: Identify the 6 cognitive distortions (i-am-a-fraud, fraudulence-is-safety, genuineness-is-dangerous, exposure-is-inevitable, i-must-hide, my-achievements-are-unearned) and build balanced beliefs. Practice genuineness-tolerance clarification ('Genuineness is a capacity I can exercise without provoking harm; the act of genuineness is the act of genuineness, not the cause of the next exposure, and the self that can allow its own genuineness is the self that is whole, not the self that is dangerous') to break the self-imposter-avoidance. Apply the values-based-committed-action reframe (the valued life includes the willingness to be genuine, and the committed action is the living that exercises the full range of original self-expression). Practice autonomy-need restoration (Deci & Ryan 2000: the genuineness is the living, not the harming; 'Am I allowing my own genuineness or avoiding genuineness against the not-yet-the-rejected' rather than 'Must I be a mask to be ok'). Rehearse self-compassion responses (Neff 2003) for impostor-fear scenarios ('This imposter-avoidance is hard, and my fear is worthy of care; the safety is the living not the avoiding genuineness, and the kindness I offer myself is the permission to be the genuine self that the fraudulence withheld'). Distinguish the fraudulence that is genuinely serving values from the not-being-genuine that is the imposter-avoidance-demand (the first is chosen, the second is driven).

Component 3 — Behavioral Practice & Maintenance: Practice one genuineness-allowance per day that intentionally is the inhabiting-of-genuineness in a context where the fraudulence is the default (one act of genuineness made without dread, one genuineness exercised without self-maskulence, one genuineness inhabited without the demand for the mask, one self-expression taken without the demand for the safety), starting with low-stakes (one small genuineness, one safe domain) and escalating to higher-stakes (a significant genuineness exercised without avoiding genuineness, a domain where the not-being-genuine is the norm, an genuineness inhabited without the demand for the guarantee). Track the outcome (the genuineness practiced, one's own imposter-avoidance during it, the dread that fired, the distortion that maintained the fear, whether the genuineness revealed that the genuineness was not the danger and whether the practicing shrank the i-am-a-fraud conviction). Process the discomfort of the genuineness (the fear that fires is the genuineness-is-dangerous distortion, not a signal the genuineness was wrong). Apply self-compassion (Neff 2003) when the fear persists (kindness to the imposter-avoidance, not evidence of failure). Use ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) to maintain the practice through the fear ('the genuineness I allow is the living, not the not-mask genuineness, and the meaning is the allowing, not the not-fearing'). Use imposter-syndrome research (Clance & Imes 1978) to shift from avoiding genuineness to allowing: genuine living is associated with greater well-being, and the allowing requires the willingness to be the genuine self first. Use self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) to restore the genuineness through the genuineness: the genuineness builds the genuineness, and the genuineness is the living, not the fraudulence-avoidant-self. Generalize to higher-stakes domains as the i-am-a-fraud conviction shrinks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is fear of being a fraud the same as fear of being different?

Fear of being a fraud and fear of being different are related but not identical. Fear of being different is the conviction that the different self is dangerous and the conformity is the safety — the anticipation that difference will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is conformity-avoidant-of-individuality. Fear of being a fraud is the conviction that the genuine self is dangerous and the fraudulence is the safety — the anticipation that genuineness will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is maskulence-avoidant-of-genuineness. The overlap is substantial: the different-fearing self may fear being a fraud (the genuineness is the different, and the different is the dangerous, so the only safety is in the fraudulence), and the fraudulence-fearing self can fear being different (the difference might require the genuineness, and the genuineness is the vulnerability, so the only safety is in the mask). But the primary distortion differs: fear of being different's distortion is about the social dimension as threat (is being fundamentally fake safe), fear of being a fraud's distortion is about the ontological dimension as threat (is genuineness safe). A person can fear being different without dominant fraudulence-fear (anxious about standing out while being basically able to rest in chosen genuineness), and a person can fear being a fraud without dominant difference-fear (unable to rest in genuineness while being basically unbothered by standing out in domains that don't produce genuineness). In practice, the two patterns often co-occur, and CBT for one will often address the other. The self-compassion (Neff 2003), ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999), and imposter-syndrome research (Clance & Imes 1978) tools on this page serve both patterns: they build the capacity to allow genuineness (fear of being a fraud) and the capacity to allow the difference (fear of being different) through the same allowing-of-the-full-range-of-experience frame.

How is fear of being a fraud different from fear of being authentic?

Fear of being a fraud and fear of being authentic overlap but differ in focus. Fear of being authentic is the conviction that the real self is dangerous and the performance is the safety — the anticipation that authenticity will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is performance-avoidant. Fear of being a fraud is the conviction that the genuine self is dangerous and the fraudulence is the safety — the anticipation that genuineness will be punished, the conclusion that the self is okay only insofar as the self is maskulence-avoidant-of-genuineness. Fear of being authentic is about real self vs performance as threat (is authenticity safe); fear of being a fraud is about genuineness as threat (is genuineness safe). A person can fear being authentic without dominant fraudulence-fear (anxious about real-self-vs-performance while being basically able to rest in chosen genuineness), and a person can fear being a fraud without dominant authenticity-fear (unable to rest in genuineness while being basically ok with authenticity in domains that don't produce genuineness). In practice, the two patterns can interact (the authentic-fearing self may fear being a fraud — the genuineness might reveal the authenticity, and the authenticity is the vulnerability, so the only safety is in the fraudulence; and the fraudulence-fearing self may fear being authentic — the authenticity is the original, and the original is the dangerous), and the CBT tools on this page address the underlying imposter-avoidance pattern. The self-compassion (Neff 2003) is the unifying frame: both patterns involve a threat to the self's acceptability (the authentic self and the genuine self both threaten the performed-self), and the restoration — through acceptance of the full range of experience rather than demand for the self to be performed — is the core intervention.

What if I really do need fraudulence — isn't some hiding appropriate?

Yes — and this is a crucial nuance. Fear of being a fraud is not the claim that all fraudulence is bad or that one should never adopt a mask. The performed self is the self that chooses many forms of maskulence, and the fraudulence that serves one's values is the valued-action, not the imposter-avoidance-driven avoiding genuineness. The diagnostic question is not 'am I being performed' but 'am I living my life or avoiding genuineness against the not-yet-the-rejected'. The avoiding genuineness that comes from the i-am-a-fraud distortion has a specific signature: it is driven (the person cannot inhabit genuineness, the genuineness produces dread, the genuineness feels like a harm), not chosen (the person could inhabit genuineness, the fraudulence is welcome, the genuineness is a gift). The fraudulence that serves values is chosen and can include genuineness; the avoiding genuineness that serves the imposter-avoidance distortion is driven and cannot include genuineness. The ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999) framework makes this explicit: the valued life includes both fraudulence and genuineness, and the genuineness is not the not-safety but the ground from which the fraudulence comes. The goal of CBT for fear of being a fraud is not to eliminate fraudulence but to liberate it — to move from avoiding genuineness that is driven by the fear of being a fraud (and therefore cannot pause, and therefore anxiously guards against genuineness, and therefore is unsustainable) to fraudulence that is chosen from values (and therefore can pause, and therefore is sustainable, and therefore is more effective). The self-determination theory autonomy need (Deci & Ryan 2000) is the core frame: the genuineness is the living of the full range, and the full range includes the fraudulence and the genuineness, and the genuineness is the genuineness-support, not the genuineness-failure.

What does CBT for Fear of Being a Fraud involve and does it work?

CBT for Fear of Being a Fraud involves genuineness-tolerance clarification, cognitive restructuring of the imposter-avoidance distortions, ACT values-based committed-action (Hayes 1999), self-determination theory autonomy-need application (Deci & Ryan 2000), genuineness-oriented practice, and self-compassion practice (Neff 2003). The core components: (1) genuineness-tolerance clarification — the i-am-a-fraud distortion treats every act of genuineness as a harm; the first step is clarifying that genuineness is a capacity one can exercise without provoking harm, and recognizing that the self that can allow its own genuineness is the self that is whole, not the self that is dangerous; (2) cognitive restructuring — identifying the 6 cognitive distortions (i-am-a-fraud, fraudulence-is-safety, genuineness-is-dangerous, exposure-is-inevitable, i-must-hide, my-achievements-are-unearned) and building balanced beliefs (genuineness is a capacity not a vulnerability, fraudulence is one option not safety, achievement is earned not faked, genuineness is safety not danger, the self can be genuine, and identity is one's own not mask); (3) ACT values-based committed-action — the Hayes (1999) approach: the valued life includes the willingness to be genuine, and the committed action is the living that exercises the full range of original self-expression. Yes, CBT for fear of being a fraud works — the cognitive distortions are well-established in the CBT literature, the ACT values-based committed-action approach has strong empirical support for experiential avoidance patterns, self-compassion (Neff 2003) is empirically supported for reducing the self-criticism that maintains the imposter-avoidance, and imposter-syndrome research (Clance & Imes 1978) documents that genuine living is associated with greater well-being and lower anxiety.
