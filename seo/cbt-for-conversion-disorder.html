What is Conversion Disorder (Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder)?

Conversion disorder (DSM-5 300.11, also called functional neurological symptom disorder or FNSD) is characterized by one or more altered motor or sensory functions that are inconsistent with a recognized neurological or medical condition. The symptoms are not intentionally produced or feigned. They cause clinically significant distress or impairment. Common presentations include functional paralysis or weakness, non-epileptic seizures (also called psychogenic non-epileptic seizures or PNES), functional tremor, functional dystonia, functional gait disorder, functional blindness or vision changes, aphonia (loss of voice), globus (lump in throat), and sensory loss or alteration. The term "functional" refers to a problem in the functioning of the nervous system rather than its structure — the brain is generating real symptoms without a structural lesion, similar to how a software bug can crash a computer without any hardware damage.

Why Conversion Disorder Needs Specific CBT

General anxiety tools say "you worry, let's examine the worry." General somatic-symptom tools say "you catastrophize a symptom, let's examine the catastrophizing." General chronic-illness tools say "you have a condition, let's cope with the condition." General medical-gaslighting tools say "you were dismissed, let's process the dismissal." None of them address the specifics: the My-Body-Has-Betrayed-Me overgeneralizes one episode of altered motor or sensory function into permanent betrayal in a way general anxiety tools cannot address because the betrayal-framing is about the body not the worry, the Its-All-In-My-Head dismisses real neurological symptoms as imaginary in a way general somatic tools cannot address because the dismissal is about the scan-negative not the symptom-catastrophizing, the I-Must-Find-The-Real-Cause drives the test-seeking loop in a way general anxiety tools cannot address because the seeking is for the lesion not the reassurance, the My-Symptom-Is-Permanent catastrophizes a functional symptom into forever in a way general chronic-illness tools cannot address because the permanence is a distortion about a fluctuating condition not a coping challenge about a stable condition, the I-Should-Be-Able-To-Control-This blames the will for a brain-body disconnect in a way general anxiety tools cannot address because the will-blame is about the motor-control not the worry, and the No-One-Believes-Me fuses alienation with the symptom in a way general medical-gaslighting tools cannot address because the alienation is about the functional-diagnosis not the dismissal. This produces specific, identifiable thought patterns that CBT for conversion disorder can target directly:

The "My-Body-Has-Betrayed-Me" Distortion (Betrayal-Framing / Overgeneralization)

Distortion: "My body has betrayed me. The leg that worked yesterday does not work today, and the betrayal means I can never trust my body again, and the not-trusting means I must monitor every sensation, and the monitoring means I am always bracing for the next betrayal, and the bracing is the trap. The body is not a system that fluctuates; the body is a traitor, and the traitor is the one thing that means I am never safe."
Reframe: "I am examining the evidence and challenging the My-Body-Has-Betrayed-Me. The My-Body-Has-Betrayed-Me (the leg that worked yesterday does not work today) was a betrayal-framing and an overgeneralization: the mind takes one episode of altered motor function and inflates it into permanent betrayal, and because the inflation feels like evidence of treachery, the inference is that the body is the enemy. The betrayal-framing (the body has betrayed me) is the distortion: the episode is real, but the conclusion that the body is a traitor is the overgeneralization of one fluctuation into a permanent character — the body is a system that fluctuates, and the fluctuation includes episodes of altered function and episodes of restored function. The overgeneralization (I can never trust my body again) is the distortion: one episode does not predict all episodes, and the bracing for the next betrayal is the vigilance that maintains the symptoms. CBT helps by examining the evidence (what is the actual evidence that the body has betrayed you versus the evidence that it is a fluctuating system, what would a neutral observer say about a body with one episode versus what they would say about a traitor, what is the base rate of functional symptoms fluctuating and improving), challenging the betrayal-framing (the episode is real, but the conclusion that the body is the enemy is the distortion), and reducing the vigilance (symptom-monitoring, bracing, body-scanning) that maintains the overgeneralization — so the mind learns that the body is a fluctuating system, not a traitor. The episode is real; the conclusion that the body has betrayed you is the distortion.

The "Its-All-In-My-Head" Distortion (Dismissal-Internalization / Invalidation)

Distortion: "It's all in my head. The doctors found nothing on the scan, so the symptom must be imaginary, and imaginary means I am making it up, and making it up means it is my fault, and my fault means I should be able to stop it, and the not-stopping is the proof that I am weak. The scan being negative is the proof that the symptom is not real."
Reframe: "I am examining the evidence and challenging the Its-All-In-My-Head. The Its-All-In-My-Head (the scan was negative so the symptom is imaginary) was a dismissal-internalization and an invalidation: the mind takes a negative scan and concludes the symptom is imaginary, and because the conclusion feels like evidence of fault, the inference is that the symptom is the person's fault. The dismissal-internalization (it is all in my head) is the distortion: the scan being negative means there is no structural lesion, not that the symptom is imaginary — functional neurological symptoms are real neurological events that happen without structural disease, like a software crash without hardware damage. The invalidation (I am making it up) is the distortion: the symptom is not intentionally produced, the brain is generating genuine motor or sensory alterations, and the not-being-able-to-stop is evidence of a real brain-body disconnect, not evidence of weakness. CBT helps by examining the evidence (what is the actual evidence that the symptom is imaginary versus the evidence that it is a real functional neurological event, what would a neurologist say about a negative scan versus what they would say about the symptom's reality, what is the base rate of functional symptoms being real-but-not-structural), challenging the dismissal (the scan is negative, but the conclusion that the symptom is imaginary is the distortion — the symptom is functional, not fake), and reducing the self-blame that the dismissal-internalization produces — so the mind learns that functional is real, and the symptom is a brain event, not a character flaw. The scan is negative; the conclusion that the symptom is imaginary is the distortion.

The "My-Symptom-Is-Permanent" Distortion (Permanence-Fatalism / Catastrophizing)

Distortion: "This is permanent. The paralysis will not come back, the blindness will not lift, the tremor will not stop, and the future is a body that does not work. The symptom is not a fluctuation; the symptom is a forever, and the forever is the one thing that means nothing I do will matter, and the nothing-mattering is the trap."
Reframe: "I am examining the evidence and challenging the My-Symptom-Is-Permanent. The My-Symptom-Is-Permanent (the paralysis will not come back) was a permanence-fatalism and a catastrophizing: the mind takes a present symptom and projects it into forever, and because the projection feels like evidence of hopelessness, the inference is that nothing will improve. The permanence-fatalism (this is forever) is the distortion: functional neurological symptoms fluctuate — they can worsen, improve, shift, and sometimes resolve completely, and the prognosis is better than the fatalism suggests (CBT plus physiotherapy produces ~40-60% improvement in trials). The catastrophizing (the future is a body that does not work) is the distortion: the future is uncertain, and the uncertainty includes the possibility of improvement, not only the certainty of decline. CBT helps by examining the evidence (what is the actual evidence that the symptom is permanent versus the evidence that functional symptoms fluctuate, what would a clinician say about the prognosis versus what the fatalism says, what is the base rate of functional symptoms improving with treatment), challenging the permanence (the symptom is present, but the conclusion that it is forever is the distortion), and running behavioral experiments (graded activity, valued action, tracking fluctuations) that test the permanence belief — so the mind learns that the symptom fluctuates, and the future is uncertain, not doomed. The symptom is present; the conclusion that it is permanent is the distortion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is conversion disorder the same as somatic symptom disorder?

No. Conversion disorder centers on altered motor or sensory function that is incompatible with a recognized neurological disease — the paralysis, the non-epileptic seizure, the functional tremor, the blindness — the symptom is neurological in presentation and the question is whether it is structural or functional. Somatic symptom disorder centers on excessive distress about any physical symptom (which may be medically explained or unexplained) — the chest tightness, the stomach pain, the fatigue — with the distress, medical seeking, and identity-fusion being the disorder rather than the symptom's neurological presentation. The My-Body-Has-Betrayed-Me, the I-Should-Be-Able-To-Control-This, and the No-One-Believes-Me are conversion-specific patterns that general somatic tools miss because they target the neurological-betrayal and the will-blame rather than the symptom-catastrophizing. Use the conversion tools if the primary problem is altered motor/sensory function; use the somatic tools if the primary problem is distress about a symptom.

Are the symptoms real or am I making them up?

The symptoms are real. Conversion disorder is NOT faking, NOT imagining, NOT making it up, and NOT all in your head. The brain is generating genuine neurological symptoms — the leg genuinely does not move, the vision genuinely is altered, the seizure genuinely happens — without a structural lesion explaining them. This is a functional disorder (the nervous system is malfunctioning) not a structural disorder (there is no damaged tissue) and not a factitious disorder (the symptoms are not intentionally produced). The Its-All-In-My-Head is itself the cognitive distortion: the scan being negative does not mean the symptom is imaginary, it means the symptom is functional — the brain produces real symptoms without structural disease, like a software crash without hardware damage. CBT does not tell you the symptom is not real; it targets the cognitive distortions layered on the symptom — the betrayal-framing, the dismissal-internalization, the permanence-catastrophizing, the will-blame. The symptom is real; the conclusion that it is imaginary, permanent, or your fault is the distortion.

How is conversion disorder different from factitious disorder or malingering?

Conversion disorder, factitious disorder, and malingering all involve symptoms that are not explained by disease, but they differ critically in intention. Conversion disorder: the symptoms are NOT intentionally produced or faked — the brain is generating genuine neurological symptoms without structural disease, the person is not making it up, the symptom is real and the person is distressed by it. Factitious disorder (including Munchausen syndrome): the symptoms ARE intentionally produced or feigned — the person is deliberately causing or faking symptoms to assume the sick role, the symptom is not real and the person is aware they are producing it. Malingering: the symptoms ARE intentionally produced or feigned for external gain — the person is deliberately faking symptoms for money, time off work, legal advantage, the symptom is not real and the person is aware they are producing it for a goal. The Its-All-In-My-Head distortion conflates these three by treating "not structural" as "intentionally produced," but conversion disorder is the one where the symptom is real and not intentional. CBT for conversion disorder targets the cognitive distortions in a real, non-intentional symptom; CBT for factitious disorder targets the sick-role-seeking behavior; malingering is not a mental disorder and is not treated with CBT.
