What Is a CBT Thought Record?

A CBT thought record is a structured worksheet used in Cognitive Behavioral Therapy to help you identify, examine, and restructure unhelpful automatic thoughts. It is one of the most evidence-based tools in clinical psychology, with decades of research showing it reduces anxiety and depression when practiced regularly.

The thought record works by creating a gap between a triggering situation and your emotional response. Instead of accepting your first interpretation as truth, you systematically look for evidence — both for and against your automatic thought — and then construct a balanced alternative.

How to Use This Thought Record Tool

  • Step 1 — Situation: Describe what happened objectively, as if a camera recorded it.
  • Step 2 — Automatic Thoughts: Write what went through your mind. These are the hot, immediate interpretations.
  • Step 3 — Emotions: Name the emotion and rate its intensity (0–100%). Note where you feel it in your body.
  • Step 4 — Distortions: Identify which cognitive distortions are present in your thinking.
  • Step 5 — Evidence: List facts that support AND contradict the automatic thought.
  • Step 6 — Balanced Thought: Write a new perspective that accounts for all the evidence.
  • Step 7 — Re-rate: Score the emotion intensity again. It usually drops.

10 Common Cognitive Distortions

  • All-or-nothing thinking: Seeing things in black-and-white categories.
  • Overgeneralization: A single event becomes a never-ending pattern.
  • Mental filter: Dwelling on one negative detail and ignoring the rest.
  • Disqualifying the positive: Rejecting good experiences as if they don't count.
  • Jumping to conclusions: Mind-reading or fortune-telling without evidence.
  • Magnification / minimization: Exaggerating the bad or shrinking the good.
  • Emotional reasoning: "I feel it, so it must be true."
  • Should statements: Rigid rules that create guilt or pressure.
  • Labeling: Attaching a fixed label to yourself instead of describing the behavior.
  • Personalization & blame: Taking responsibility for things outside your control.
