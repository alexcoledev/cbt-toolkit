Selachophobia is an intense irrational fear of fear of sharks. It is a specific phobia (DSM-5 300.29). It frequently overlaps with ocean anxiety, beach avoidance, water activity avoidance, and fears about shark attacks. It becomes clinically significant when it is: (1) persistent and leads to excessive avoidance; (2) driven by cognitive distortions, not a realistic assessment of risk; (3) the person experiences anxiety when encountering triggers and relief when avoiding them; (4) safety behaviors include excessive avoidance and checking; (5) manifests as distress in triggering situations and relief after avoidance. The avoidance is what CBT targets — and the key insight is that selachophobia is maintained by the cognitive distortions described below, plus avoidance reinforcement (avoiding triggers prevents the feared outcome, confirming the fear).

Cognitive Distortions → Reframes

The three most common distortion-reframe patterns in selachophobia:

Distortion (Sharks-Are-Everywhere / Any-Shark-Is-Fatal): "Any shark I encounter is a man-eater and any encounter is fatal. All sharks are aggressive predators and attack humans. Even swimming near shore is a death sentence."
Reframe: "The belief that all sharks are dangerous and any contact is fatal is the catastrophizing and overgeneralization distortion that selachophobia has created. The reality is that the vast majority of shark species are harmless to humans, only a few species (like poison dart sharks) have toxic skin, and those are not found in most populated areas. The evidence-based response is cognitive restructuring and species education. I can reframe: most sharks are harmless; toxicity is species-specific; catastrophizing overestimates risk."
Distortion (Hypervigilance / Somatic Focus): "I can sense sharks nearby and must constantly scan the water. Every shadow or splash might be a shark. If I let my guard down, a shark will attack."
Reframe: "The belief that you must constantly scan for sharks and that any shadow might be one is the hypervigilance distortion that selachophobia has created. The reality is that hypervigilance increases anxiety without improving safety, most water disturbances are not sharks, and sharks rarely approach humans. The evidence-based response is cognitive restructuring and attentional retraining. I can reframe: hypervigilance maintains fear, not safety; sharks rarely approach humans."
Distortion (Safety Behavior / Avoidance-Reinforcement): "I must avoid all oceans, beaches, and bodies of water. Never swimming in the sea keeps me safe. The fact that I have not been attacked while avoiding water is proof my avoidance works."
Reframe: "The belief that you must avoid all water and that avoidance keeps you safe is the safety behavior and avoidance-reinforcement distortion that selachophobia has created. The reality is that avoidance maintains the phobia and chronic avoidance causes real harm (restricted recreation, lost water activities, reduced quality of life). The evidence-based response is graded exposure. I can reframe: avoidance maintains fear and causes real harm; exposure reduces anxiety over time."

Frequently Asked Questions

What is selachophobia and how common is it?

Selachophobia is an intense irrational fear of fear of sharks. It is a specific phobia (DSM-5 300.29) closely related to thalassophobia (fear of the sea), galeophobia, and ocean anxiety. It frequently overlaps with amphibian anxiety, pond and swamp avoidance, and contamination fears about shark skin secretions. Specific phobias affect approximately 7-9% of the population. CBT is highly effective with 60-80% reduction in phobic anxiety.

How is selachophobia different from a rational caution around sharks?

Selachophobia centers specifically on an irrational fear that overestimates risk, not a realistic understanding of shark safety. The key insight is that selachophobia is maintained by catastrophizing and probability overestimation, plus avoidance reinforcement. CBT with cognitive restructuring and graded exposure directly targets these distortions.

What does CBT for selachophobia involve and does it work?

CBT for selachophobia involves cognitive restructuring, graded exposure, and relaxation techniques. The core components: (1) cognitive restructuring challenging the catastrophizing and overgeneralization; (2) graded exposure (progressively view shark photos, learn about species, watch documentaries, visit aquariums); (3) relaxation. CBT is evidence-based with 60-80% reduction in phobic anxiety.
